# Legacy academic data and historical field review

Reviewed 2026-11-08 against the legacy documentation, migration definitions and academic views, and the current domain models, migrations and write/read repositories; implementation status refreshed 2026-11-09. This is an implementation input and gap review, not a claim that the missing fields or event streams have been built. Apply the [event-sourcing direction](event-sourcing.md) and [architecture/retrofit order](event-sourcing-architecture.md) before expanding academic writes. The [development roadmap](development-roadmap.md) consolidates remaining work.

Schema columns alone are evidence that a value existed, not proof it was reliably populated or had a complete workflow. For example, legacy course schemas declare `maxCredit`/`increCredit`, while core forms primarily expose `minCredit`; variable-credit support needs a validated new workflow rather than blind copying of those columns. Nullable/zero dates, numeric IDs, freeform rules and cascaded code changes must not be treated as sound historical design.

## Course definitions

Current [Course](../src/Domain/Catalog/Course.php) and `sis_courses` store a ULID, full code, one title, fixed credit thousandths and activity. Prerequisite relationships exist separately. The catalog editor's optimistic revision token and course-clone receipts are not immutable published course versions. The new [explicit publication flow](course-publication.md) separately preserves code, title, fixed credits and prerequisite identities in the protected journal; version 2 now also retains an optional transcript title and plain-text catalog description. Empty sections can now be explicitly bound to a chosen publication; their future institutional credits retain that reference and snapshots. Offering-plan publication now requires those choices and atomically binds/activates its drafts. Existing/unbound and transfer records remain unbound. Institutional credit and transfer award decisions now use shared-journal histories. Classification/policy fields below, complete section versions and their retained academic references remain pending.

Course versions must permit substantive title/credit/classification changes without losing prior versions under the same stable identity/code. Current global code uniqueness is an identity rule, not justification for overwriting a published definition or recreating the old many-row same-code model.

## Section offerings and teaching

Current [Section](../src/Domain/Registration/Section.php), `sis_sections` and `sis_section_delivery` provide course/term references, a section code, one optional faculty login, capacity, activity, web registration, one section fee/currency and delivery mode. Current offering templates version reusable defaults, not the complete historical academic definition of an offered section. Explicit course-version binding supplies a retained inherited course definition for an empty section; it does not itself version section fees, dates, delivery or grading policies. The separate [section-publication workflow](section-publication.md) now retains reviewed instructional dates and existing offering facts, including canonical faculty identity; explicit initial selection and reviewed future-registration amendments now retain their references in new academic facts without rewriting earlier attempts. Offering-plan activation now retains those reviewed versions atomically; complete policy coverage remains pending.

## Student academic facts and related data

Current `sis_academic_credits` already retains course code/title, attempted/completed credits, grade points, source IDs/type, term and status. Those limited snapshots are useful historical facts, not duplication to remove. Institutional creation/grade/drop decisions now retain these facts in replayable [academic-credit history](academic-credit-history.md), with reasoned corrections and known registration-time policy references. [Transfer award history](transfer-credit-history.md) now retains initial awards, corrections, voids and reinstatements in the same journal. Older history is explicitly unknown. Full section versions, closed-record replacements and remaining lifecycle conversions are tracked in [the architecture review](event-sourcing-architecture.md).

## Avoiding duplication without losing history

The intended chain is **Course identity → published CourseVersion → Section identity/published SectionVersion → student attempt/award events → projections/documents**. These are proposed ownership boundaries, not new tables already created by this review.

Normalize shared definitions: subjects, departments/schools, academic/course levels, campuses, rooms, grading schemes and award classifications have stable ULIDs and retirement/version rules. Keep readable codes as business attributes rather than foreign-key identities. Only retain a section override when that offering genuinely differs from its selected course version.

A reference to an immutable version avoids copying an entire definition into every student row. A retained award-time title, credit value, grade policy input or issued-document snapshot is intentional evidence. Removing it merely because a current join can supply a value would destroy history. Conversely, GPA totals, seat counts and repeated composed identifiers are derived read models, not additional writable sources of truth.

Material corrections append amendments and preserve the earlier version. Institutional-ID changes continue resolving the current ID through the same person; they do not rewrite recorded historical documents or domain events. Historical identity snapshots are retained only where justified and protected by the appropriate retention/access policy.

## Implementation priorities and acceptance examples

Restore reference/classification and published definition fields together with the secure event foundation, then convert authoritative academic awards and registration. Every added historical field must reach its intended draft editor, validation, publication/snapshot/event contract, persistence, replay, authorized queries and relevant report/portal/transcript presentation. Adding a nullable column alone does not complete the capability.

The first vertical slices must prove:

- Renaming/reclassifying `MATH.101` or changing its credit policy creates a new published version. Students enrolled under the earlier version keep their original title, classifications, credits and applicable grade policy.
- Two sections of one course may use different instructional dates, delivery/meeting arrangements and fee components without duplicating editable course definitions. A student attempt retains the selected version and actual credits.
- Amending a grade preserves the original grade, who changed it, why, effective/recorded times and applicable policy; current projections rebuild to the amended result while a previously issued document remains reproducible.
- An early drop removes current academic/transcript projections and reverses the original course charge, while protected history remains retained. A late drop keeps the institutional grade/charge policy. A retake is a distinct academic and billing-term attempt.
- Program/cohort and term/level reports reproduce their historical scope after catalog, reporting-group or grading-policy changes. Corrections never invent missing past values; imported unknowns remain explicitly unknown with provenance.

This review intentionally creates no speculative schema or live-data conversion. Field restoration and event-sourcing conversion remain implementation work. No working database or student data was accessed or modified.
