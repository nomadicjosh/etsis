# Human resources

Employment remains a capability under the canonical [Person identity and account](person-identity-and-accounts.md). Adding employment does not create a second person or grant security roles. This guide consolidates HR setup, hire/end/rehire dates, dated position agreements, retained profile changes and connected time review. New time entries retain their accepted position references. Institutional time-review thresholds, documented exceptions and section contact-hour conversion now share the policy workflow below. Explicit primary-position designations now connect employment and time-entry context. Institution-specific mandatory-primary rules, further employment approvals and payroll remain roadmap work.

## Job and pay-grade setup

Open **Team & timesheets → HR setup**, or open it from **Timesheets**. One workspace manages **Job definitions** and **Pay grades**. Search by code/title, page through fifty definitions at a time, create, edit and retire definitions. Codes are case-normalized and unique within each category; dots, slashes, underscores and hyphens are supported. Definitions use ULIDs. These are ordinary authorized reference records with required metadata audit, not a new event-sourced workflow.

Set up pay grades with a code, title, currency, **Hourly**, **Monthly** or **Annual** rate basis, and minimum/maximum amounts. Minimum cannot exceed maximum. Monthly amounts are amounts per month, annual amounts are per year, and hourly amounts are per hour. The system does not convert between these units or infer salary from weekly hours. Currency precision comes from the installed Money library's ISO currency data: for example, JPY has no fractional minor units, USD has two and BHD has three. Enter decimal amounts without currency symbols or thousands separators. Values are stored as exact, nonnegative minor-unit integers within the existing portable monetary bound; excess fractions are rejected rather than rounded.

A job definition contains a code/title, currency and rate basis, optional pay grade, optional default rate, and optional weekly hours/additional minutes. Blank rate or workload means unknown; zero is an explicit value. Hours/minutes are stored as one minute count, without calculating pay or imposing a timesheet limit. The default rate, if set, must fall within the selected grade, and currency/basis must match. An active job requires an active selected grade.

If a grade is missing while entering a job, choose **Create pay grade**. Saving it returns to the job draft with the new grade selected and entered title/rate/workload intact. **Back to job draft** returns without saving a grade. Grade choices use bounded search and paging rather than loading the whole library. Rejected saves preserve form values and show accessible errors. Currency/basis changes require reviewing entered amounts; the interface performs no exchange or annualization conversion.

Retire jobs before retiring their pay grade. A grade's units or range cannot be changed in a way that invalidates active linked jobs. Retired definitions remain editable reference records and cannot be deleted through this workflow. Current employee titles/status, recorded employment changes, academic facts and financial postings are unchanged by library edits. Use **Position assignments** on an employee to accept reviewed defaults and record an individual agreement. Current employee title/status maintenance retains its separate form.

Every setup read/write requires `sis:hr:manage`, including current user/permission rechecks. Writes serialize with role changes, lock existing definitions, check an integer expected revision and commit the record plus required metadata audit atomically. Repeated unchanged saves write nothing; stale revisions cannot overwrite newer edits, including values changed back later. Audit failure or changed authority rolls back the mutation. Responses are private/no-store, browser mutations preserve framework CSRF, and debug collectors stay suppressed. Setup rates/ranges are not added to ordinary Report Studio sources or person-owner responses, and drafts use no browser storage.

The additive `20261115000001_CreateSisHrDefinitions` migration creates empty `sis_hr_pay_grades` and `sis_hr_jobs` tables, with matching ULID references and an indexed grade/active-job lookup. It is applied to the configured test database. Bounded verification preserved the existing ten person records, employee/time records and all twenty event rows byte-for-byte. No earlier job, wage, position or pay-grade history is invented. Compilation covers the framework's five database dialects, with SQLite integration and native MariaDB migration checks; broader live-server qualification remains open.

## Position assignments

Open **Team & timesheets → select an employee → Position assignments**. Record an assignment by searching for an active job, reviewing its pay grade, and entering the actual from/through dates, agreed rate, weekly hours/minutes and reason. **Review assignment** previews the agreement before confirmation; returning to the form preserves the draft. Rejected confirmations preserve the review and entered values. Dates are institution calendar dates; through dates are inclusive. Blank rate/workload means unknown, while zero is an explicit value. Actual rates must fit the reviewed grade when one is selected. Monthly agreements retain their monthly unit and exact amount in the existing protected position history, just as hourly and annual agreements do. Later job or pay-grade edits do not change an earlier agreement's unit, workload or rate. Monthly compensation is a rate basis; payroll periods, proration and payment schedules remain separate institutional policies.

Each assignment retains the accepted job/pay-grade identities, revisions, labels, currency, rate basis, ranges and defaults, alongside the actual agreement and currency precision. Changing or retiring today's setup cannot relabel an earlier agreement or change its amount. Unknown earlier positions remain unknown: recording a retrospective agreement retains the actual recording time and reason rather than asserting it was recorded in the past. Several positions may run concurrently under the same employee and canonical Person.

Choose **Change position or agreed terms** to record a subsequent agreement on one screen. Its effective date must follow the previous agreement's start and fall within its reviewed interval or immediately after its end. Confirmation closes the previous agreement the day before the new start and records the new job/rate/workload together. Both decisions, projections and required audits commit atomically. A stale assignment or changed job/pay grade requires a fresh review. The original terms remain retained. For an independent concurrent position, use **Record position assignment** instead.

**Change end date** records a reasoned closure or clears a closure when left blank. **Void assignment** marks an incorrect agreement without deleting it; **Restore assignment** records its reinstatement. End-date and void/restoration decisions retain attribution and expected versions. Exact authorized retries return successfully without undoing later decisions, including a later reopened or restored assignment. Correcting agreed terms uses a new agreement; an end-date change cannot alter the retained rate or job.

All assignment reads and writes require `sis:hr:manage`; owning an employee record does not expose compensation. Another super account requires a super administrator. The workflow reuses Person/Employee authorization, history verification and locking, then rechecks authority before completion. Private responses exclude reasons, raw events and internal operation identities, use no-store and suppress debug collectors. Browser mutations and page requests retain CSRF protection. Drafts stay in page memory, with escaped labels, keyboard access and narrow-screen tables.

The additive `20261115000002_CreateSisEmployeePositions` migration is applied to the configured test database. It creates an empty `sis_employee_positions` projection with ULIDs and an employee/ID paging index; snapshots and agreed terms are encrypted, with authenticated ID/employee/version binding. Every agreement uses an `employment_position` stream in the existing protected `sis_history_events` journal. No second event table or inferred earlier assignments are introduced. Verification preserved all ten person records, the employee record, existing setup/time rows and all twenty event rows byte-for-byte.

Lists read twenty projected agreements at a time and compare their indexed journal versions. Opening or changing one agreement verifies its complete stream, bounded to 2,000 events; missing or altered tracked projections stop the operation. Lists are current projections, not a reconciliation of every historical stream. They do not replay each listed agreement. Broader projection-rebuild/reconciliation tooling remains operational roadmap work. Compilation covers the framework's five database dialects; SQLite integration and the native MariaDB migration are exercised.

Position agreements must fit a recorded employment period once employment dates have been entered; new agreements, end-date changes and restorations recheck that boundary. Position agreements do not choose a primary job automatically: concurrent positions may exist. The general employee profile label and active flag remain independently managed. Assignments do not grant security roles, modify Person/student records, establish legal hire/termination dates, calculate pay or reassign earlier time entries to a rate. Time entries remain audited CRUD, with protected position/version references for newly selected work. Payroll and institutional exception rules still need their own policies.

## Primary position designation

An employee can hold several concurrent appointments while HR designates one as their main appointment. This is separate from the free-text employment title. No designation is inferred from an older employee row or from having only one appointment.

Open **Team & timesheets → employee → Position assignments → Open position assignment → Designate as primary position**. Select an appointment covering today, enter a reason, review the previous and proposed designation, then confirm. Both `sis:hr:manage` and the independent `sis:hr:primary` duty are required. The new duty has no automatic role/user grants; an authorized access administrator can assign it to the appropriate HR role. The designation takes effect at confirmation. Backdated/future designation schedules and institution-required primary appointments remain future policy work.

For example, an employee may hold library and lab appointments. HR can designate the library appointment as primary while both agreements remain valid. This changes no compensation, workload, security roles, teaching assignment or payroll result. A timesheet for lab work must still name the lab appointment.

Open **employee → Primary position** to read the retained job code/title and present status. HR can select another appointment or **Clear primary designation** with a reason and new review. Clearing leaves an explicit decision, distinct from never recording a designation. An end-date or void-status change marks the designation for review; ending or replacing an appointment never silently follows a replacement or clears the old designation. Job-definition renames do not change accepted labels. HR can explicitly reaffirm a changed appointment that still covers today, choose another current agreement or clear the designation.

Employees can read their own minimized designation. Its label also appears beside eligible time-entry choices. One matching work appointment remains preselected; several matches still require an explicit choice, even when one is primary. The displayed designation is current context, not evidence of the primary position on a past work date. Historical time entries retain their original worked-position references.

**Primary designation history** requires both `sis:hr:manage` and `sis:audit:read`, with existing super-account safeguards. Fixed-boundary twenty-decision pages show accepted appointment labels, effective/recorded times, current recorder labels and private reasons. Earlier designations remain unknown. Owner summaries exclude reasons, recorder identities and compensation. Reviews bind actor, employee, operation, expected designation/appointment versions and reason, expire after twenty minutes, and are rechecked with current authority/source after required audit. Accepted original retries preserve the earlier receipt without restoring an old designation after later changes or expiry. Failed saves retain the in-memory review and draft.

The `employment_primary_position` stream uses the single protected journal. `sis_primary_positions` is an encrypted reconstructible projection keyed by the employee ULID. Reads verify projection/history agreement; selected appointments verify their own retained history. Replay is capped at 2,000 decisions. Changes share existing employee/Person/authority locks and the event/projection/audit transaction. No parallel journal or inferred historical designation is created. Browser mutations retain framework CSRF and private/no-store responses without debug collectors.

## Hire, end and rehire dates

Open **Team & timesheets → select an employee → Employment dates**. Owners can read their dates; HR staff can choose **Record hire or rehire**, **End employment** or **Review employment dates**. Enter the actual hire/employment start, an optional inclusive last employment day and a required reason. Rehire adds another nonoverlapping period under the same employee and Person; no second identity or account is created. Start dates are actual employment dates, not offer-letter dates or inferred creation timestamps. Unknown earlier dates remain unknown until staff explicitly record them.

**Review employment decision** shows the proposed dates and each position change before confirmation. An employment ending closes assignments that extend beyond the last employment day. Later scheduled assignments outside all recorded periods are marked void, with their original facts retained. An assignment covered by a separate recorded rehire remains intact. Date corrections and marking an incorrect period void use the same review. Earlier ended assignments before the first known employment dates remain untouched. No private compensation amounts are copied into the impact preview.

**Confirm employment decision** saves the date decision, listed position changes, encrypted projections and required audits together. If a position or employment revision changed after review, save nothing and require another review. Failed confirmations preserve the preview and entered dates/reason. A successful original retry remains successful after later corrections without closing or restoring positions again. Clearing an end date or unmarking an incorrect period records the new decision; it does not restore positions automatically. Review any desired position restoration separately against the corrected dates.

Actual work submitted through the owner or staff screen must fit one non-voided recorded period once dates are known. The institution's application timezone determines calendar boundaries; a shift may end at midnight immediately after the inclusive last employment day. A later submission of legitimate earlier work is allowed within that period when the manual time-submission flag remains enabled. New approvals recheck the actual work dates too. Existing approved/pending/rejected entries remain intact when HR corrects dates; an out-of-period pending entry may be rejected or its supporting dates reviewed, but cannot receive a new approval without coverage. A record with no employment dates retains the earlier flag-based behavior. Future dates take effect through these checks without a scheduled status-flipping worker.

New position agreements, end-date changes and restorations must fit a recorded employment period. Existing position facts are retained. Earlier unassigned time stays unassigned; newly submitted work retains its accepted position/version reference, as described below. Ending employment does not disable the Person account, revoke security roles, alter student pursuits or submit an external Moodle action. Review account/access changes under their existing permissions. Payroll, statutory employment procedures and institution-specific approvals are not calculated or certified by recording these dates.

The workflow reuses HR/Person/Employee authorization and locks, including super-account protection and final authority checks. Dates are owner-readable; proposals and confirmations require `sis:hr:manage`. Responses are private/no-store, exclude raw history/reasons and suppress debug collectors. Browser proposals and confirmations preserve framework CSRF; private drafts remain in page memory. Reviews do not write events. Date decisions use one `employment_lifecycle` stream per employee in the existing protected journal; earlier title/status and position events remain unchanged. Recorded and effective dates stay separate, with the recorder, reason and original facts protected in history.

The additive `20261115000003_CreateSisEmploymentDates` migration creates an empty `sis_employment_dates` encrypted projection with a matching employee ULID primary/foreign key. It is applied to the configured test database. Verification preserved all ten Person rows, ten account rows, the employee, position/setup/time/student rows and all twenty preexisting event rows byte-for-byte. No hire date was copied from an active flag, assignment start or record creation time. Compilation covers the framework's five database dialects, with SQLite integration and a native MariaDB migration check.

Each selected timeline is bounded to 2,000 events and 100 retained employment periods. A reviewed decision inspects at most 500 assignment projections, changes at most 50 assignments atomically and replays at most 5,000 existing affected-position events in total. Limits stop the whole decision rather than returning partial results or leaving half an employment ending committed. Unaffected assignment streams are not replayed. General directories remain indexed reads; the dates review loads on demand. Broader operational rebuild/reconciliation and retention tooling remains on the roadmap.

## Employment profile changes

Open **Team & timesheets → select an employee → Edit employment** to change the job title or active status in place. Enter a reason for the change. HR maintenance requires `sis:hr:manage`; another super account requires a super administrator. Owners may read their own employment and time entries without gaining HR maintenance or self-approval authority.

Employment remains a child of the same `Person`. Creating it neither creates another account nor grants a security role. The **Time submissions enabled** flag can pause new time submissions while leaving earlier entries and pending reviews available. Recorded employment periods also constrain actual work dates; changing dates does not silently reset that manual flag. It does not disable sign-in, close a student program, or change student status. The active flag and recording date are not legal hire/termination dates.

Genuine new employment and subsequent title/status decisions use one `employment_profile` stream per employee in the existing protected `sis_history_events` journal. This separate HR boundary retains the person reference without copying person names, addresses or account credentials. The existing `sis_employees` table remains the current projection. Payloads and change reasons are encrypted; ordinary employee responses and metadata audits exclude private reasons and raw events.

Older employment rows remain unchanged on reads and unchanged saves. Their first actual edit records a current observation followed by the accepted change, explicitly marking earlier history as unknown. It does not invent a past hiring decision. Each accepted change retains its recorder, trusted recording/effective time, previous title/status and reason. This title/flag workflow records changes effective now. Hire/end/rehire dates use the separate retained workflow above; payroll-effective calculations still need their own rules.

Role/person/employee locks, expected revisions, history, projection and required audit commit in one transaction. Authority is checked again before completion. Revisions include the stream version, so changing a value back does not make an old form current. An exact authorized retry returns successfully without undoing later changes, including later reactivation. Another actor cannot adopt that receipt. Rejected UI saves preserve the entered title, status and reason.

Directories and searches use indexed current rows. Opening a selected employee or authorizing a time submission verifies that employee's protected history and canonical person; altered tracked projections are rejected. Replay is bounded to 2,000 events, and a change cannot exceed that ceiling. Reading adds no historical events. Time-entry detail reuses its selected employment result rather than loading the same employment stream twice.

Title/status history uses the existing employee projection and protected journal, without a profile-row conversion or additional event table. Time entries retain their existing audited CRUD and version checks; this change does not claim that payroll calculations or historical job titles at the time of work are implemented. Job/pay-grade setup and dated position agreements are described above. Hire/end/rehire date decisions are described above. Faculty assignments, payroll boundaries and operational rebuild/retention tooling remain separate work.

## Position-linked time

Choose the team member and actual work interval. **Position worked** loads matching assignments through a private request; one match is preselected, while several matches require an explicit choice. **Refresh work positions** reloads choices without discarding the entered interval. If recorded positions exist but none covers the whole interval, split work at an assignment boundary or ask HR to review the dates. Assignment dates use the institution timezone shown by the selector; the time inputs retain their existing browser-local display.

The same rules apply to staff-entered and owner-entered work. A stale selected assignment requires refreshed choices. Selected work must fit a non-voided assignment for the full interval, with inclusive end dates and midnight immediately after the last day permitted. Existing employment-period checks, the manual time-submission flag, maximum 24 elapsed hours, independent approval and cross-position overlap prevention still apply. New entries retain the selected position identity and accepted stream version. General employment labels remain current profile information. Time-entry detail, recent time history and weekly review display the retained **Position worked** separately. Entries without a recorded assignment show **Not recorded**. Recording positions later does not assign earlier work, and an unassigned entry already pending review is not silently changed.

Approval replays and verifies the selected position, then checks current assignment and employment-date coverage. An assignment that was ended early or marked incorrect can stop a new approval while preserving the submitted interval and original reference. Its work remains readable, including the retained job label; HR can reject it or correct the supporting assignment dates before deciding again. A rejected entry can be resubmitted with the correct position through the normal workflow. Earlier approved time is not recalculated or reclassified. Correcting position dates does not change the reference accepted with a submitted entry.

Owners can read only their own choices and work. HR can review another employee subject to existing super-account protections. Choice and time responses exclude pay amounts, workloads, grade ranges, private reasons, raw events and encrypted references. Browser lookups preserve framework CSRF; responses are private/no-store with debug collectors suppressed. Drafts remain in page memory, labels are escaped, and failed saves retain the chosen position and interval. Keyboard controls, status announcements and narrow-screen tables cover the selection and review.

Position replay now lives in one shared internal ledger used by position management and time decisions. Choices inspect bounded protected projections for one employee, at most 500 assignments and 50 matches. Recent/weekly labels use one batched projection query for their distinct positions, with at most 500 entries; they do not replay every assignment for every row. ## Weekly time review

The review uses Monday–Sunday calendar weeks and the institution's configured application timezone. The server supplies the current week; the browser's timezone does not decide its boundaries.

Cards show pending, approved and rejected elapsed time separately, with unique entry counts. Seven daily rows split overnight work at local midnight. Entries crossing a week boundary contribute only the portion inside that week. Spring/fall clock changes use actual elapsed seconds, and rejected entries never inflate approved or pending totals. This is a calendar review, not a payroll-period definition, overtime calculation, break deduction or rounding rule.

**Review weekly time entry** opens the existing full-interval decision screen. Approve/reject uses the displayed version and existing HR/self-approval safeguards. **Back to weekly time review** returns to the selected week and refreshes totals. A failed refresh keeps the last successful week and its displayed date range visible. The shared labeled controls, status/error messages and tables support keyboard access and narrow screens. Data remains in page memory.

Owners can read only their own employee record; authorized HR staff can review another employee subject to super-account safeguards. Inactive employment retains weekly history and pending review. Current person and employment history are verified before reading, and actor/permission checks run again afterward. The private, CSRF-protected read adds no event, audit, receipt or projection. Time entries continue using their existing audited CRUD rather than acquiring an unnecessary event stream.

The query selects overlapping entries for one employee and one week. The existing maximum shift of 24 elapsed hours provides a lower work-start bound, and the `sts_employee_work` index supports the employee/time range. At most 501 rows are read; more than 500 refuses the review rather than displaying incomplete totals. ## Institutional time review and teaching contact policy

Open **Team & timesheets → Time review policy**. Publishing requires both `sis:hr:manage` and the protected `sis:hr:time-policy` duty. One reviewed institution policy contains two separate calculations: clocked-time exception checks and teaching contact-hour conversion. It never calculates compensation, legal overtime, breaks, credit awards or payroll.

The fallback contact-hour rule is **50 instructional minutes per contact hour**, rounded half up to the nearest thousandth, expressed **per typical teaching week**. This is the user's selected default, consistent with the [University of Illinois contact-hour guidance](https://provost.illinois.edu/policies/policies/courses/credit-hour-definition/assigning-credit-hours/); it is not a claim that all institutions use the same definition. Institutions can publish a different 1–120 minute unit and nearest/down/up rounding. Section authors enter actual typical weekly instructional minutes; neither academic credits nor a cancelled/special meeting week supplies an assumed value. A fifty-minute classroom contact hour remains separate from sixty minutes of elapsed timesheet work.

Clocked-time checks start without imposed daily/weekly thresholds or overlap flags. Optional daily/weekly limits are positive **minutes**, with blank meaning no threshold. Select advisory warnings or required exception approval, optional overlap flags and the institution's timezone. The week runs Monday through Sunday. Pending and approved entries across the employee's positions contribute actual elapsed seconds, clipped at local day/week boundaries; rejected time is excluded. Overlapping intervals are summed, never silently deduplicated. These are operational review thresholds, not statutory overtime or paid hours. New overlapping submissions remain blocked by the existing invariant; overlap flags support review of existing/imported overlaps.

**Review time policy → Confirm time policy** preserves the draft on revision, requires a reason and uses an encrypted twenty-minute actor/operation/version proof. Publications append to `timesheet_policy` in the existing protected journal. An accepted retry returns the original publication after expiry or a later policy change, with current authority. Existing time decisions and published offerings retain their original rules. The small `sis_time_policy_state` table holds only a current revision/serialization gate; it is not a policy journal or cache authority. A current row lock plus protected-stream comparison refuses an enclosing stale database snapshot or projection disagreement. A mismatch never creates an observed policy or silently repairs history.

## Time-policy exceptions and retained decisions

Position selection during time submission now shows policy guidance for the entered interval. Daily/weekly flags do not prevent recording pending work. Existing employment dates, position coverage, elapsed-duration bounds and non-overlap submission checks still apply.

Open a pending **Time entry** from the employee or weekly review. The record and its approval confirmation show captured daily/weekly totals, flags, source count, policy version and timezone. Advisory flags permit the normal independent HR decision. Required exceptions additionally need `sis:hr:time-exception`, a reason, and the displayed current review revision. Employee owners cannot approve or reject their own work, including when they hold HR duties. Staff can reject flagged work without the exception duty.

Approval serializes role/person/employee and policy reads, verifies current employment/position coverage, and checks the target, neighboring intervals/statuses/versions, policy and authority again after the required audit. A changed source refuses the decision and retains entered form values. Refresh the record and review again; an old reason or revision does not grant an exception. A source or authority failure rolls back the journal, projection and audit together. A matching accepted retry returns the original decision and never reevaluates it under today's policy.

New decisions append `TimeEntryWasDecided` to one `timesheet_decision` stream per submitted entry in the **same `sis_history_events` table**. Frozen evidence retains employee/entry/position references, the actual policy, observed source intervals/versions/statuses, precise totals and decision attribution. It includes no person names, institutional IDs, wages or account credentials. Reasons and evidence are protected by the existing event codec. The current time row remains a reconstructible decision projection; working submissions continue as authorized audited CRUD. Old approved/rejected rows without a recorded decision are explicitly unrecorded, not reconstructed as earlier approvals.

Owners can read their own retained flags/totals/policy; HR exception reasons stay HR-only. Selected reads verify a recorded decision against the current row before returning curated details. A later policy or neighboring entry change never rewrites accepted decision evidence. Ordinary reports and jobs receive no raw decision payload. All browser writes retain framework CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression.

Each affected-week assessment includes at most **200 entries across at most two touched weeks**, and frozen evidence is capped at 55,000 JSON bytes. Over-limit approvals fail closed; the record still offers rejection with an explicitly selected-entry-only observation. Rejection does not claim a complete weekly assessment. Policy replay is bounded to 2,000 events, 4 MiB and five cooperative seconds; no automatic continuation or polling is added.
