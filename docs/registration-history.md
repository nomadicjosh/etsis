# Registration decisions and section order

Accepted registrations and early/late drops now have their own replayable enrollment history in **`sis_history_events`**. Each enrollment ULID identifies one `EnrollmentHistory` stream. [Academic-credit decisions](academic-credit-history.md) and finance remain separate domain facts, saved in the same transaction. There is one authoritative event table.

## Registration and staff workflow

Self-service, staff registration and reviewed batch registration use the same acceptance service. It acquires student/person/section locks before consistent eligibility reads, checks active status, prerequisites, applicable restrictions, registration window, web-registration policy, term course limit and actual remaining capacity, then records the accepted decision. Existing active registrations are verified and returned without another seat or charge. A closed late-drop enrollment requires a separate replacement/re-enrollment decision; an early retake receives a new enrollment and academic-credit identity.

The accepted event retains the enrollment timestamps/status, selected course publication when known, section/term codes, delivery mode, assigned faculty reference, capacity and fee/currency in use. It also retains the evaluated prerequisite identities, accepted result, registration window, course limit and staff/self-service context. This is a snapshot of the admission basis, not a claim that complete immutable section definitions, every supporting prerequisite-grade identity or all historical eligibility policy versions are already implemented.

In **Students → Student record → Registration history**, authorized registrars can review recorded enrollment decisions, including restricted early drops. The screen groups decisions by enrollment and shows effective/recorded times and section order. It has bounded pages, keyboard controls and narrow-screen reflow. Ordinary student ownership, teaching access and `sis:student:read` do not grant this restricted history: `sis:registration:manage` is required and rechecked after acquiring the student lock. Responses remain private/no-store and browser reads retain CSRF protection. Raw events, reasons, eligibility payloads, other students' identifiers and cross-enrollment predecessor references are withheld.

Early add/drop still deletes current enrollment/academic-credit projections and reverses the original course charge. Restricted enrollment and academic events remain. Late drop retains attempted credit, the institution's selected grade/GPA rules and the charge while releasing the seat. Hiatus closures delegate to the same drop service. Previously saved drop/hiatus receipts remain unchanged; their earlier contents are evidence, not a second canonical registration stream. Previously issued transcript documents remain independent retained evidence.

## Registration completion checks

The same command handles student self-service, individual staff entries and each new registration in a reviewed group. It now checks the trusted actor and required authority again after its academic, financial and audit writes, before returning success. Staff authority is required throughout a staff registration, including when staff register their own student record or enter another student without an explicit browser mode flag. A changed student/person link while waiting for locks stops the request instead of continuing with the former person's hold lock. A changed linked login account during completion also stops the original authorization.

Prerequisite verification may take time, so eligibility uses the clock after that verification. Before completion, the command checks the registration window and dated holds again through the existing `RegistrationPolicy`. A self-service request is rejected when that final check falls after its deadline or within a blocking hold's active dates. The existing inclusive end instant, staff window exception and institution-selected staff-blocking hold behavior remain in effect. These final checks use indexed current reads and the already verified prerequisite result; they do not replay academic inputs again.

A failed final permission, actor, identity, deadline or hold check rolls back the seat decision, academic credit and pursuit attribution, charge, receipts and required audit together. If a later student in a reviewed group fails, the outer group transaction rolls back earlier new registrations too. No partial group is accepted.

An already accepted active-registration retry still returns its original enrollment without another seat or charge, even after a deadline or new hold. Its history and current access are verified before that private result is returned; a retry does not bypass current authorization or change its original admission basis. This extension adds no event format, table or migration.

## Holds and restriction history

In **Holds & restrictions**, authorized office staff can find a person, place a hold, review private notes and set or shorten its end date. Staff with student-record read access can use the same tools under **Students → Student record → Restrictions & holds**. End-date changes now require a reason. The reason also appears as an encrypted, append-only staff note. **Review hold history** shows the original placement and later end-date decisions without displaying raw events or protected reasons. Notes remain available in the existing notes panel.

Dates are institutional calendar dates, including the entire end day. Severity 99 or higher blocks student registration while the hold is active. The separate staff-registration switch determines whether staff may register the student. Shortening an end date does not delete the hold, earlier decisions or notes. An already-ended hold cannot be rewritten through this screen. Creation and ending use actor-bound operation IDs: retrying an accepted form preserves its original decision and never restores an earlier end date after a later change. Changed retry inputs and stale new decisions are refused.

The parent is the **person**, including someone who later changes programs, graduates or becomes an employee. A single `person_restrictions` stream under that person's ULID retains placements and end-date changes in **`sis_history_events`**. It is not a second event table. The events retain the hold identity, code, severity, start/end dates and staff-blocking rule, with protected actor/reason, effective time, recorded time, predecessor and expected version. Conversations remain encrypted operational notes; appending a note does not append another hold decision.

For older untracked holds, reads create no events. The first real accepted change observes the current inventory and then records the actual new decision in the same transaction. An observation identifies the current observer/time, not an original placement date or author; earlier provenance remains unknown. A newly tracked person's inventory can be empty. No migration fabricates past hold decisions.

Registration, group registration and return-from-leave checks verify the current hold inventory against its recorded history. Verification detects a changed, missing or extra projection row after tracking begins. A discrepancy stops the request without accepting enrollment or billing changes; a web request never repairs the discrepancy automatically. Hold writes share registration's person lock, and journal append, projection, reason note and required audit either commit together or roll back together. Permission and actor are checked again before completion, including accepted retries.

Verification concerns one selected person, not an institution-wide list. Replay reads 50 events per page, with an allowance of 5,000 events and 500 retained holds per person. Oversized history uses the existing private records-office review response rather than continuing indefinitely. Staff lists and notes retain their 50-entry pages; curated history examines 20 decisions per page using protected, stream-bound cursors with a frozen committed head. Because multiple holds share a stream, a history page can contain no changes for the selected hold while another page remains available. Ordinary person/student directory searches do not replay hold histories.

SQLite rollback/reapply preserves recorded facts; index creation compiles for MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQLite, SQL Server and Oracle. Development validation applied this additive index to the configured MariaDB installation and verified that existing journal events, hold rows and notes were unchanged. Other PDO backends remain subject to framework schema support.

### Set up offices and delegate hold work

Offices are responsible work units such as Registrar, Student Accounts or Library. They are independent of academic departments and physical rooms. No fictional offices or automatic staff memberships are added to an institution's database.

1. An administrator with `admin:access:manage` opens **Roles & access → Offices & delegation**, or the same setup button in **Holds & restrictions**. Add an office code and readable name.
2. In **Roles & access**, grant appropriate staff the protected `sis:restrictions:manage` permission through a custom role or an extra user grant. The former combined registration permission no longer authorizes private hold management. Existing system roles remain protected; the system SIS group includes the new capability, and super authority remains unchanged.
3. Select **Manage delegates → Add delegate**, search for an enabled account, and set the start date and optional end date. Both days are included and use the institution calendar. Disable the delegation to revoke it immediately. Delegation does not add roles or permissions, registration rights, grades, account access or general student-record access.
4. Staff open **Holds & restrictions**. The worklist contains only their offices' holds, with name/ID search and fifty-entry pages. **Place a hold** first finds a person by name or institutional ID, then selects the owning office. A single eligible active office is selected automatically; multiple offices require an explicit choice. The private person search returns up to twenty-five minimal identities after at least two characters, without contact details or academic records.
5. **Review hold** connects the office, inclusive dates, effects, notes, retained decisions and ending action in one drawer. Administrative account-access authority plus hold-management permission permits cross-office review. Ordinary office staff cannot read or change another office's hold through direct URLs, notes, history or mutation endpoints.

Office setup and delegations use revision-checked, audited CRUD. They are current access relationships, not independent academic event streams. `sis_offices`, `sis_office_delegations` and `sis_restriction_offices` hold the small reference, delegation and routing projections. New hold placements/endings retain the accepted office identity/code/name and administrative or delegated authority, including the delegation identity/revision when used, inside the same protected hold event. Later office or delegation edits do not rewrite that evidence. Recorded office routing is checked against that history; a changed or missing routing projection cannot silently move a recorded hold to another office.

Administrative routing of an earlier unassigned hold requires an explicit **Set owning office** action and reason, stored as a private note and required audit. It does not claim an original office, create a past placement event, lift the hold or rewrite its rules. Staff outside administrative scope cannot access unassigned holds. Already assigned holds use the separate reviewed handoff below; this initial-routing action cannot transfer them.

Hold writes serialize on the person and owning office. Delegation changes use that same office lock, and transaction-time reads obtain current office/delegation rows after a lock wait. Completion rechecks the actor, role capability, enabled account, office and institution-calendar delegation date. Concurrent expiry or revocation rolls back events, routing, notes and required audit with the decision. Office retirement prevents new holds and ordinary delegated work; existing hold effects remain in force, and authorized administrators can still review/end them. Revocation and retirement never delete or automatically resolve a hold.

The worklist filters office scope in SQL before applying its page limit; other offices cannot fill a delegate's page or appear in totals. Opening a private hold verifies the relevant person's bounded history. Ordinary worklist/person searches do not replay every person's stream. Scope lookup is bounded to one hundred offices; oversized configurations require a separate administrative review rather than partial access results. Registration, group registration and return checks deliberately evaluate every applicable hold across offices, independently of whether the acting staff member can read those offices' private notes.

Earlier untracked current rows remain unassigned until administrative routing. ### Hand off a hold to another office

An office handoff changes who handles a hold, including access to its private notes and retained history. It does not end the hold, change its dates or change registration eligibility. Scheduled, active and ended holds can be handed off without rewriting their original decisions.

1. Grant designated coordinators **both** `sis:restrictions:manage` and the separate protected `sis:restrictions:handoff` permission. They also need current delegation to both the owning and receiving offices, unless they have explicit `admin:access:manage` authority. Merely assigning an office never grants either permission.
2. Open **Holds & restrictions → Review hold → Hand off to another office**. Choose a different active receiving office and enter a reason.
3. Select **Review office handoff**. Check the person, offices, hold details and number of notes. Review creates no decision, note or audit entry. **Change handoff** returns to the draft without losing its reason.
4. Acknowledge the receiving office's access to the hold, its notes and its history, then select **Confirm office handoff**. The reason becomes a protected staff note. Confirmation also clears the previous individual assignment and follow-up date, as shown in the review. Source-only delegates lose case access; eligible receiving-office delegates gain it. General student, academic and financial permissions remain separate.

A review is bound to its signed-in author and hold and expires after twenty minutes. A changed hold inventory, added note, individual follow-up, person identity label, office setup or delegation requires a fresh review. The protected review stays only in the open form, outside browser storage. A failed response can be retried with the same confirmation: an already accepted confirmation returns its original result without adding events or moving the hold back after a later handoff. Every retry still requires current permission and access to the hold's current office.

Confirmation shares the person/hold locks and acquires all involved office locks in a consistent order. It rechecks current authority, delegation dates, the active receiving office and review expiry before completion. One transaction appends `RestrictionOfficeWasChanged` to the existing person hold stream, moves its routing, saves the encrypted reason note and records the required audit. Failure rolls back the whole decision. The new fact retains both office identities and labels, the authority used and the actual delegation identity, revision and dates when applicable. Earlier placement/end events and original hold facts stay intact. Office and delegation setup remain audited CRUD; there is no additional queue or event table.

### Individual follow-up

Office staff can use **Review hold → Follow-up & assignment** to choose a responsible colleague and an optional follow-up date in one drawer. Search by name, choose a colleague, then **Save follow-up**. **Assign to me** is available when the signed-in staff member is eligible. **Leave unassigned** removes only the colleague from the draft; **Clear follow-up** clears both fields. Changes take effect when saved. No extra permission, case queue or student-facing notification is created.

An assignee must have an enabled account, the independent hold-management permission and a current dated delegation to the owning active office. Assignments never grant access. If that delegation or permission is later revoked, the assignment stays visible to authorized office staff with an access-review warning, while the former assignee loses access through the existing scope checks. Administrative cross-office authority alone does not make someone an eligible assignee: add an actual office delegation if they need individual work assigned there.

The existing worklist adds **All office work**, **Assigned to me**, **Unassigned** and **Due today or earlier** filters. Assignment, due date, search and office scope are applied in SQL before the fifty-entry page bound. Dates use the institution calendar. Undated work is excluded by the due filter. Ended holds can still have unfinished follow-up; ending a hold does not erase operational work. Clear the date when the follow-up is finished. Colleague search scans twenty-five dated, enabled delegates per page, checks their permissions with bounded reads and returns only account IDs and names. A page can have no eligible results while a next page is available; full-name searches narrow the choices.

Saving shares the person/hold/office locks, verifies the existing hold history, checks current scope and assignee eligibility, and commits the operational fields and required metadata audit together. The office identity and revision prevent stale edits and saves after a handoff. An immediate repeat of the same accepted save makes no duplicate change; an older retry cannot overwrite later work. Browser interruptions retain the open form for a retry. A handoff review now includes the current colleague/date, requires a fresh review if either changes, and clears both fields atomically with the accepted office change. Earlier hold decisions, notes and registration effects remain intact.

Existing holds keep their office routing, with no invented individual assignment or follow-up date. This is audited operational CRUD, with no new event type, table, queue or replay requirement. ## Retained prerequisite evidence

New registrations retain the current pursuit and exact supporting academic-record, award/enrollment and pursuit-applicability versions as protected `prerequisite_evidence` metadata on the existing acceptance event. The evidence contains only the required course identities and one verified passing record per course. It does not duplicate grade bodies, student profiles, reasons or raw historical events. Institutional and transfer work keep their different stream identities; an earlier untracked enrollment version remains explicitly zero. A recorded passing zero-credit course can satisfy a course prerequisite.

The command evaluates available verified work under its student lock, filters it to the current pursuit or explicitly approved reuse, then accepts the seat, credit, charge, evidence and required audit atomically. If several eligible records satisfy one prerequisite, it consistently selects the lowest credit ULID; source iteration order does not change the witness. This is a deterministic evidence choice, not a repeat-credit/GPA policy. Courses without prerequisites skip academic collection while retaining the actual empty requirement set and pursuit.

**Registration history** shows **Prerequisites at acceptance**, with required-course and supporting-record counts. It reads the retained acceptance decision, so a later grade correction, reuse revocation or program change does not rewrite why the student was registered. An accepted retry keeps its original result. Older acceptance events and observed earlier enrollments show **Supporting prerequisite references were not recorded**; the system never manufactures earlier evidence. These references are protected historical facts; ordinary browser/report responses expose only the curated status/counts.

### Group registration review

**Register a group → Review selected group** now shows a **Prerequisites** column: verified for this pursuit, missing prerequisites, no course prerequisites, or an existing registration whose earlier eligibility is kept. Missing prerequisites disable confirmation in the browser and are also refused by the backend. **Change selection** preserves the selected students.

The reviewed context includes a fingerprint of each new registration's selected evidence. Grade or reuse decisions that change that support require a fresh review before confirming; a still-passing grade amendment changes its academic version and therefore also invalidates that pending review. Already-completed batch retries return their original receipt before checking current prerequisites. Review creates no academic decisions, charges or audit-result records.

All new students' preview checks share the existing 400-page/2,000-event academic replay allowance. Confirmation uses one allowance across its context verification and registration rechecks; it does not cache earlier request results or reset the allowance for each student. An oversized or inconsistent history stops the operation with a private records-office notice, without a partial batch. Current actor/permission are checked after the history reads and before returning or committing. Restricted registration-history pages also share a bounded replay allowance across their twenty entries.

This extends the existing acceptance event with explicitly optional historical evidence: earlier events remain valid with unknown references. It adds no event type, schema migration, parallel journal, permission or inferred attribution. The shared journal's operator verifier now resolves both directions of the `work_pursuit` stream mapping, including its protected receipts; the isolated debugger run confirmed the missing `WorkPursuit` aggregate mapping before the repair.

Full versioned prerequisite/co-requisite expressions, minimum-grade prerequisite policies and complete eligibility-policy publication remain separate roadmap work. The current prerequisite contract requires all listed courses.

## Accepted server decision order

A section's locked allocation boundary assigns a monotonically increasing **section decision sequence** to each accepted allocation/release. The event retains that sequence, previous accepted decision reference and used-seat count before/after the decision. The unique section/sequence projection index guards duplicate ordinals. A failed registration, interrupted rolled-back batch, repeated active registration or exact completed-drop retry does not consume another committed sequence.

This order records accepted committed server decisions under the section lock. It is not browser-click order, request arrival order, a denied-request log or a waitlist/fairness policy. A batch commits its section decisions together in its deterministic processing order. Its partial decisions never become visible after rollback.

The first sequence begins when instrumentation accepts its first real action for that section. Its used-seat count can include older untracked enrollments; their earlier order is unknown. No migration manufactures their registrations. A current-state enrollment is observed only when a real reviewed closure needs its baseline; the observation has the current actor/time and no invented original admission basis. Merely reading history or retrying an existing older active registration creates no baseline event.

The next decision checks the retained index head against its protected enrollment stream, verifies routing/index consistency and compares the last committed used-seat count with current active enrollments. Altered counts, missing routing or inconsistent decision indexes stop the operation. A reduced capacity can prevent another allocation while still permitting a release. Current capacity/fee/code edits do not rewrite values retained by an earlier accepted decision.

This design replays a short stream for the relevant enrollment and latest section decision, rather than replaying every student's enrollment history for each seat. Capacity/course-load reads use indexed SQL counts rather than materializing a section's rows in PHP. Full reconciliation still needs controlled replay: as with the shared journal itself, database-wide rollback or coordinated corruption requires the documented external anchors and operational controls, not an unsupported claim of complete tamper proofing.

## Storage, deployment and replay

Apply `20261109000005_CreateSisRegistrationHistory` before deploying this write path. It creates two reconstructible support projections:

- `sis_registration_streams`: enrollment/student/section/term routing and latest section sequence. Routing survives deletion of the current early-drop row.
- `sis_registration_decisions`: operation/enrollment/section/playhead pointers and unique section sequence, with no event bodies or academic/eligibility payloads.

All primary identities are ULIDs; the sequence is an ordering value. Neither table is another event store. The migration also adds retry-safe section/status and student/term/status indexes for capacity and course-load counts. There are no synthetic-event backfills or live-data conversions. Down migration refuses to discard retained routing/order. Older recorded event schemas, bytes and operation receipts are preserved.

The new version-1 contracts are `enrollment-was-established` and `enrollment-was-closed`. Establishment distinguishes genuine acceptance from an observation of earlier current-state facts. Closure retains the actual early/late outcome and reviewed grace/policy reference. Expected-version append, protected context, projection/index changes, academic events, charges/reversals, receipts and audit share the unit of work. New accepted academic-credit events use the enrollment as their correlation/causation reference; earlier event context remains unchanged. Existing framework financial events retain their earlier attribution limitations and their enrollment operation references.

Replay reconstructs enrollment routing, order indexes and current enrollment rows without evaluating today's rules, assigning another seat, charging again, sending messages or recreating removed early-drop rows. A staging check reconstructs a removed enrollment's routing/order while leaving its current row absent. Production rebuild/swap tooling is still outstanding; web requests never repair corrupt projections automatically. Enrollment-row timestamps retain their existing UTC second precision, while event context retains its supplied effective instant/timezone and the journal's recorded time. Sequence provides ordering without relying on timestamp precision.
