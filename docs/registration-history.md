# Registration decisions and section order

Accepted registrations and early/late drops now have their own replayable enrollment history in **`sis_history_events`**. Each enrollment ULID identifies one `EnrollmentHistory` stream. [Academic-credit decisions](academic-credit-history.md) and finance remain separate domain facts, saved in the same transaction. There is one authoritative event table.

## Publish registration windows and course limits

In **Academic catalog → Terms**, open a term and choose **Registration policy**. Set when student registration opens/closes and the maximum number of registered courses. Enter a reason, choose **Review policy**, then **Publish policy**. **Revise** and **Policy history** preserve the unsaved form. Dates in this editor use the browser's stated timezone; the server receives explicit offsets and retains exact UTC instants. The institution timezone is retained with the publication. The closing instant remains inclusive.

Catalog maintenance permission is required for setup, publication and its private history. Registration duty alone does not grant it. Reviews belong to the reviewing account, expire after twenty minutes and become invalid when the term, earlier configuration or policy changes. Confirmation locks the term and atomically appends the protected publication, updates `sis_reg_windows` and writes the required audit. Repeating an accepted confirmation returns its original receipt without replacing a later policy.

Student registration uses the published window and the section's self-registration setting. Authorized staff retain their existing window/self-registration override; the course-count limit applies to both. This is a course-count limit, not a credit-load limit. Individual registration, grouped entry, schedule plans and section replacement share verification. A policy change requires a fresh pending registration review even when its dates and limit happen to be identical. Final checks roll back seats, credit, charges and audit if their reviewed policy changes before completion.

New enrollments retain the actual window dates and limit with their eligibility evidence. If a publication exists, they also retain its exact term/publication/version/hash reference in the same protected journal. Later policy changes leave earlier decisions and charges unchanged. Shared student/staff readiness shows the applicable dates, limit and whether a publication is recorded; restricted registration history shows the originally accepted values. These summaries omit raw event envelopes and protected reasons.

Existing window rows are not converted into invented approvals. The first genuine publication may record the earlier setup as an observation, with its original author/date explicitly unknown. Older enrollment eligibility remains intact. A missing or changed projection for a known publication blocks new registration until repaired; it is never silently treated as unpublished setup.

Migration `20261120000002_AddSisRegistrationPolicyReferences` adds nullable reference columns and the nullable section self-registration fact. It does not rewrite earlier events, settings or publications. The migration has SQLite integration and five supported SQL-dialect compiler coverage; this does not certify every PDO driver or live database engine. Bounded verification on the configured MariaDB database preserved all twenty existing events and the event/stream/operation checksums.

## Review and publish term drop policies

In **Academic catalog → Terms**, open a term and choose **Drop / withdrawal policy**. Add any dated grade periods and a reason, choose **Review policy**, then **Publish policy**. The review shows the exact deadline, timezone and grade periods being approved. **Revise** keeps entered values. **Policy history** shows retained publications in twenty-entry pages without exposing protected command reasons or raw event envelopes. Catalog-management authority is required; registration authority alone does not permit policy changes or this history.

Publication retains the term code, term dates, inclusive drop/add deadline, institution timezone and ordered grade-code periods. These are immutable definitions in the existing protected `sis_history_events` journal, under one `term_drop_policy` stream per term. `sis_term_drop_policies` is the verified current projection, with a publication ULID and calendar snapshot. There is no second event store and no parallel editable copy of the grade periods.

New staff, student and guided-hiatus drop decisions use the latest applicable published policy at confirmation—an active section override, otherwise the term policy— and their academic events retain its publication/version/hash reference alongside the actual grade and financial outcome. Grade codes resolve through the student's retained grading definition. Publication is not retroactive: completed drops, prior receipts, earned records and charges are unchanged. Early drop/add still removes current enrollment/academic projections and reverses the original course charge; later drops retain the attempt and charge. A pending drop must be reviewed again if its policy changes, even when its visible grade periods look identical.

Editing live term dates does not overwrite a published drop deadline. The policy editor flags the difference; review and publish the updated setup when it should govern future drops. A changed institutional timezone requires a new publication before another drop can proceed, avoiding ambiguous calendar-date interpretation. Sections inherit this term policy unless catalog staff publish an explicit [section override](#section-drop-policy-overrides).

Unpublished terms retain the existing default W marker, excluded from GPA, and their live term deadline. Earlier saved custom periods remain usable but explicitly lack a retained publication. Reads and migration do not manufacture policy events. Their first actual publication records the previous current revision/periods as an observation at that time, without inventing the earlier approving actor, approval date or missing calendar definitions. Earlier drop events remain unchanged and unlinked where their source is unknown.

Reviews are actor-bound and expire after twenty minutes. Confirmation rechecks authority, calendar, grade choices, displayed revision and stream position under the term lock. An accepted retry returns its original publication even after expiry or later changes. Event append, projection and required audit commit together. Ordinary policy resolution verifies one authenticated self-contained head against the current projection; publication commands verify the predecessor chain, bounded to 1,000 publications. Further publication at that boundary requires operator review. Discrepancies stop decisions without repairing rows on a web request. These checks do not complete other historical eligibility policies or projection-rebuild tooling.

Migration `20261117000011` adds nullable publication references/calendar snapshots only. Existing policies, receipts and events are preserved; no historical cutover is inferred.

## Review eligibility and registration charges together

Students use the portal's **Review & register** action. Staff use **Students → Student record → Register student**, or the existing **Register a group** screen for up to 25 selected students. These entry points now share one registration review and confirmation service, the existing acceptance command and one price calculator. Registration, AcademicRecord, Finance and Portal retain their separate responsibilities; their accepted registration, credit and monetary facts commit in the same transaction.

The review shows the current course/section, academic-term dates, credits, seats, pursuit-scoped prerequisite readiness, timetable assessment and proposed new charges. Activity, applicable holds, course limits and self-service availability use the same eligibility policy as acceptance. A confirmed meeting overlap follows the institution's existing block/warn setting; unpublished schedules remain explicitly not checked. A review does not reserve a seat, create an account, append a domain event, or promise that later timetable publications cannot create conflicts.

**New registration charges** includes the section fee, applicable one-time term fees and credit-based prices using the actual course credits. Credit prices share the existing integer half-up calculation used for posting. Previously assessed flat fees are labeled **Not added with this registration** without revealing whether a previous entry was reversed. An existing active enrollment shows no new charges. Mixed currencies and unsupported amounts block confirmation with an explanation; currencies are never silently converted or summed together.

For example, a **10.00 section fee**, a **5.00 new term fee** and **3 credits at 1.25 per credit** produce **18.75** in proposed charges. Another registration in that term adds **13.75** when the same term fee has already been assessed. The display describes new charges, not an account balance, a payment request or a financial-aid award. Registration authority does not grant finance-history or balance access; those fields and account identities are omitted from this response.

Choose **Confirm registration** after reviewing. **Refresh review** keeps the selected section or student group and obtains a fresh assessment and price. Group reviews show each student's proposed amount; fee details load into the page only when their native disclosure is opened. Prices and protected academic inputs are calculated on the server. Changing visible browser fields cannot supply charge instructions or replace the reviewed student selection.

Reviews expire after 20 minutes and are bound to the signed-in actor, student/group, section, operation, displayed revision and staff/self-service context. Confirmation rechecks the current selected context under ordered student/person/section/course locks. Changed pricing, account state, prerequisite support, timetable evidence or displayed academic facts require another review. Accepted retries return their original result after expiry, later changes or a subsequent drop, while current actor/owner/permission checks still apply. They never recreate the dropped enrollment or charge it again. Existing active enrollment keeps its identity and accepted facts; closed-enrollment replacement remains a separate registrar workflow.

The final command uses the verified pricing view from that confirmation rather than looking up another set of rules midway through posting. Applicable rules are loaded once for a selected group and shared under the bounded pricing/prerequisite replay allowance; each student retains a separate account and quote. A rule committed after the transaction's verified definition view applies to later work, rather than establishing a global lock on all billing configuration. Existing account expected-version checks and acceptance-time eligibility/timetable checks still apply. Failure of a selected registration, required audit, final authority or review-expiry check rolls back the entire affected group, including seats, academic credits, charges and the operation receipt.

Student self-service is restricted to one owned student record and cannot turn a student proof into a staff override or a multi-student batch. Staff entry requires `sis:registration:manage`; the portal always uses self-service policy even if the account also has staff privileges. CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression protect both entry points. Tokens remain in the active private form, without URL parameters, browser storage, logs or jobs.

This alpha API change requires clients to review before browser/API confirmation. `/sis/registrations/review/` accepts `student_id`, `section_id` and `by_staff`; `/portal/api/registration/review/` accepts the owned student and section. Confirmation sends the returned `operation_id`, `revision` and `review_token` with the same selection. Group confirmation now also requires its returned review token. The internal acceptance command remains available to authorized domain orchestration. Existing completed receipts remain readable, and no earlier events, fees or receipt rows are converted. Pending earlier review screens need a refresh.

No migration or new event store is needed. Completed results reuse `sis_registration_batches` as operation support containing registration identifiers/statuses; authoritative academic, registration and financial decisions remain in the same protected journal. ## Review several course sections together

Staff use **Students → Student record → Registration → Plan registration**. Students open **Course registration**, select their student record and use **Add to schedule** on course-search results. Both editors keep up to ten distinct section choices in page memory, allow removal and search without loading a full course directory, and send the same bounded selection to the existing registration review service. The student identity remains visible in the review. No seat is reserved and no account, enrollment, credit or historical event is created while selecting or reviewing.

**Review schedule** shows every selected section with expandable eligibility, prerequisites, timetable and charge details, plus the combined new charges. The review checks overlaps between proposed sections as well as conflicts with existing enrollments, using the institution's existing block/warn policy. Unpublished meeting schedules remain explicitly unchecked. Course limits include all proposed sections in the same term. Selecting a prerequisite course does not count it as completed work. Existing active or closed enrollments must be reviewed separately rather than silently moved or reopened by a schedule plan.

Term-wide fees are counted once within each billing term; each section fee and registered-credit price is counted separately using the existing integer pricing calculation. For example, section fees of 10.00 and 20.00, one new term fee of 5.00 and six credits at 1.00 per credit produce 41.00. Already assessed or reversed term fees retain the existing billing behavior. Totals stay separate by currency, and sections within one billing term must use the same currency. Unsupported amounts produce an explicit pricing-review result rather than an approximate or partial total. The response does not disclose account balances or account identities.

**Revise selection** preserves the chosen sections. **Confirm schedule** rechecks the reviewed context, then uses the same per-section registration, academic-credit and financial acceptance path as individual registration. All selected student/person/section/course locks are acquired in a consistent order. Every accepted section is checked again after the final required audit; changed prices, selected definitions, meetings/policy, owner/actor/authority, eligibility or expiry roll back every registration, credit, charge, receipt and audit in this confirmation. The selection is bounded and shares the existing history-read budget; it cannot bypass prerequisite, seat, hold or financial rules.

Reviews expire after twenty minutes and bind the actor, student, distinct section set, staff/self-service channel, operation and displayed revision. The browser sends only this reviewed confirmation; it cannot supply alternative charge amounts. If a confirmation response is interrupted, **Confirm schedule** retries the same operation and proof. A previously accepted retry returns its original receipt even after expiry or a later drop, without restoring removed enrollment/credit projections or charging again. Refreshing an unaccepted changed selection requires another review. Staff completion shows the saved schedule reference, with actions to return to the student or plan another schedule. The existing staff registration-receipt lookup can also display that coordinated result. Staff cannot use another actor's receipt, and portal actions verify the signed-in owner rather than accepting staff flags from the browser.

The coordinator extends the existing `RegistrationBatchService`, quote calculator and timetable reader. Each accepted section retains its normal independent enrollment/academic/financial history; the existing `sis_registration_batches` support table holds the coordinated result and accepted section identities. Its first canonical section is a routing reference, not a new academic authority. The working schedule is not another event-sourced aggregate. No schema migration, new journal, historical backfill or compatibility model is introduced.

Staff endpoints are `POST /sis/registration-plans/review/` and `/commit/`; owner endpoints are `POST /portal/api/registration-plan/review/` and `/commit/`. Review takes `student_id` and `section_ids`; confirmation also takes `operation_id`, `revision` and `review_token`. Staff routes enforce registration-management authority; owner routes enforce self-service. Both retain framework CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression. Browser selections and proofs use no local/session storage. Closed-attempt reinstatement, larger/background populations and formal live multi-engine concurrency acceptance remain separate roadmap work.

## Registration and staff workflow

Self-service, staff registration and reviewed batch registration use the same acceptance service. It acquires student/person/section locks before consistent eligibility reads, checks active status, prerequisites, applicable restrictions, registration window, web-registration policy, term course limit and actual remaining capacity, then records the accepted decision. Existing active registrations are verified and returned without another seat or charge. A closed late-drop enrollment requires a separate replacement/re-enrollment decision; an early retake receives a new enrollment and academic-credit identity.

The accepted event retains the enrollment timestamps/status, selected course publication when known, section/term codes, delivery mode, assigned faculty reference, capacity and fee/currency in use. It also retains the evaluated prerequisite identities, accepted result, registration window, course limit and staff/self-service context. This is a snapshot of the admission basis, not a claim that complete immutable section definitions, every supporting prerequisite-grade identity or all historical eligibility policy versions are already implemented.

In **Students → Student record → Registration history**, authorized registrars can review recorded enrollment decisions, including restricted early drops. The screen groups decisions by enrollment and shows effective/recorded times and section order. It has bounded pages, keyboard controls and narrow-screen reflow. Ordinary student ownership, teaching access and `sis:student:read` do not grant this restricted history: `sis:registration:manage` is required and rechecked after acquiring the student lock. Responses remain private/no-store and browser reads retain CSRF protection. Raw events, reasons, eligibility payloads, other students' identifiers and cross-enrollment predecessor references are withheld.

Early add/drop still deletes current enrollment/academic-credit projections and reverses the original course charge. Restricted enrollment and academic events remain. Late drop retains attempted credit, the institution's selected grade/GPA rules and the charge while releasing the seat. Hiatus closures delegate to the same drop service. Previously saved drop/hiatus receipts remain unchanged; their earlier contents are evidence, not a second canonical registration stream. Previously issued transcript documents remain independent retained evidence.

Prerequisite authoring also supports “at least two of these three courses” and the same course in several requirements. The shared student/staff/group/schedule review shows the minimum and verified distinct-course count for each requirement. Repeated attempts do not count as different courses. Only verified passing work allowed for the current pursuit can qualify. See [Prerequisite choices](course-publication.md#prerequisite-choices) for setup and limits.

The safe response adds `required_count` and `completed_count` alongside each group's existing course positions and status; it does not return supporting academic identities. New accepted evidence retains explicit count lists inside the same protected enrollment event. Older choice evidence without those counts retains its original one-course meaning. Count changes stale pending confirmations; accepted retries preserve their original result. At acceptance, the selected course facts are checked against the protected publication under the existing bounded command allowance. Modified projection counts cannot authorize a weaker registration.

### Minimum grades within prerequisite requirements

Staff may set an optional per-course grade-point minimum for each prerequisite requirement. The registration review shows that minimum and how many distinct courses qualify. Only verified passing institutional/transfer work allowed for the current pursuit is considered; GPA inclusion and credit quantity do not substitute for per-course grade points. A shared course can meet one requirement but fall below another's minimum. Blank thresholds preserve passing-only behavior; an explicit zero still requires a recorded passing grade.

Rules with grade minima select one strongest passing attempt per course and retain its exact point value with the protected supporting reference. Later grade corrections invalidate pending reviews but cannot rewrite accepted evidence or repeat earlier registration/charges. Earlier evidence without grade minima keeps its exact format and meaning. Public readiness responses contain requirement minima and counts, not supporting grade records or their identities. Course publication, clone, removal and projection checks use the same current rule model; no separate event stream or reporting exposure is introduced. ### Shared course verification during confirmation

A registration confirmation verifies each selected immutable course publication once and reuses that verified definition for the affected students and final checks. Every current course projection is still compared with the protected facts. Different publications of one course remain independent, and each section retains its own assessed policy. A 25-student fictional group with 21 publications reads that course history in two pages total; a new command starts a fresh verification. Ordinary listings and browser previews retain their existing bounded read-model queries.

New registrations take the target course lock after the student/person/section locks, sharing the boundary used by catalog and prerequisite editing. Final checks compare the assessed and current course identity, publication, credits, activity and prerequisite rule after the required audit. Reviewed groups also check after their coordinator audit; a changed source rolls back all affected enrollment, academic, financial, audit and receipt changes. Multi-section plans share the same verification through their existing completion checks. Successful earlier retries keep their original result without checking a newer course policy.

Only verified immutable course definitions and small per-section fingerprints are reused within that confirmation. Student academic inputs, holds, access and balances retain their separate checks. The map is capped at 100 publications/section selections, shares the existing history budget and is discarded after the invocation. A partial or failed stream scan never becomes a verified entry. This adds no persistent cache, schema, journal or recovery workflow. Production concurrency/load acceptance across database engines remains separate.

## Registration completion checks

The same command handles student self-service, individual staff entries and each new registration in a reviewed group. It now checks the trusted actor and required authority again after its academic, financial and audit writes, before returning success. Staff authority is required throughout a staff registration, including when staff register their own student record or enter another student without an explicit browser mode flag. A changed student/person link while waiting for locks stops the request instead of continuing with the former person's hold lock. A changed linked login account during completion also stops the original authorization.

Prerequisite verification may take time, so eligibility uses the clock after that verification. Before completion, the command checks the registration window and dated holds again through the existing `RegistrationPolicy`. A self-service request is rejected when that final check falls after its deadline or within a blocking hold's active dates. The existing inclusive end instant, staff window exception and institution-selected staff-blocking hold behavior remain in effect. These final checks use indexed current reads and the already verified prerequisite result; they do not replay academic inputs again.

A failed final permission, actor, identity, deadline or hold check rolls back the seat decision, academic credit and pursuit attribution, charge, receipts and required audit together. If a later student in a reviewed group fails, the outer group transaction rolls back earlier new registrations too. No partial group is accepted.

An already accepted active-registration retry still returns its original enrollment without another seat or charge, even after a deadline or new hold. Its history and current access are verified before that private result is returned; a retry does not bypass current authorization or change its original admission basis. This extension adds no event format, table or migration.

## Holds and restriction history

In **Holds & restrictions**, authorized office staff can find a person, place a hold, review private notes and set or shorten its end date. Staff with student-record read access can use the same tools under **Students → Student record → Restrictions & holds**. End-date changes now require a reason. The reason also appears as an encrypted, append-only staff note. **Review hold history**, available under **More** when there are several secondary actions, shows the original placement and later end-date decisions without displaying raw events or protected reasons. Notes remain available in the existing notes panel.

Dates are institutional calendar dates, including the entire end day. Severity 99 or higher blocks student registration while the hold is active. The separate staff-registration switch determines whether staff may register the student. Shortening an end date does not delete the hold, earlier decisions or notes. An already-ended hold cannot be rewritten through this screen. Creation and ending use actor-bound operation IDs: retrying an accepted form preserves its original decision and never restores an earlier end date after a later change. Changed retry inputs and stale new decisions are refused.

The parent is the **person**, including someone who later changes programs, graduates or becomes an employee. A single `person_restrictions` stream under that person's ULID retains placements and end-date changes in **`sis_history_events`**. It is not a second event table. The events retain the hold identity, code, severity, start/end dates and staff-blocking rule, with protected actor/reason, effective time, recorded time, predecessor and expected version. Conversations remain encrypted operational notes; appending a note does not append another hold decision.

For older untracked holds, reads create no events. The first real accepted change observes the current inventory and then records the actual new decision in the same transaction. An observation identifies the current observer/time, not an original placement date or author; earlier provenance remains unknown. A newly tracked person's inventory can be empty. No migration fabricates past hold decisions.

Registration, group registration and return-from-leave checks verify the current hold inventory against its recorded history. Verification detects a changed, missing or extra projection row after tracking begins. A discrepancy stops the request without accepting enrollment or billing changes; a web request never repairs the discrepancy automatically. Hold writes share registration's person lock, and journal append, projection, reason note and required audit either commit together or roll back together. Staff lists and notes retain their 50-entry pages; curated history examines 20 decisions per page using protected, stream-bound cursors with a frozen committed head. Because multiple holds share a stream, a history page can contain no changes for the selected hold while another page remains available. Ordinary person/student directory searches do not replay hold histories.

### Set up offices and delegate hold work

Offices are responsible work units such as Registrar, Student Accounts or Library. They are independent of academic departments and physical rooms. No fictional offices or automatic staff memberships are added to an institution's database.

1. An administrator with `admin:access:manage` opens **Roles & access → Offices & delegation**, or the same setup button in **Holds & restrictions**. Add an office code and readable name.
2. In **Roles & access**, grant appropriate staff the protected `sis:restrictions:manage` permission through a custom role or an extra user grant. The former combined registration permission no longer authorizes private hold management. Existing system roles remain protected; the system SIS group includes the new capability, and super authority remains unchanged.
3. Select **Manage delegates → Add delegate**, search for an enabled account, and set the start date and optional end date. Both days are included and use the institution calendar. Disable the delegation to revoke it immediately. Delegation does not add roles or permissions, registration rights, grades, account access or general student-record access.
4. Staff open **Holds & restrictions**. The worklist contains only their offices' holds, with name/ID search and fifty-entry pages. **Place a hold** first finds a person by name or institutional ID, then selects the owning office. A single eligible active office is selected automatically; multiple offices require an explicit choice. The private person search returns up to twenty-five minimal identities after at least two characters, without contact details or academic records.
5. **Review hold** connects the office, inclusive dates, effects, notes, retained decisions and ending action in one drawer. Administrative account-access authority plus hold-management permission permits cross-office review. Ordinary office staff cannot read or change another office's hold through direct URLs, notes, history or mutation endpoints.

Office setup and delegations use revision-checked, audited CRUD. They are current access relationships, not independent academic event streams. `sis_offices`, `sis_office_delegations` and `sis_restriction_offices` hold the small reference, delegation and routing projections. New hold placements/endings retain the accepted office identity/code/name and administrative or delegated authority, including the delegation identity/revision when used, inside the same protected hold event. Later office or delegation edits do not rewrite that evidence. Recorded office routing is checked against that history; a changed or missing routing projection cannot silently move a recorded hold to another office.

Administrative routing of an earlier unassigned hold requires an explicit **Set owning office** action and reason, stored as a private note and required audit. It does not claim an original office, create a past placement event, lift the hold or rewrite its rules. Staff outside administrative scope cannot access unassigned holds. Already assigned holds use the separate reviewed handoff below; this initial-routing action cannot transfer them.

Hold writes serialize on the person and owning office. Delegation changes use that same office lock, and transaction-time reads obtain current office/delegation rows after a lock wait. Completion rechecks the actor, role capability, enabled account, office and institution-calendar delegation date. Concurrent expiry or revocation rolls back events, routing, notes and required audit with the decision. Office retirement prevents new holds and ordinary delegated work; existing hold effects remain in force, and authorized administrators can still review/end them. Revocation and retirement never delete or automatically resolve a hold.

The worklist filters office scope in SQL before applying its page limit; other offices cannot fill a delegate's page or appear in totals. Opening a private hold verifies the relevant person's bounded history. Ordinary worklist/person searches do not replay every person's stream. Scope lookup is bounded to one hundred offices; oversized configurations require a separate administrative review rather than partial access results. Registration, group registration and return checks deliberately evaluate every applicable hold across offices, independently of whether the acting staff member can read those offices' private notes.

### Hand off a hold to another office

An office handoff changes who handles a hold, including access to its private notes and retained history. It does not end the hold, change its dates or change registration eligibility. Scheduled, active and ended holds can be handed off without rewriting their original decisions.

1. Grant designated coordinators **both** `sis:restrictions:manage` and the separate protected `sis:restrictions:handoff` permission. They also need current delegation to both the owning and receiving offices, unless they have explicit `admin:access:manage` authority. Merely assigning an office never grants either permission.
2. Open **Holds & restrictions → Review hold → More → Hand off**. Choose a different active receiving office and enter a reason.
3. Select **Review office handoff**. Check the person, offices, hold details and number of notes. Review creates no decision, note or audit entry. **Change handoff** returns to the draft without losing its reason.
4. Acknowledge the receiving office's access to the hold, its notes and its history, then select **Confirm office handoff**. The reason becomes a protected staff note. Confirmation also clears the previous individual assignment and follow-up date, as shown in the review. Source-only delegates lose case access; eligible receiving-office delegates gain it. General student, academic and financial permissions remain separate.

A review is bound to its signed-in author and hold and expires after twenty minutes. A changed hold inventory, added note, individual follow-up, person identity label, office setup or delegation requires a fresh review. The protected review stays only in the open form, outside browser storage. A failed response can be retried with the same confirmation: an already accepted confirmation returns its original result without adding events or moving the hold back after a later handoff. Every retry still requires current permission and access to the hold's current office.

Confirmation shares the person/hold locks and acquires all involved office locks in a consistent order. It rechecks current authority, delegation dates, the active receiving office and review expiry before completion. One transaction appends `RestrictionOfficeWasChanged` to the existing person hold stream, moves its routing, saves the encrypted reason note and records the required audit. Failure rolls back the whole decision. The new fact retains both office identities and labels, the authority used and the actual delegation identity, revision and dates when applicable. ## Per-section waitlists

Every section has its own **Allow waitlisting for this section** setting, disabled by default. Under **Academic catalog → Sections → selected section → Waitlist policy**, authorized staff review a reasoned change and confirm it within twenty minutes. The institution selects that section's active-request limit (one through one hundred; initial suggested limit fifty) and explicitly accepts **first accepted, first served** ordering with **oldest request may register** allocation. This policy is a retained operational registration decision; it does not edit the course or rewrite an earlier section publication. Enabling one section never enables another. Disabling new requests or reducing the limit preserves existing places and their priority.

Students use **Course registration → full section → Waitlist options**, select their student record and review/confirm joining. **Your waitlist requests** shows their own waiting places and completed outcomes, with ten-request pages. A waiting student may review and confirm withdrawal; joining again creates a new request at the back. Joining records interest only: no enrollment, credit, charge, reserved seat, prerequisite waiver or eligibility guarantee results. An active student, active section/course and no current or closed enrollment are required. Owner requests also require student self-registration and an open registration window. Joining is available when the section is full or has an existing queue, within its limit. Staff retain the existing registration-window override; no browser flag supplies staff authority.

A section queue displays twenty active requests per page in accepted order, with explicitly current names and institutional IDs. **Review removal** requires a reason and binds the selected request identity, so an old row cannot remove a replacement request. The removal reason remains restricted; students receive their own outcome without private reasons, other students' identities or raw journal data. Queue access and staff interest changes require both `sis:registration:waitlist` and `sis:registration:manage`. Policy setup requires `sis:registration:policy` and registration management. The new protected duty receives no automatic role or user grants. Assigned teaching access alone does not grant queue or policy access.

When a seat is available, the oldest waiting student or authorized registrar opens the **existing eligibility-and-fee registration review**. Holds, prerequisite/co-requisite rules, meeting conflicts, current publications, window rules, capacity and charges are all checked normally. A blocked oldest request keeps its place until withdrawn or removed through reasoned review; the system never silently skips it. Individual, grouped, schedule-plan and section-change registration all use the same seat guard. Later requests and nonqueued students cannot take an available seat ahead of the queue, including after new joining is disabled. Register waiting students sequentially; coordinated allocation of several waiting students in one group is not supported. The enrollment, authoritative academic credit, financial charge, completed waitlist request, accepted histories, receipt and required audit commit or roll back together. Retrying an accepted registration cannot register or charge again, including after a later drop.

Queue priority follows the protected section stream's accepted sequence under the existing section lock. Browser timestamps, preview ULIDs and preview order establish no priority. Two join reviews can coexist; whichever confirmation is durably accepted first receives the earlier place. Every confirmation rechecks capacity, current policy and source eligibility. Withdrawals/removals pin the exact request; policy changes pin the reviewed section stream version. Actor-bound protected proofs expire after twenty minutes. An original accepted retry returns its original receipt after expiry or subsequent resolution under current authorization; it never reopens a request. Unaccepted stale or expired reviews require another review. Browser drafts/proofs use no local or session storage.

`section_waitlist` uses typed `WaitlistPolicyWasSet` and `WaitlistPlaceWasChanged` events in the **existing `sis_history_events` journal**. Attributed command context retains effective/recorded dates, reasons and operation identities. The registered outcome references the genuine enrollment even if an early drop later removes its current projection. `sis_waitlist_state` is an encrypted, reconstructible current-policy/active-place projection; `sis_waitlist_entries` is a minimized indexed discovery projection. Both are checked against protected history for selected decisions. They are not additional event journals. Missing/damaged/current-read disagreement fails closed. No earlier requests or priorities are inferred from enrollment rows.

Execution is deliberately bounded: one hundred active places per section, two thousand accepted stream events, no new join once the stream reaches 1,800 events and no policy change once it reaches 1,900. Reserved space permits resolution of existing places. Selected queue commands share a 6,000-event, four-MiB, five-second cooperative replay allowance; grouped/schedule context uses the existing stricter shared academic-history budget and verifies a section once per group. Owner discovery shares its allowance across the page. Exhausted verification saves no partial decision. Larger/long-lived queues need separately qualified checkpoint/archive/rebuild support. There are no automatic offers, held seats, expirations, notifications, ranked priority categories, lotteries or bypass overrides. Those require explicit additional institutional policies. Live multi-engine contention, large-institution load and institutional retention/disposition acceptance remain separate deployment gates.

Staff API roots are `/sis/sections/{id}/waitlist/` and its `policy/` resource; mutations use `review/` and `confirm/`, and student-specific staff context uses `context/`. Owner reads use `/portal/api/waitlists/{student}/` and `sections/{id}/`, with reviewed mutations beneath the latter. Selected-request review requires explicit `entry_id` (null for joining, the exact active request for withdrawal/removal). Every owner route checks the canonical person/account link, even for privileged users. Browser mutations retain framework CSRF; responses are private/no-store with debug collection suppressed.
