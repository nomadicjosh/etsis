# Student degree-audit exceptions

A **course waiver belongs to one student-program pursuit**. It removes the requirement to take the course wherever it is required within that program, regardless of course group. A dual-degree pursuit needs its own approval. Waiving a course never creates an earned award, grade, GPA points, resident credit or a financial reversal.

A **minimum exception** explicitly reduces a published credit/GPA threshold for that student. It is separate from a waiver and applies to the exact degree publication reviewed. Neither action changes the institution's published curriculum or another student's requirements.

A **course-publication acceptance** allows a specific additional publication of a required course for one student-program pursuit and selected degree publication. It preserves the student's actual academic record. Use curriculum setup instead when the allowance should apply to everyone using a newly published course group.

These controls retain approved inputs and show their effective thresholds. The [course-group placement review](student-course-placement.md) now applies approved waivers, substitutions and group minimum/grade-floor reductions. Approved general minimum reductions now also appear in the [combined degree-progress review](student-degree-progress.md), alongside their original published thresholds. Specialization-specific exceptions and component routing use these same controls; retained final degree evaluations remain a subsequent stage; these controls do not award credentials or manufacture academic facts.

## Setup and permissions

Apply `20261109000000_CreateSisRequirementAdjustments` after `20261108000007_CreateSisStudentRequirements` and its dependencies. Development tooling has not applied it to a working database. The migration creates an active-projection table and registers the protected **`sis:degree-audit:adjust`** permission. It creates no second event table and backfills no approvals.

Approvals and revocations require both `sis:degree-audit:adjust` and `sis:student:read`. Catalog, registration and requirement-assignment authority alone are insufficient. The new permission is independent of the broad `sis` permission, and the migration does not expand existing role/user grants. Super administrators retain their universal authority. An access administrator can grant it to a custom registrar role or as an additional user permission; protected system definitions remain uneditable.

Owners and authorized student readers can inspect active approvals and curated history without approving or revoking them. No new faculty/advisor-wide student access is granted.

## Staff workflow

1. Open **Students → the student → Student programs → Degree requirements → Student exceptions** for the relevant program pursuit. Assign a reviewed degree publication first if none is selected. Resolve a closed program or pending cohort correction before changing approvals.
2. Choose **Approve an exception**. The full-width workspace has searchable targets on the left and approval details on the right. The source displays at most 30 cards at a time and supports paging; it does not build thousands of DOM controls.
3. For a **Course waiver**, choose the required course itself. There is no group selector. The review lists all groups where the course currently appears, as context. A waiver follows that stable course identity throughout this same pursuit if its selected degree version later changes. If the course is absent from the current requirements, the approval remains retained without adding a requirement or earned credit. It becomes relevant wherever the course is required again in that pursuit, until revoked.
4. For a **Requirement minimum**, choose a degree or group threshold and enter the **new required value**, not the amount to subtract. The review shows the published value and the proposed value. Supported degree fields are total credits, resident credits, cumulative GPA and overall GPA. Groups support their credit minimum, course-count minimum and optional per-course grade-point floor when the selected publication defines them; older groups retain their original average-GPA minimum. The earlier average-GPA exception never changes the per-course threshold. Course counts accept whole numbers only; credits and grade points retain thousandth-unit precision. A missing/no-minimum field cannot be reduced. Decimal values support up to three places and use integer thousandths in the domain/API.
5. Enter a reason, choose **Review exception**, inspect its exact effect, then **Approve reviewed exception**. Returning with **Change review** preserves the selected target, amount and reason. Review alone changes nothing.
6. Approving another exception for the same minimum replaces the active exception with a new decision and retains the earlier approval. The uniqueness boundary includes the degree version. A later degree publication does not inherit the old minimum reduction; it remains visible as outside the current requirements until explicitly reviewed/revoked. Course waivers use the pursuit/course identity instead of a group or degree-version key.
7. **Review revocation → Prepare revocation → Revoke reviewed exception** requires its own reason and retains the original approval. Revoking a minimum exception restores the published threshold, subject to other active exceptions. Revoking a waiver restores the course requirement where applicable in that program.
8. **Exception history** lists approvals, replacements and revocations in bounded pages. Approval labels resolve their original published course/group references, never today's mutable catalog titles. Ordinary history omits protected reasons and actor details; a revocation references its original approval.

Resident-credit requirements cannot exceed total-credit requirements after applying exceptions. Reduce the resident minimum before approving a lower total that depends on it; restore the total before revoking a dependent resident exception. Published rules, unwaived course requirements and unrelated thresholds remain intact. Waivers never silently reduce credit-pool, choose-count or GPA requirements; the evaluator must apply the retained course-waiver policy explicitly when it is implemented.

The **Degree requirements** screen identifies itself as the published baseline and links to **Student exceptions**, whose current minimums include approved reductions. This distinction prevents presenting an altered student requirement as an altered publication. Neither screen claims a completed student degree audit.

## Group course-count and grade-point reductions

A registrar can reduce **Required courses** for one published group in this student's selected degree. Enter the new whole-number total, including zero when explicitly approved. The required course lists and choose-count rules remain in force: reducing the group's total does not itself waive a named required course or change a card's rule. A separate course waiver still has its own pursuit-wide meaning.

A **Minimum grade points per course** reduction changes the eligibility floor within this one group. For example, an approved 2.0 → 1.7 reduction uses the selected degree publication's retained grading maximum and changes no recorded grade, GPA calculation, grade definition or other group's floor. A later change to today's grading scale does not alter that display; earlier degrees without a retained scale use their group's recorded authoring maximum. It creates no earned credit. A blank or zero published floor has no reducible minimum and is not offered as a target.

The review and current-exception screens show group minimums separately from degree minimums, using retained group names. Expand a group to inspect its current values. Review expands the affected group and shows its post-decision values before confirmation. Replacement, revocation, history and exact retries use the existing protected approval stream. A later selected degree publication does not inherit the earlier reduction, even when it uses the same group.

The existing event/projection shape is unchanged. New minimum names are validated explicitly. In the recorded `original_milli`/`required_milli` quantities, whole course counts use multiples of 1,000 (three courses = 3,000); effective `min_courses` remains the ordinary integer 3. Grade-point fields remain their existing integer thousandths. Earlier recorded values keep their original units and meaning; no event is rewritten or inferred, and no migration is needed. Approval authority and actor identity are rechecked after replay and before returning a committed result, including accepted retries.

## Preview requirements with approved exceptions

Open **Degree requirements → Preview with approvals**, or **Student exceptions → Preview requirements with approvals**. The full-width view defaults to **With approved exceptions**. Switch to **Published baseline** to compare the same selected publication; switching views makes no request and changes no student record. **Refresh preview** rereads the current selection and approvals together.

The adjusted view shows approved degree/group minima, marks course waivers wherever the course appears, and displays each valid substitution beside its original requirement using the replacement's retained publication label and catalog credits. It keeps required cards and course quantities visible. Waivers and substitutions create no earned work; course-count/grade-point reductions apply only to their explicit group targets. Grading context comes from the selected degree publication when recorded.

Old version-bound reductions remain retained without being applied to a newer selected degree. Pursuit-wide waivers continue where their course is required. Conflicting substitutions are omitted from the adjusted display and flagged for registrar review; the original requirement stays visible. A changed matriculation cohort produces a review notice without moving the selected requirements. Approval counters describe retained applicable approvals, not completed courses or a graduation decision.

Students can inspect their own preview; authorized student readers can inspect it without approval or catalog-editing authority. The preview has no approval controls. The private GET endpoint at `degree-adjustments/preview/` uses the existing owner/assignment checks, student lock and verified histories/projections to return the baseline and approvals from the same read. It exposes no raw events, protected reasons or actors. Missing or inconsistent history/projections block the read rather than trigger repair.

This is a live, read-only preview. It is not a retained audit result, issued document or calculation of completion/GPA. No event, academic credit, financial posting, requirement selection or approval is created by opening, refreshing or switching it. Stored publications and approvals remain unchanged; no migration is needed.

## Course substitutions

Choose **Course substitution**, select the original required course, then search for a replacement and explicitly choose one of its published versions. This is allowed before the student takes either course. Current catalog code/title are navigation aids only; approval and history retain the exact replacement publication and its title/credits. An unpublished catalog entry cannot be approved. The finder returns at most 20 catalog entries per page and ten publications per version page, with literal wildcard escaping.

The substitution applies wherever the original is required within this one pursuit, bound to the degree publication reviewed. Another pursuit or another selected degree publication needs its own approval. It creates no completed attempt, earned credits, GPA points or charges. Actual earned work and the retained grading policy remain evaluator inputs.

In the selected student's placement review, a substitute can also contribute to a saved-rule card when its own retained course facts satisfy that rule. One combined placement counts its credits and course identity once in the group. The original target does not inherit the replacement's rule match, and one actual course cannot fill two different named targets. Revocation removes the alternative from subsequent verified reviews; it changes no course award or earlier approval. [Placement details and limits](degree-audit-builder.md#place-matching-awards-in-course-groups) explain the shared calculation.

Approvals reject self-replacements, chains/cycles and active waivers overlapping either endpoint. If selecting an older degree publication later exposes a conflict, the read remains usable: the screen identifies the conflict, omits ambiguous substitutions from the effective map and allows reasoned revocation. It never invents an allocation or silently chooses an approval. Revocation retains the original decision. Authorized student readers can inspect substitutions; office authority is required for replacement searches and approval/revocation.

## Accept a course publication for one student

Use **Student exceptions → Approve an exception → Accept course publication** when this student's retained work uses another publication of a curriculum course. Select the required course, choose **Accept publication version …**, explain the registrar's reason, and review the exact retained publication before **Approve reviewed exception**. Curriculum-wide setup belongs in the [course-group accepted-publication list](degree-audit-builder.md#accept-other-publications-of-the-same-course).

This exception is specific to one student-program pursuit and the selected degree publication. It adds one explicit allowed course publication; it neither substitutes another course nor changes the student's accepted work, title, credits, grade, GPA or charges. Different publication approvals for the same course coexist independently. A publication already accepted by every applicable curriculum group is not a new exception, and an already active identical approval is rejected. Foreign publications and courses outside the selected curriculum cannot be approved.

Review and revocation confirmations expire after 20 minutes and bind the signed-in reviewer, selected requirements and expected approval-stream version. They require both `sis:degree-audit:adjust` and `sis:student:read`, plus the student's pursuit scope. Changed selections, concurrent approvals, an inactive pursuit, a changed cohort or lost authority reject a new confirmation. A completed exact retry returns its original receipt even after expiry or later revocation, without restoring an obsolete allowance.

**Review revocation** removes only that publication allowance. Other approved publications and curriculum allowances remain. The course's original acceptance, academic events and earlier approval/revocation facts remain recorded. Current placement rereads applicable allowances and can return needs review again when a used version is no longer accepted; it does not rewrite history or retroactively change an issued document.

The existing `RequirementAdjustmentWasGranted`/`WasRevoked` stream and active projection retain these decisions, with current approval kind `course_version`, exact course/publication identities and a publication-specific key. No new event table, numbered model, schema migration or data conversion is needed. Earlier waiver/substitution/minimum/placement facts and receipts keep their contracts. The generic 500-active-approval bound still applies. Curated labels use verified publications; request-local metadata is shared with the overlay and never persists as academic truth. Reads remain private, bounded and owner/office checked, while browser mutations retain CSRF protection.

## Course placement preferences

Open **Student exceptions → Approve an exception → Course placement**. Choose a course from the selected curriculum or an **Approved substitute version … for …** card, explicitly choose one of its retained groups, explain the registrar's decision, and review before confirming. No group is selected automatically. **Change review** preserves the course, group and reason. An authorized advisor can request the change; approving it requires the same independent `sis:degree-audit:adjust` and `sis:student:read` permissions as other student exceptions.

This is a restriction on eligible group choices: while active, actual work for that course identity is considered **only in the approved group** for this pursuit and degree publication. It does not bypass a passing grade, per-course grade-point floor, course publication reference, substitution or repeat policy. It does not choose a particular academic attempt. Retakes remain subject to the existing group-specific eligibility and repeat-credit calculation. Other courses retain their ordinary sharing choices, including when the degree permits multi-group sharing.

The approval does not apply coursework automatically or guarantee that a complete group plan exists. Reserving a shared course for one group can leave another group unmet. Course matching and placement show the approved restriction; the exact combined search still decides whether the supplied eligible work fits. Earned credit, cumulative/overall GPA, resident-credit totals, grades, transcript facts and charges remain unchanged.

The server derives course/group publication references from the selected immutable curriculum. Approved substitute cards additionally identify their existing substitution approval, retained replacement version and original required course. Staff can select only groups containing that original requirement; unrelated catalog courses and groups cannot become placement choices this way. The same substitute approved for different required courses has separate labeled source cards. Waiver and substitution target lists continue to contain only curriculum courses.

A substitute preference reserves the actual course identity, including eligible retakes and saved-rule matches, for the chosen group. Its existing grade, exact replacement publication, repeat-credit and sharing gates still apply. The preference alone creates no eligibility. If the source substitution is revoked or replaced, the independent group restriction remains until separately changed or revoked. The next review uses only currently valid course eligibility; an old confirmation cannot restore the substitution. The preference's original course labels and source-approval reference remain interpretable after that source is revoked.

Clients submit course/group identities and, for a substitute, the existing approval identity. They cannot supply course/group publication references or the required target. Confirmation verifies the unchanged approval-stream version; replay checks that the exact same-pursuit, same-degree substitution was active when the preference was accepted. Choosing individual attempts, arbitrary saved-rule-only courses without a curriculum/substitution target, routing work between degree and selected specialization components uses the reviewed workflow below. Moving work between separate student-program pursuits remains outside this workflow.

A preference is tied to the reviewed degree publication and one student-program assignment. Another degree or dual-degree pursuit does not inherit it. A later degree publication leaves the earlier approval recorded but inapplicable to that new selection. Changing the chosen group supersedes the earlier active preference for the same course/publication; an unchanged preference is rejected. **Review revocation** restores ordinary eligible choices without editing academic records or discarding the original approval.

New preference grant and revocation reviews expire after 20 minutes. A changed requirement selection, competing approval, different actor or current permission loss rejects a new confirmation. An already completed exact retry returns its original receipt even after expiry or later revocation, and never restores or replaces newer preferences. Existing waiver/substitution/minimum events and saved receipts keep their original contracts.

Preferences use the existing grant/revocation stream and active projection. The current approval grammar retains explicit course and group publication references under its schema-2 contract, plus paired `substitution_adjustment_id` and `required_course_id` references for substitute placements; no new event type, event table, schema migration or role grant is introduced. Its unique key permits one active preference per course and degree publication within an assignment. History resolves labels from the retained publication, rather than today's library. Failed audit, stale review or rejected confirmation rolls back event, receipt and active projection together.

Connected integration checks leave academic and financial rows unchanged. Browser checks exercise explicit group cards, retained labels, preserved review fields, private CSRF transport, literal text escaping, mobile reflow and automated accessibility scans. Additional integration and browser checks cover substitute-only choices, rejected or revoked sources, stale confirmation, historical dependency verification, saved-rule restrictions, independent source revocation and unchanged academic/financial facts. No complete degree/graduation decision is claimed by this stage.

## History, concurrency and privacy

`RequirementAdjustmentWasGranted` and `RequirementAdjustmentWasRevoked` schema 1 use the existing authoritative `sis_history_events` table. The stream is keyed by the program-assignment ULID, with stable student/program ownership. Grants retain the selected requirement decision/template/version, the typed course/minimum target and predecessor approval reference. Revocations reference the approval and have their own operation identity. Substitution grants explicitly register approval schema 2, with exact replacement publication references; schema-1 grants and all existing receipts remain unchanged. Revocations keep their original schema-1 contract. Trusted actor, reason and effective/recorded context remain protected by the existing authenticated event envelopes.

`sis_requirement_adjustments` contains only active query projections, with a unique assignment/target key. Replacement and revocation remove obsolete projection rows, while their authoritative events remain. It is not a parallel history table. Replayed active facts are compared to the projection with strict value types and without treating associative JSON key order as a fact. Changed facts, deleted rows, invalid keys and failed event verification fail closed. No automatic live rebuild or fabricated baseline history is introduced.

Commands hold the existing student lock, verify current owner/office authority, replay bounded event pages and bind review to the selected degree decision and expected adjustment-stream version. A changed requirement decision, cohort/status change or competing approval invalidates an unaccepted review. An accepted exact retry returns its original receipt without reverting later approvals. Journal/head/receipt, active projection and audit commit or roll back together, including on late audit failure.

Replay retains at most 500 active approvals per pursuit; history/approval-label reads use pages of ten. Old revoked/superseded payloads are not accumulated in aggregate state. Encrypted review tokens are capped at 32 KiB. Browser routes under `/sis/students/{student}/programs/{assignment}/degree-adjustments/` retain CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression. Entered reasons and review tokens stay out of browser storage, logs and queues. Curated reads expose no raw event envelope.

## Specialization exceptions and course routing

Specializations now use the same exception editor, approval/revocation service, aggregate, journal stream and projection as the degree. No parallel exception system or event table is created.

For a selected specialization, open **Student programs → Specialization choices → Requirements for … → Specialization exceptions**. Approve minimum reductions (general credits/GPA or group quantities/grade floors), substitutions, additional accepted course publications and preferred group placements in the existing visual review. These approvals apply to the exact assigned specialization publication and assignment decision. Reassigning requirements, removing and re-adding a choice, or changing the cohort never transfers an earlier approval automatically. An independent comparison of earlier retained requirements keeps the approvals accepted for that exact earlier assignment, with a cohort-change notice. Combined progress requires the corrected cohort to be resolved. Pending confirmations reject changed assignments; an already accepted confirmation returns its original receipt without restoring earlier projections.

**Course waivers retain their pursuit-wide meaning**, including when entered from a specialization. Waiving a required course removes that named obligation throughout the same degree/program pursuit. Removing the specialization does not revoke a pursuit-wide waiver. Required quantities, earned credit, GPA and charges are unchanged. A second program pursuit needs its own waiver.

Open **Degree requirements → Student exceptions → Course routing** to direct an actual course identity to the degree or one assigned specialization. Choose **Route a course**, select the receiving component, browse/search the catalog and select a published course version for its retained label, then enter a reason and review/approve. This also supports courses that will be taken later or matched by saved subject/level rules. It is a restriction, not a substitution or course-publication acceptance: the selected component must still accept the actual work, grade, course publication and repeat evidence. A route cannot make unrelated or ineligible work satisfy a requirement.

The course is excluded from the other components even when the program permits sharing. All publications and retakes of that stable course identity follow the restriction; the selected publication identifies the course reviewed, not a fabricated student attempt. Existing within-component placement preferences still apply separately. Overall earned credit and GPA retain their original calculation and are never reduced by this structural exclusion.

Routing retains the exact reviewed degree decision, specialization choice/binding decisions and program/cohort reference as a context fingerprint. A curriculum assignment change makes the old route need review. Combined progress stops with a registrar message until an authorized reviewer replaces or revokes it; removal/re-addition cannot silently reactivate an earlier route. Independent degree/specialization comparisons remain clearly labeled; use combined progress for cross-component restrictions.

Current and obsolete specialization approvals remain visible from **Student exceptions** on the pursuit, with labels from their original publications. Authorized staff can revoke obsolete approvals there after the choice has been removed. **Exception history** is the shared pursuit history, not a second component ledger. Protected reasons and actors remain outside the curated history response. Modern specialization and routing reviews expire after twenty minutes and require the original actor, unchanged expected stream version and current authority. Accepted retries remain safe after later changes or pursuit closure.

Staff approval requires both `sis:degree-audit:adjust` and `sis:student:read`. Selection/assignment or catalog authority alone does not approve exceptions. Browser changes retain CSRF protection, private/no-store responses and suppressed debug collectors. The portal exposes only curated runtime progress to the signed-in student; it provides no exception approval controls or raw approval identities/reasons.

Specialization endpoints use the same controller and registered action map as degree adjustments:

`/sis/students/{student}/programs/{assignment}/specializations/{specialization}/adjustments/`

The existing `review/`, `commit/`, `revoke-review/`, `preview/`, course search/publication chooser and pursuit-wide `history/` actions are reused. The degree adjustment prefix additionally provides `GET routing/`. Component identities and retained references are resolved by the server; clients cannot choose a historical assignment or supply a concurrency fingerprint.

No migration is needed for this completion: new decisions use the existing protected approval format, journal and JSON projection. Earlier recorded approvals retain their original scope and interpretation. This feature calculates current progress; retained final audits, credential awards and other degree-audit milestones remain separately tracked.
