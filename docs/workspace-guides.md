# Guides for captured reviews

The separate [etSIS system glossary](glossary.md) explains terms such as pursuit, publication, review, journal and projection.

Implemented 2026-11-20. Open **Admissions guide**, **Registration guide** or **Meeting plan guide** inside the relevant review. These compact, collapsible guides explain the next steps using the information already on screen. They complement the existing degree-progress guide.

## Admissions

Open an application in **Admissions**. The guide follows its recorded status:

- Submitted: review responses, saved checklist readiness and supporting documents before deciding. A received document still needs the applicable verification.
- Accepted: review the admission offer and applicant response under institutional policy, check program and entry term, then review matriculation. Admissions acceptance, applicant acceptance and matriculation are separate steps.
- Rejected or withdrawn: use the existing authorized correction or return-to-review workflow. Application withdrawal does not withdraw an existing student from the institution.
- Matriculated: continue from the student record while keeping the original application and intake history. The person remains the parent identity.

**Go to checklist**, **Go to documents** and **Go to admission offer** move focus to the existing controls. They do not open a new form, load a panel or discard unfinished casework. Open the focused control normally when ready. The guide never infers that an offer was accepted or that a checklist is complete from the application status alone.

## Registration

Sections now have an explicit [waitlisting setting and queue workflow](registration-history.md#per-section-waitlists). **Waitlist policy** starts disabled for each section; **Section waitlist** provides staff queue review, while students use **Waitlist options** and **Your waitlist requests**. Joining does not enroll or charge. When a seat is available, the oldest request uses the same registration guide, eligibility review and fee confirmation described below.

Registrars can separately [reinstate an unchanged tracked late withdrawal](registration-history.md#reinstate-a-tracked-late-withdrawal) through **Students → Registration history → Reinstate this withdrawal**. The reasoned review reuses the shared readiness/timetable explanations and shows the original attempt/credit effect. Existing account postings stay unchanged. It requires the independent reinstatement and closed-record duties in addition to registration management; broader unknown/removed/amended-source reopening remains separate.

The guide appears in staff single/group registration and shared staff/student schedule reviews. A multi-section schedule gets one guide for the complete selection.

It distinguishes existing active registrations from closed attempts, directs staff to outstanding registration checks, explains missing prerequisites and companion courses, separates blocking timetable conflicts from permitted warnings, and calls out unavailable schedules or pricing. An unpublished meeting plan is not evidence of a clear timetable. Proposed registration charges are separate from the account balance, aid and a payment request.

**Show registration checks**, **Show timetable details** and **Show proposed charges** move focus to the existing details, opening enclosing disclosures where needed. In a group or multi-section review, the links prefer the first displayed check/timetable/charge that needs attention. No request is sent by these links. The existing confirmation controls and server policies continue to determine whether a selection can be accepted; the guide does not calculate eligibility, reserve seats or create charges.

## Meeting-plan publication

Open **Academic catalog → Sections → Meeting plan**. The guide distinguishes pending booking decisions, unchecked faculty coverage, blocking or permitted faculty overlaps, meeting times/locations, deliberately empty plans and an approved schedule that already matches its publication.

The reviewed confirmation has a fresh guide for that captured review. Its faculty links focus the existing assessment and its meeting link focuses the displayed schedule. The publication reason remains untouched. Working booking changes take effect in the academic timetable after the revised meeting plan is published. The guide does not approve bookings, acknowledge faculty warnings or publish a schedule.

## Check meeting-plan history

From **Sections → Meeting plan**, use **Check meeting-plan history** to open the existing support drawer. The action also appears on publication-history pages and when damaged evidence prevents the ordinary plan editor from loading. It requires scheduling, catalog, history-verification and audit duties together. An access denial does not expose the action or restore an earlier draft.

Choose **Run history check** to compare the retained plan, current pointer and original offering references using a fixed 200-event allowance. The result shows safe counts and component names, followed by a restricted support reference. An explicitly published plan with no meetings is different from no recorded plan. This check publishes or repairs nothing and does not certify current booking/faculty/student conflicts.

**Back to record** rechecks access, restores the unfinished publication reason or the selected history page, and returns focus to the action. Existing publication reviews and confirmations remain separate. See [staff meeting-plan support checks](event-history-foundation.md#staff-meeting-plan-support-checks) for permission, privacy, receipt and limit details.

## Refresh, privacy and accessibility

All three workflows use one shared presentation component and fixed guidance text. The guide reads captured response flags in page memory; it does not fetch other records, call an AI provider, write browser storage, append events or change the database. Personal names, staff notes, tokens and reasons are not copied into the guidance model. Opening or following a guide adds no permissions and bypasses no review or institutional policy.

Refreshing a review replaces its guide with the new captured result and closes the disclosure. Old detached guide controls cannot act on the replacement screen. Unknown or incomplete supported input produces a refresh instruction rather than a positive readiness claim. Changes elsewhere require the normal refresh/review workflow; the guide is not a live monitor.

Native disclosure controls work with keyboard and assistive technology. Links move visible focus to the existing control or detail without submitting forms. The shared styles preserve narrow-screen reflow, contrast and focus, with no movement animation. The guides are available without configuring optional AI assistance. External student-progress AI sharing remains deferred.


## Major and minor curriculum setup

**Program details & publications → Majors, minors & specializations** now offers explicit component types. Configure optional type limits beside the overall selection policy, publish the program, then use the existing student component-selection and requirements workflow. Combined staff/owner progress displays retained major/minor roles and applies the same sharing rules. See [major/minor setup and historical limits](program-publication.md#majors-minors-and-curriculum-component-types).

For a broad program with one required major and an optional minor, the institution must permit at least two components and the program maximum must be two. Require exactly one major and allow zero or one minor; the overall required count is one. Follow the [worked broad-program example](program-publication.md#example-a-broad-ba-program-with-one-major-and-an-optional-minor) for the exact controls, requirements assignments, degree progress and current application/portal boundaries.


## Department and curriculum responsibility

Set each department's optional school/college in its reference editor. Set each major/minor's responsible department in its component editor, then review and publish the program. A broad program may keep its own department blank. Student choices and combined progress display the retained organization, with earlier missing ownership explicitly unknown. Follow [Departments and curriculum ownership](program-publication.md#departments-and-curriculum-ownership) for the connected setup and historical boundaries.


### Required major for a concentration, track or specialization

In **Programs → Program details & publications → Majors, minors & specializations**, first create the major, then edit its related concentration/track/specialization and choose **Choose required major**. Only this program's majors appear. Returning preserves your unsaved component fields and responsible department. Save, configure overall/type counts for both components, and explicitly review/publish the program. Use **Clear required major** to make a component independent. In **Student programs → Specializations**, select the required major together with its linked component; an unresolved link blocks review while preserving the checked choices. See the [worked linked-curriculum setup](program-publication.md#required-major-relationships), including an optional minor and retained student progress labels.
