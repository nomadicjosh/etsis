# Student course-group placement review

Open **Students → the student → Student programs → Degree requirements → Review course placement**. The same action is available from **Academic work**. The review loads on demand and uses the pursuit's selected degree publication, exact group/course publications, current verified academic work and approved exceptions.

This reviews **course-group placement**. It does not evaluate overall degree credit/GPA/residency minimums, specialization rules, standing, graduation eligibility or issued transcripts. It saves no academic changes or retained audit result.

## Using the review

The result distinguishes a complete supported placement, no complete placement for the supplied current work, missing/conflicting evidence, an unsupported retained rule and a search or read limit. None of these changes the student's academic status. An unsuccessful or interrupted search returns no speculative partial placement.

Each course-group card shows its effective credit or course-count requirement. Choose **Inspect group …** to see its retained course requirements and proposed placements. Expand a requirement card to see the exact published course list and approved waivers. Saved-rule cards show their retained conditions and derived eligible course targets from this captured review; those temporary lists do not change the publication. Course lists are created only when expanded. Requirement cards and placements have separate 20-item pages; the review does not build thousands of controls on opening.

A proposed placement shows the actual course, the required-course target, any approved substitution, actual eligible earned credits and supporting award alternatives. Equal-credit evidence remains explicit: the review does not choose an institutional-origin or GPA winner merely to display a placement. Multiple eligible awards can support one distinct actual course when the retained repeat-credit policy allows them. **Requirement contributions** expands the saved-rule placement into its per-card credits. A seven-credit pool can support three credits on one card and four on another without supplying seven credits to either card.

**How current work was considered** explains eligible work, insufficient grades, in-progress work, drops, voids, unknown publications and repeat/sharing effects. Work and placement pages use the same captured result without another request. **Refresh course placement** performs a fresh bounded review; **Back to previous screen** returns to the degree or work screen.

Named courses and courses matched by retained saved rules can use the same reviewed group preference. The [rule-course picker](student-degree-exceptions.md#prefer-a-group-for-a-course-found-by-saved-rules) lets staff choose a matching group without adding a named curriculum course. Approved substitute courses can also have their own [reviewed group preference](student-degree-exceptions.md#course-placement-preferences), selected through the existing substitution approval. The restriction follows the actual course identity across named and saved-rule matches. It does not approve a substitute or restore a revoked approval; passing-grade, publication and repeat gates remain independent. Revocation of the substitution preserves the separate placement decision and its retained labels.

## Supporting coursework

Open **Inspect group … → a proposed placement → Supporting coursework** to see the accepted academic records behind that placement. The table shows actual course labels, the retained local posting term when known, institutional/transfer origin, recorded grade code and per-course grade points, recorded earned credits and the actual completion date when known. Missing grade evidence is **unavailable**, while a verified ungraded institutional attempt is **awaiting a final grade**. A grade with zero recorded points remains a known zero. No current grading default, course title, term label or completion date supplies missing historical evidence.

A known grade without a retained grading publication is labeled explicitly. Earlier-history gaps keep their existing notice; displaying a current accepted grade does not invent an earlier publication or prove archive completeness. Recorded grade points are not an average GPA or an implicit conversion between scales.

Equal-credit repeat alternatives are shown one at a time with **Previous/Next evidence alternative**. The viewer preserves each alternative's own grade, posting term and source; it does not choose an institutional-origin, resident-credit or overall-GPA winner. When several accepted awards contribute together, **More supporting records** pages their twenty-row table. Those rows remain one actual-course placement. Recorded award credits are not necessarily applied to every requirement card and must not be summed as another overall earned-credit total.

Opening, paging and returning reuse the captured review; they trigger no request, replay, recalculation or academic mutation. Wide tables have a labeled keyboard-focusable scroll area, and paging moves focus to the new range. Course/grade labels are escaped. Raw events, grade/scale identities, actors and protected reasons do not enter the evidence view. Refreshing after a correction obtains new accepted facts without changing the previously captured response. This remains a transient explanation rather than a retained audit result or graduation decision.

## Applied requirement checklist

For a complete supported plan, group cards show **applied credits and distinct courses**, using actual eligible awards. **Inspect group …** shows **Group quantity met in this proposed plan** and a concise checklist on each requirement-card summary. All/choose cards show covered versus required courses; credit cards show the actual credits applied against their own minimum. A pool has no separate card minimum. Open a card to inspect its retained course choices and the applied contribution.

Group quantities and card contributions are different scopes. A three-credit course satisfying two overlapping cards contributes three credits to each eligible card and three credits once to the group. An all-eligible repeat pool may contribute seven group credits while only three satisfy one card and four satisfy another. Several supporting awards still represent one distinct actual course. Shared groups may each use a course under the retained policy; these displays never sum those uses into additional overall earned credit or GPA.

An all-card waiver reduces its required-course obligations without creating applied credit or a completion. Actual eligible work for a waived course can still contribute to group quantity and other cards. Choose and credit minimums remain unchanged unless independently reduced through their supported controls. The checklist distinguishes waivers from actual applied work.

No applied checklist or zero totals are returned for unknown, unsupported, unsuccessful or interrupted plans. Those outcomes retain their existing evidence-bound explanations without a speculative partial placement. The checklist only describes the complete plan already found; it performs no new search, database read, replay or academic write, and does not establish graduation eligibility. It is shared by staff degree progress, temporary draft/published audit tests and the owner's portal progress. Requirement cards retain their existing paging and lazy course lists.

## Understanding shortfalls

Group cards now identify clear shortages in current eligible work. Choose **Inspect group … → What current work can support** for details:

- The credit bound shows the most eligible credit that could count toward the effective minimum, capped at that minimum. A 12-credit group with at most 9 eligible credits needs at least 3 more. The bound uses actual retained awards after group grade floors and repeat-credit treatment, never today's catalog credit values.
- The course-count bound uses distinct actual courses and distinct required targets. Repeated awards for one course, or an original course and its alternative for one target, cannot inflate that bound.
- Required-course and choose cards show how many contributions are required, which targets have evidence and the tighter capacity from distinct eligible actual courses. Expand a card to see which retained courses have eligible completed evidence. A missing course on a choose card is an option, not an instruction to complete every listed course.

A waiver removes an **all** card's named obligation wherever applicable. It supplies no completed course or credits and does not reduce a choose count. Real earned work for a waived course can still support an unchanged quantity minimum. Approved minimum reductions are already included in the comparison.

These are optimistic bounds, not applied credits, a best partial plan or a complete placement. Alternatives can compete for one required target; one actual course can compete for several targets or groups. No clear shortage therefore does **not** establish that a group is satisfied. The existing combined placement search remains responsible for those decisions. A shared-course conflict can produce no complete plan even when each group has no simple local shortage.

Relevant unknown grades/publications, conflicting approvals and unsupported retained rules suppress these shortage comparisons. The screen asks for review rather than treating unknown evidence as zero. A search limit still shows no partial placements; independently established evidence bounds may remain visible, without changing the interrupted search into academic failure. A collection/preparation/replay limit returns no partial result.

The same explanations are available through **Degree progress → Inspect course-group placement** from the captured review, without an additional request. Evidence bounds use only the planner's already prepared, bounded inputs. They add no database queries, stream replay, additional search, stored evaluation or academic write, and expose no raw events or source references.

## Shared-course shortages

When a verified search finds no complete plan, **Groups competing for completed courses** explains supported shortages across overlapping groups. The same explanation appears in **Degree progress**, and opening the detailed placement view reuses that captured result.

For example, two groups may each require six credits and each accept the same two completed three-credit courses. Each group can appear sufficient in isolation, but the retained one-group policy permits those courses to contribute only six credits across both groups. The combined twelve-credit requirement therefore has a shortage of at least six credits. The review names the affected groups without inventing a partial assignment.

Explanations can show a credit shortage, a distinct-course contribution shortage, or both. Course-count bounds retain zero-credit completed courses. Required-course waivers remove their all-rule obligations but create no earned credit and do not lower group quantities or choose counts. Overlapping rules inside one group are not added as though they require separate copies of the same course. A course with different eligible repeat-award amounts is counted once at the most optimistic eligible amount, so a smaller alternative cannot create a false shortage.

These are **lower bounds on a shared shortage**, not an exact plan for which courses a student must take. They do not change retained requirements, approvals, academic records, earned-credit/GPA totals or sharing policy. Groups with a simple individual shortage keep their existing local explanation. A multi-group sharing policy produces no one-group competition claim within an independently reviewed component.

The analyzer checks overlapping pairs and complete connected groups of competing evidence. It does not enumerate every subset or explain every possible target/rule conflict. It returns at most ten nonredundant explanations and indicates when more were found. No explanation does not mean that the groups can be satisfied; the exact search remains authoritative. Relevant unknown inputs, unsupported rules and interrupted/limited searches produce no cross-group shortage claim.

This adds bounded comparisons over the already prepared inputs, with no database query, stream replay, additional placement search, stored evaluation or write. Existing student ownership/office checks, CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression remain in force. Fictional tests compare reported shortages with exact searches across small deterministic cases, and cover published student selections, unchanged events/receipts/finance, sharing, escaped labels, mobile reflow and automated accessibility checks.

### Degree and specialization shortages

The combined curriculum review uses this same analyzer and displays **Shared-course shortages** when eligible work cannot meet the joint requirements. It honors both the published program policy for sharing between components and each component's own policy for sharing between groups. A course may supply several requirement contributions when those policies permit it; these are uses toward requirements, never additional earned credits or GPA points.

For each course, the analyzer takes the largest eligible contribution within a component that allows one group, or adds eligible contributions when that component permits several groups. It then takes the largest component contribution when only one component is permitted, or adds component contributions when sharing is permitted. Amounts are capped at the requirements being checked. These optimistic bounds can prove a shortage but cannot establish feasibility, choose a replacement course, or diagnose every target/rule conflict.

The review reuses the same overlapping-pair/connected-component checks and ten-explanation cap. A shortage may involve groups inside one component, groups across components, or several components whose pairs have enough work individually. Groups with known individual shortages keep their local explanations. Feasible, unresolved and limited joint reviews make no shared-shortage claim.

**Inspect Degree · …** or **Inspect Specialization … · …** opens the affected captured group; returning restores the combined review. Staff and student screens share this presentation. The portal receives only numeric bounds and response-local component/group positions, with labels supplied by its existing curated component view. Private identities, raw search aliases, protected approvals and rule grammar are not exposed. Neither explanation nor drilldown repeats history reads or placement search, stores an evaluation, or changes academic/financial facts.

## Rules applied

- Group eligibility uses retained passing-grade facts, explicitly accepted course publications or approved substitution publications, and the group's effective per-course grade-point floor. Grade-point comparisons require compatible retained grading publications; no conversion or current 4.0 display default supplies missing evidence.
- Eligibility is checked **before** the retained repeat-credit policy is applied for each actual-course/group/target combination. A larger award that fails the group's grade floor cannot hide a smaller qualified award. These group choices do not remove attempts from overall GPA or overall earned-credit calculations.
- Named all/choose/credit/pool cards, supported saved-rule cards and group credit/course quantities enter one exact bounded placement search. One-group sharing prevents placing the same actual course in several groups. Multiple-group sharing permits a placement in each eligible group without creating another earned award or another GPA contribution.
- A [reviewed course placement preference](student-degree-exceptions.md#course-placement-preferences) restricts actual work for that curriculum course to its approved group within the selected degree publication. It does not override eligibility, choose a retake or apply work automatically; the combined search can still find no complete plan. Other courses keep their retained sharing policy.
- A pursuit-wide waiver removes a required course wherever required. It creates no earned credit and does not reduce group quantities or choose counts. Actual earned work for that waived course can still meet unchanged quantity requirements when eligible.
- Applicable approved group credit/course minimum and per-course grade-floor reductions affect the search. An approved substitution supplies an alternative for the original target; the original and alternative cannot inflate that target into two distinct completions. Conflicting approvals require registrar review.

The separate [combined degree-progress review](student-degree-progress.md) now compares approved general credit/GPA minimums alongside this placement result from the same verified read. This standalone placement screen does not silently treat those minimums as satisfied. Earlier average group-GPA requirements are explicitly unsupported by this structural review. A group cannot be declared satisfied after dropping an unsupported constraint.

Saved-rule cards use verified accepted course publications and captured predicate decisions, then apply group grade floors before repeat-credit selection. Attempts with different memberships remain distinct highest-eligible placement alternatives, or contribute their own card-specific amounts within an all-eligible pool. They never lend a rule match to another attempt. An active approved substitute can cover its named target and matching saved-rule cards in one placement. Each card receives only its own matching awards; credits and course identity enter the group total once. A publication outside the curriculum and applicable student allowance sets, or a conflicting approval, still requires review. [Saved-rule placement](degree-audit-builder.md#place-matching-awards-in-course-groups) explains supported combinations and remaining limits. Too few known eligible matches produces a current-work shortage; missing potentially relevant evidence does not become zero credit.

## Named-course review

Open **Inspect group … → Named-course review** to compare each named course in the retained group with matching current attempts. The same inspection is available in temporary degree-audit tests and the student's **Course-group details**. It uses the captured review; expanding details and paging make no additional requests.

Each row distinguishes a **required course** from a **course option**. A named course in an “All of these” card is required; appearing in a choose/credit/pool list does not make every option mandatory. Approved waivers are identified independently and create neither credits nor quantity reductions. A row with no matching current work says exactly that; it does not prove the student never took the course or that the historical archive is complete.

Expand **Matching attempts** to inspect the actual retained course/term labels, recorded grade, earned credits and eligibility reason for each attempt. Reasons include awaiting a grade, not passing, insufficient per-course points, missing/different publications, dropped/voided work and approved placement restrictions. An approved alternative retains its actual course label beside the original requirement. Earlier attempts remain visible alongside later attempts; this inspection never picks a favorable repeat or replaces its recorded grade.

**Evidence in proposed placement** appears only when the combined search found a complete plan. All tied supporting attempts remain alternatives. Eligible but unused work is distinct from insufficient evidence; an incomplete, uncertain or limited search displays **Placement not established**, with no applied checkmarks or partial plan. A waived course can still supply genuine earned work toward unchanged quantities when eligible.

Named lists retain their exact published labels. Saved-rule-only targets are not invented as named obligations; inspect the separate retained rule/checklist and supporting coursework for those requirements. Unsupported retained constraints leave this inspection unavailable rather than presenting an empty list as proof of missing work.

The inspection reuses the complete course-matching checks already prepared for placement, preserving the existing 200-work, 25-group, 200-named-courses-per-group and 5,000-match bounds. The browser builds at most 20 course rows at a time and 10 attempts per expanded row. Student responses contain curated labels/statuses and response-local record positions; they omit internal identities, reasons, actors and raw predicates. Evidence references share the existing 20,000-reference response ceiling. No academic write, extra replay, cache, permission, event format or migration is introduced.

## Unknown facts and historical protection

Relevant unknown grades, missing/different course publications, incompatible grading context and conflicting approvals produce **needs review**, with no definitive placements. A work record unrelated to every selected course group does not block this structural check merely because its grade is unknown; overall GPA/degree evaluation has a separate scope.

Named targets now accept their required publication plus explicitly retained [curriculum allowances](degree-audit-builder.md#accept-other-publications-of-the-same-course) and applicable [student publication approvals](student-degree-exceptions.md#accept-a-course-publication-for-one-student). The review preserves actual and required titles separately. A saved-rule-only placement does not claim it needed a named-course publication exception; predicates still use the accepted award's own facts. No code, date or current catalog match creates equivalence automatically.

Older unbound institutional work and transfer awards without retained accepted-course publication/equivalence evidence are not automatically matched to today's curriculum. New [transfer awards retain a deliberately selected receiving-course publication](transfer-course-publications.md) and can match that exact version. An explicit registrar acceptance review can now resolve a mistaken or unknown course reference within that same institutional course. Other earlier evidence gaps and general cross-publication equivalence still need review support. The reviewer never creates a historical publication binding from today's catalog.

Known in-progress, non-passing, late-drop and voided work cannot supply completed-course placement. Early-drop references are verified internally and omitted from current records, counts and returned evidence. Unknown earlier history and a changed matriculation cohort are identified. The check covers available surviving current/history references; it does not certify a complete archive or reconstruct earlier cumulative results.

## Bounds and access

Every request verifies owner or `sis:student:read` access and the student-program scope, then holds the student lock while reading the selection, retained requirements, group/course publications, approvals and academic inputs. It rechecks authority before returning. Private POST requests retain framework CSRF protection, private/no-store responses and debug suppression.

The same 400-page/2,000-event replay allowance covers those reads, including replacement-course publications. Academic discovery remains capped at 200 distinct sources. Saved-rule inspection allows at most 5,000 rule/work comparisons, with at most 200 derived/named course targets in each group. Placement preparation separately allows at most 5,000 relevant work/target eligibility checks and 20,000 selected evidence references and 20,000 explicit card contributions; it never feeds the truncated 25-match display pages to the planner. The existing planner bounds groups/resources/choices and defaults to 100,000 preparation/search steps. Exhaustion yields a controlled incomplete result; the API accepts no larger budget or alternate policy.

Only 20 current work records, requirement cards or placements are displayed per relevant page. Required course lists load into the DOM on expansion. Current snapshots live only in the open review, with no browser storage, persistent result cache, new event or receipt. Failed nested reads abort the read transaction before a controlled failure result is returned.

Private endpoint: `/sis/students/{student}/programs/{assignment}/degree-requirements/academic-work/allocation/`.

This slice requires no migration or stored event/schema conversion. The [structural engine](degree-audit-allocation.md) and [repeat-credit/GPA engine](degree-audit-calculations.md) retain their separate responsibilities. Next are remaining historical transfer/equivalence inputs, complete degree minimum/constraint integration and reproducible retained evaluations with controlled graduation decisions.

When component routing excludes a course from a saved-rule group, the captured rule decisions are still verified before the work is excluded. A known excluded course can therefore produce an explained shortfall rather than a false history-integrity error. Missing, duplicate or foreign captured rule decisions remain integrity failures even for excluded work.
