# Institutional credits and grade history

Institutional credits now have a replayable history from accepted registration through final-grade decisions and reviewed early/late drops. These events use the existing **`sis_history_events`** journal. There is no additional event store. [Transfer awards and corrections](transfer-credit-history.md) now also use the shared journal. [Accepted enrollment/seat decisions](registration-history.md) now have their own replayable history and committed section order. Full section-definition versions and student/program transitions remain staged work.

## Staff workflow

Faculty use **My teaching → Section → Grade & attendance → Record final grade**. Select a retained institutional grade, effective date and optional note, then review and explicitly confirm. A dual-degree student with different captured policies requires an explicit program-pursuit choice. Assignments, attendance and assignment scores remain separate from authoritative final-grade decisions.

Authorized records officers use **Students → Student record → View transcript → Review final grade**. A posted grade requires both `sis:gradebook:amend` and `sis:student:read`, with a mandatory correction reason. The correction permission is a separate protected system definition; broad teaching, registration and student-read permissions do not imply it. Assign it deliberately to the appropriate registrar staff. Initial grades require the assigned faculty or `sis:gradebook:manage`; correction permission alone cannot submit an initial grade. Super administrators retain their universal authority.

The review shows the before/after grade, grade-point units, effective date and reason. New reviews expire after **20 minutes**. **Change review** preserves the selected grade, effective date and reason, including after a rejected confirmation; review again to obtain a fresh confirmation. Confirmation rechecks actor, authority, active enrollment, protected history, current projection, retained policy and expected stream version inside the transaction, and rechecks actor/authority before returning success. A competing decision requires a new review. Exact completed retries return the original receipt, including after expiry or a later correction, without overwriting the newer grade. Earlier accepted tokens without an expiry keep their original event/receipt contract; unaccepted earlier tokens require a fresh review. An expired new confirmation rolls back its grade event, any observation, projection, audit and receipt together. Current correction support covers active institutional enrollments; changing a closed enrollment, awarded course, credits or posting term needs a separate reasoned replacement workflow.

**Grade history** provides bounded pages of curated decisions with effective/recorded timestamps. Student owners, assigned faculty and staff with academic-read authority can read the authorized record. Reasons and actor identifiers are withheld from this ordinary history view; raw protected streams have no browser/reporting endpoint. An early-dropped current record is not resurrected through this view.

### Resolve an unknown course publication

Authorized registrars use **Students → Student record → View transcript → Course publication**. This opens one record on demand rather than replaying a directory. The same review is available from the grade record's **Review course publication** action. It requires both `sis:gradebook:amend` and `sis:student:read`; initial-grade authority is not required. Course choices are paged and contain only publications of that record's same stable course identity.

This workflow is for an active institutional record whose opening history did not record a course publication or a published offering. It does not replace known registration-time definitions. Select the publication supported by institutional evidence, enter the acceptance effective date and a reason, then review the before/after labels and confirm. **Change course publication review** preserves entered values. Confirmation checks the original actor/current permissions, locked enrollment/student/section scope, protected history, exact publication and expected credit/stream versions. Reviews expire after 20 minutes. A competing grade/completion/acceptance decision requires a fresh review.

The append records a **later acceptance**, not proof that this publication applied at registration. Original course labels and the original unknown reference remain in opening history. Subsequent corrections to this acceptance append another decision. Exact retries return the original receipt after later selections or an early drop, without changing the newer reference or restoring removed projections. A new review cannot reopen a dropped/closed record. Grade history includes the acceptance's curated course label and timestamps, withholding protected reasons and actors.

An untracked older row receives an explicit observation at the actual review time, followed by the real acceptance decision. Earlier actors, action times and course references remain unknown. Both appends, immutable course projection, accepted reference, current credit version and audit commit or roll back together. Grades, earned/attempted credits, quality points, completion dates, original offering references, enrollment and charges remain as recorded; future registrations in the section are unaffected. The new reference can resolve a degree-audit course match, but it supplies no missing grading or instructional chronology evidence. Issued transcript artifacts retain their existing immutable snapshots.

The change adds the registered `institutional-course-was-accepted` event to the existing academic-credit stream/journal and reuses `sis_credit_course_versions`. It adds no table, migration, permission grant or automatic backfill. The configured `nomadicjosh_sis` database was inspected read-only before implementation; no institutional credit rows were present, and no live rows were changed. Tests use fictional registered and older untracked records.

## Historical facts and policies

A credit stream is keyed by its academic-credit ULID. Its opening event records the accepted enrollment, student, section and term references, credit units, course code/transcript title, available published course reference and explicit applicable grading-publication references. A later scale publication or program-pursuit closure does not change a known captured policy, even before the credit receives its first grade. Unknown inception policy remains explicitly unknown; the first reviewed grade chooses an actual available policy. Already graded records retain their exact grade publication.

Grade decisions retain the selected definition's code, per-course points, GPA/credit flags and scale/publication references. Replay calculates earned credit and credit-weighted quality points from these retained facts. It does not ask today's catalog or policy to reinterpret an old decision. Effective business dates may be earlier than the recording date, from the enrollment date through today. Effective dates do not imply that the institution knew the decision before it was recorded.

[Actual completion-date decisions](academic-completion-dates.md) now use this same academic-credit stream. Authorized registrars can record, correct or explicitly clear a completion date after a verified posted grade. Each decision preserves the previous date and decision lineage, increments the academic projection version and retains a protected reason. Grade amendments preserve the completion date. A completion date may predate administrative entry; it is validated independently from a grade decision's effective-date range and is never inferred from a posting timestamp or offering end date. Curated history shows the date change without exposing protected reasons or actors.

Existing untracked credits are **not** backfilled by a migration or read. Their first reviewed grade/drop records an explicit observation of existing facts, attributed to the current action, followed by the actual decision in the same transaction. Earlier author/action dates and policy provenance remain unknown. Existing earned units, grade references, quality points and row revision are preserved in that observation. A missing or altered tracked projection fails closed; it is never silently reclassified as a legacy baseline.

Early drop removes current enrollment/credit/course-version projections and reverses the original charge using the existing billing workflow, while retaining restricted academic events. Late drop retains attempted credit, its resolved drop grade/GPA rule and the charge. A retake/new registration receives a different credit identity. Hiatus decisions use the same drop service and ledger. Existing encrypted drop/hiatus receipts remain evidence and are not rewritten into invented earlier events.

[Source academic-period dates](academic-completion-dates.md#source-academic-period-start) now share the completion-date coordinator and editor. For verified graded records without an offering publication, a registrar can record/correct/clear a missing source start through `institutional-academic-period-was-recorded`. Both dates remain independent and cannot contradict one another. Known offering starts, grade/course facts and charges remain protected; the existing row version and stream provide shared concurrency and replay.

## Storage and deployment

Apply `20261109000003_CreateSisAcademicCreditStreams` through the normal migration process before deploying this write path. It creates **`sis_academic_credit_streams`**, a reconstructible credit/enrollment/student/section routing projection, plus the protected correction permission. It does not convert current academic rows, copy event bodies or grant a role additional authority. Routing survives early deletion and deliberately has no foreign key to the deleted current credit/enrollment. The migration is retryable and refuses to overwrite an existing custom permission of the same name. Down migration refuses to discard retained stream routing.

The registered version-1 event contracts include `institutional-credit-was-established`, `final-grade-was-decided`, `institutional-credit-was-dropped`, `academic-completion-was-recorded` and `institutional-course-was-accepted`. Each append uses expected version, operation identity and protected command context. Observation plus grade/drop, projection changes, audits and any related registration billing share the unit of work. Old event contracts and recorded bytes/receipts remain unchanged. All institutional-credit replays, including teaching choices, final-grade reviews/confirmations and curated history verification, now use `HistoryReadBudget`: at most 400 pages and 2,000 events by default. Degree reviews keep their existing shared request budget. Reaching the limit stops the operation without partial results or changes; the private response asks staff to contact the records office for a separate history review. The cap does not truncate events or grant the browser a larger limit. Reads compare typed projected facts with protected history; curated paging freezes its committed head.

Fictional SQLite checks cover root/Codefy replay, byte/receipt preservation, policy pinning, permission/reason/date checks, cross-record/actor tokens, concurrent reviews, exact retries, atomic failures, early-drop retention and rebuilding credit projections without billing/event side effects. Browser checks use fictional data, CSRF, explicit confirmation, field preservation, narrow-screen reflow and automated accessibility scans. Schema compilation covers the five installed framework dialects; live cross-engine locking, isolated rebuild/swap tooling, external integrity anchors and retention operations remain acceptance work. Development tooling has not migrated the working database or run a live projection rebuild.

The grade-review expiry and teaching replay-cap extension needs no migration or stored-event conversion. A bounded read-only check of the configured `nomadicjosh_sis` database found no institutional credit rows. No live rows were changed. Fictional tests verify expiry at the exact boundary, refreshing without lost entries, original earlier-format receipts, post-write authority loss with complete rollback, and successful 2,000-event verification followed by refusal at 2,001 events.
