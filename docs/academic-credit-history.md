# Institutional credits and grade history

Institutional credits now have a replayable history from accepted registration through final-grade decisions and reviewed early/late drops. These events use the existing **`sis_history_events`** journal. There is no additional event store. [Transfer awards and corrections](transfer-credit-history.md) now also use the shared journal. [Accepted enrollment/seat decisions](registration-history.md) now have their own replayable history and committed section order. Full section-definition versions and student/program transitions remain staged work.

## Staff workflow

Opening a section loads its bounded roster with no search required. Empty search values are accepted for roster viewing and CSV export, including the first drawer load and clearing filters; malformed or oversized searches remain rejected. Private authorization and CSRF checks apply to both requests.

Faculty use **My teaching → Section → Grade & attendance → Record final grade**. Select a retained institutional grade, effective date and optional note, then review and explicitly confirm. A dual-degree student with different captured policies requires an explicit program-pursuit choice. Assignments, attendance and assignment scores remain separate from authoritative final-grade decisions.

Authorized records officers use **Students → Student record → View transcript → Review final grade**. A posted grade requires both `sis:gradebook:amend` and `sis:student:read`, with a mandatory correction reason. The correction permission is a separate protected system definition; broad teaching, registration and student-read permissions do not imply it. Assign it deliberately to the appropriate registrar staff. Initial grades require the assigned faculty or `sis:gradebook:manage`; correction permission alone cannot submit an initial grade. Super administrators retain their universal authority.

The review shows the before/after grade, grade-point units, effective date and reason. New reviews expire after **20 minutes**. **Change review** preserves the selected grade, effective date and reason, including after a rejected confirmation; review again to obtain a fresh confirmation. Confirmation rechecks actor, authority, enrollment scope, protected history, current projection, retained policy and expected stream version inside the transaction, and rechecks actor/authority before returning success. A competing decision requires a new review. Exact completed retries return the original receipt, including after expiry or a later correction, without overwriting the newer grade. Earlier accepted tokens without an expiry keep their original event/receipt contract; unaccepted earlier tokens require a fresh review. An expired new confirmation rolls back its grade event, any observation, projection, audit and receipt together. The [optional second-person approval workflow](#second-person-grade-correction-approval) can require a different registrar to approve a posted-grade correction. Corrections cover active institutional enrollments and the narrowly scoped [withdrawn-grade correction](#correct-a-withdrawn-grade) below. Posted institutional credit units now share the explicit correction below. Reopening an enrollment or changing its awarded course or posting term requires a separate reasoned replacement workflow.

**Grade history** provides bounded pages of curated decisions with effective/recorded timestamps. Student owners, assigned faculty and staff with academic-read authority can read the authorized record. Reasons and actor identifiers are withheld from this ordinary history view; raw protected streams have no browser/reporting endpoint. An early-dropped current record is not resurrected through this view.

### Check a selected record's integrity

In **Grade history**, authorized support staff can choose **Check selected academic history** to compare this credit and its registration/source projections with available protected history. This requires `sis:history:verify`, `sis:audit:read` and `sis:student:read`, independently of ordinary history access. The shared drawer runs only on request, records a minimal restricted support receipt and returns to the captured record. Earlier gaps, changes, damaged sources and exhausted limits remain distinct. See [selected academic integrity checks](event-history-foundation.md#selected-institutional-and-transfer-academic-checks) for scope, retained early-drop references and the read-only operator command. This does not correct a grade or certify degree completion.

### Use a coursework suggestion

Faculty can now connect the working gradebook to an **initial** final-grade decision in the same **Record final grade** editor. The **Coursework suggestion** card compares the selected student's included work with the academic record's retained global/program grading ranges. Choose **Use suggested grade**, review the institutional grade, effective date and note, then explicitly confirm. Opening the editor or calculating a suggestion changes no academic or financial records.

The suggestion uses the same total-points, assignment-weight or category-weight rules as coursework feedback, including excusals, dropped scores, regular bonuses and bonus-only work. All included regular work must have scores and every applicable positive-weight category must have graded regular work. Optional ungraded bonus tasks do not count as incomplete required work. Recorded bonus points that cannot yet apply require review first. Excluded work remains excluded according to the selected setup.

Grade boundaries use exact rational arithmetic through the existing open-source `brick/math` library. The default compares the original percentage without rounding or a 100% cap. For example, **92.9994%** may display as **93.00%** in ordinary feedback but misses both a band beginning at **93%** and one ending at **92.999%**. Institutions can explicitly configure [rounding and a 100% cap](#coursework-rounding-and-the-final-grade-cap) in the section's Gradebook setup. The suggestion shows the raw result and the percentage used for grade comparison. Approximate displays are shortened to six decimals and carry an ≈ prefix; comparisons never use that shortened display. Suggestions still require one unambiguous numerical band and never select an administrative grade or fill a gap. Grade points and GPA/earned-credit choices come from the selected retained institutional definition.

**Choose manually** remains available. Changing the institutional grade clears the coursework selection; the server rejects a claim that pairs a suggestion with a different grade. An incomplete book, unconfigured numerical ranges, ambiguous context or a comparison percentage above 100% leaves manual grading available under the existing permissions. **Refresh suggestion** reloads current coursework while preserving the entered grade, date and note; staff must explicitly choose the new suggestion again.

Review and confirmation bind the selected student/section, exact calculated inputs, configuration revision, score versions, published grade/range, academic revision and staff actor. Changed marks, excusals, assignment points, category/drop/weight settings, final-grade comparison policy or selected grading context require a fresh review. Confirmation holds the existing student/section locks used by score and setup writers. The existing 20-minute expiry, required audit, current authority and academic/enrollment checks apply through completion. A failed confirmation rolls back its grade event, projection and receipt together.

An accepted suggestion retains a compact protected snapshot of the calculation mode, weights/categories, assignment identities, base/bonus/earned points, excusals, score revisions, exact raw result, explicit comparison policy, policy-adjusted exact result and selected grade range. It is metadata on the existing `FinalGradeWasDecided` event in the same journal, with the grade's retained publication reference and actor/effective/recorded context. Working drafts remain audited CRUD. No assignment names, due dates or other students' marks are copied into that evidence. Raw inputs do not appear in browser responses, ordinary grade history or reporting.

Later coursework or grading-library edits cannot change the posted grade or its saved evidence. Original confirmation retries return their saved receipt, including after expiry or later grade corrections. Corrections continue through the reasoned registrar workflow and optional independent approval; suggestions cannot silently amend an already posted or withdrawn grade. **Grade history** identifies the accepted coursework percentage and calculation mode without disclosing the snapshot or protected reason. Ordinary academic replay applies the accepted grade; it does not rerun today's gradebook or automatically post a recalculated grade. Retained inputs support explicit verification/recovery when needed.

Performance remains bounded to one selected student's book. Ordinary averages retain their fast display calculation and the existing 500-assignment limit. Exact individual-weight comparisons allow at most 64 distinct reduced denominators; unusually varied books explain that manual review is needed instead of starting expensive unlimited arithmetic. Accepted snapshots have a 48 KiB limit within the existing event budget. A 500-assignment book with ordinary point values is supported; paged scoring remains available independently. There is no whole-roster replay, new background worker, table or migration.

The configured application database was inspected read-only (migration `20261118000003`, 20 events, no academic credits/assignments/scores); live facts and schema were unchanged. Fictional checks cover all calculation modes, exact boundaries and gaps, retained program overrides, stale input rejection, immutable evidence, original retries, audit/actor/expiry rollback, failing-grade flags, private history, 500-input snapshots and bounded calculations using the library's native PHP fallback. Actual private endpoints exercise provider wiring, CSRF, safe suggestions, changed-score rejection, posting/retry behavior and denied student mutation. JavaScript checks cover explicit selection, manual grading, refresh, preserved drafts and confirmation. Full browser/screen-reader and production-load acceptance remain open.

### Resolve an unknown course publication

Authorized registrars use **Students → Student record → View transcript → Course publication**. This opens one record on demand rather than replaying a directory. The same review is available from the grade record's **Review course publication** action. It requires both `sis:gradebook:amend` and `sis:student:read`; initial-grade authority is not required. Course choices are paged and contain only publications of that record's same stable course identity.

This workflow is for an active institutional record whose opening history did not record a course publication or a published offering, or a verified withdrawn attempt under the safeguards below. It does not replace known registration-time definitions. Select the publication supported by institutional evidence, enter the acceptance effective date and a reason, then review the before/after labels and confirm. **Change course publication review** preserves entered values, student identity and source guidance, including through repeated revisions. Confirmation checks the original actor/current permissions, locked enrollment/student/section scope, protected history, exact publication and expected credit/stream versions. Reviews expire after 20 minutes. A competing grade/completion/acceptance decision requires a fresh review.

The append records a **later acceptance**, not proof that this publication applied at registration. Original course labels and the original unknown reference remain in opening history. Subsequent corrections to this acceptance append another decision. Exact retries return the original receipt after later selections or an early drop, without changing the newer reference or restoring removed projections. A new review cannot reopen a dropped/closed record. Grade history includes the acceptance's curated course label and timestamps, withholding protected reasons and actors.

An untracked older row receives an explicit observation at the actual review time, followed by the real acceptance decision. Earlier actors, action times and course references remain unknown. Both appends, immutable course projection, accepted reference, current credit version and audit commit or roll back together. Grades, earned/attempted credits, quality points, completion dates, original offering references, enrollment and charges remain as recorded; future registrations in the section are unaffected. The new reference can resolve a degree-audit course match, but it supplies no missing grading or instructional chronology evidence. Issued transcript artifacts retain their existing immutable snapshots.

The change adds the registered `institutional-course-was-accepted` event to the existing academic-credit stream/journal and reuses `sis_credit_course_versions`. It adds no table, migration, permission grant or automatic backfill. The configured `nomadicjosh_sis` database was inspected read-only before implementation; no institutional credit rows were present, and no live rows were changed. Tests use fictional registered and older untracked records.

### Review a withdrawn attempt's academic sources

Authorized registrars can use the same **Course publication** review from the transcript, or **Correct withdrawn grade → Review course publication**. **Review academic dates** in that grade record also permits a source academic-period correction when the attempt lacks a retained published offering. Both actions require `sis:gradebook:amend`, `sis:student:read` and the separate `sis:gradebook:closed` duty. Ordinary faculty access does not grant these actions.

The attempt must have verified grade history, zero earned credit and grade-point quantities consistent with its retained definition. A missing/unverified grade must first use the established withdrawn-grade evidence review. An inconsistent record first needs the [reviewed quantity reconciliation](#reconcile-recorded-withdrawn-credit-quantities) rather than an invented correction. Resolve a pending grade-correction request before changing these sources; a request arriving after review invalidates that prepared confirmation.

The course review can select only a publication of the same stable course whose inception reference was unknown. Known registration-time course and offering publications remain protected. The date editor exposes **Source academic-period start**, with no completion-date control. It cannot mark a withdrawn attempt completed. A blank source date explicitly records an unknown; it does not substitute registration, withdrawal, current-term or grade-posting dates.

Both reviews preserve the withdrawal, attempted and earned credits, grade, GPA quantities, pursuit, seat and charges. A policy-assigned passing grade on a withdrawal still earns zero credit. Source changes do not confer prerequisite or degree credit. The existing academic-credit events, version, transaction, required audit and original-result receipts govern confirmation; loss of closed-record permission rolls back a new decision and prevents an accepted-result retry while access is absent. Earlier dates, course labels and unknown provenance remain recoverable. Removed early drops remain unavailable to new reviews; an accepted course-review retry retains its established behavior without recreating a projection.

These source corrections add no event type, journal, table or migration. Verification uses fictional records and leaves live institutional data unchanged. Reopening and broader historical reconciliation remain separate work. Awarded-course corrections are described below. Posted credit-unit corrections and academic posting-term corrections are available through the connected reviews described below. Issued transcript artifacts remain unchanged; prepare a fresh current transcript when corrected sources change its content.

## Historical facts and policies

A credit stream is keyed by its academic-credit ULID. Its opening event records the accepted enrollment, student, section and term references, credit units, course code/transcript title, available published course reference and explicit applicable grading-publication references. A later scale publication or program-pursuit closure does not change a known captured policy, even before the credit receives its first grade. Unknown inception policy remains explicitly unknown; the first reviewed grade chooses an actual available policy. Already graded records retain their exact grade publication.

Grade decisions retain the selected definition's code, per-course points, GPA/credit flags and scale/publication references. Replay calculates earned credit and credit-weighted quality points from these retained facts. It does not ask today's catalog or policy to reinterpret an old decision. Effective business dates may be earlier than the recording date, from the enrollment date through today. Effective dates do not imply that the institution knew the decision before it was recorded.

[Actual completion-date decisions](academic-completion-dates.md) now use this same academic-credit stream. Authorized registrars can record, correct or explicitly clear a completion date after a verified posted grade. Each decision preserves the previous date and decision lineage, increments the academic projection version and retains a protected reason. Grade amendments preserve the completion date. A completion date may predate administrative entry; it is validated independently from a grade decision's effective-date range and is never inferred from a posting timestamp or offering end date. Curated history shows the date change without exposing protected reasons or actors.

Existing untracked credits are **not** backfilled by a migration or read. Their first reviewed grade/drop records an explicit observation of existing facts, attributed to the current action, followed by the actual decision in the same transaction. Earlier author/action dates and policy provenance remain unknown. Existing earned units, grade references, quality points and row revision are preserved in that observation. A missing or altered tracked projection fails closed; it is never silently reclassified as a legacy baseline.

Early drop removes current enrollment/credit/course-version projections and reverses the original charge using the existing billing workflow, while retaining restricted academic events. Late drop retains attempted credit, its resolved drop grade/GPA rule and the charge. A retake/new registration receives a different credit identity. Hiatus decisions use the same drop service and ledger. Existing encrypted drop/hiatus receipts remain evidence and are not rewritten into invented earlier events.

[Source academic-period dates](academic-completion-dates.md#source-academic-period-start) now share the completion-date coordinator and editor. For verified graded records without an offering publication, a registrar can record/correct/clear a missing source start through `institutional-academic-period-was-recorded`. Both dates remain independent and cannot contradict one another. Known offering starts, grade/course facts and charges remain protected; the existing row version and stream provide shared concurrency and replay.

## Coursework averages

Use **My teaching → Section → Gradebook setup** to choose a calculation for that section. Assigned faculty and staff with `sis:gradebook:manage` can edit setup. This uses the existing teaching authority; student-read or grade-correction permission alone does not grant setup access.

- **Total points** is the default, including existing sections. Divide the sum of earned points by the sum of possible points for graded assignments.
- **Assignment weights** gives each assignment a percentage, to two decimal places. The weights must total 100%; **Equal weights** can distribute them evenly before you adjust or save. Calculate each graded assignment's earned/possible ratio, multiply by its weight, then divide the sum by the combined weight of those graded assignments.
- **Category weights** organizes assignments under named categories such as Quizzes, Coursework and Exams. Category percentages must total 100%; within each category, graded assignments contribute by their possible points. An assignment belongs to at most one category.
- A blank score is ungraded and excluded from the running average. A recorded zero counts. Coverage shows the share of possible points or assigned weights with a recorded score; it is not attendance or degree progress.
- A 0% assignment or category is excluded in weighted modes. New assignments start excluded until faculty adjust the weights or place them in a category. Each score row shows its weight, and the summary shows the excluded count. Switching back to total points includes all assignments again.

For example, a quiz worth 40% with 5/10 points and an exam worth 60% with 80/100 points produce **68%**. Before the exam is graded, the running average is **50%**, with **40% graded**. The screens explain this distinction so an incomplete average is not mistaken for a final result.

Faculty see feedback under **Grade & attendance → Assignment scores**. Students see the same calculation in their portal's assignment-score view. The portal obtains scores, weights and the rounded average from one shared, bounded result. Private responses remain no-store, and another account cannot read the student's results. A late response cannot populate a closed setup drawer or reopen an outdated portal dialog.

This is current coursework feedback, rounded to two decimal places. It does not automatically select, approve or post a grade, award credit or calculate institutional GPA. Final grades still require the existing retained institutional definition and explicit review/confirmation. Changes to weights, possible points or assignment scores leave already posted academic facts and their protected events unchanged. The reviewed initial-grade suggestion above now captures its accepted inputs explicitly.

Working calculation setup is audited CRUD in `sis_gradebook_calculations`, keyed by the section ULID. It does not add an event table. Section locking and a revision covering setup plus the assignment list prevent stale edits; an audit failure rolls back the save. Migration `20261117000015` adds an empty table, leaving existing grades, scores and history untouched.

Calculation and setup reads are limited to 500 assignments per selected section, plus one row to detect overflow; oversized books return an explicit validation error rather than a partial average. Faculty's existing paged score editing and official-grade controls remain available when a running average cannot be calculated. No whole-roster calculation, stream replay or write lock occurs merely to display these averages. Category-weight books now support the optional dropped-lowest rule below. Regular assignments support the optional bonus limit below, and bonus-only work now shares the same editor and calculations. Dropped-lowest rules for total-points/individual-weight modes and automatic final-grade selection remain outside these calculation modes.

### Category-weight setup and coverage

In **Gradebook setup**, choose **Category weights**, add named categories and enter percentages totaling 100%. Names are unique within the section; up to 20 categories are supported. Use **Find assignments** to place each included assignment in one category. Assignment choices are searchable and paged 50 at a time, with selections preserved across pages and filters. Removing a category explicitly excludes its former assignments until reassigned. Switching calculation modes preserves unsaved editor choices until saving; a saved mode switch clears irrelevant category/individual-weight settings.

For example, use **Quizzes 40%** and **Exams 60%**. Quiz scores of 5/10 and 45/90 give the quiz category a 50% average. An exam score of 80/100 gives the exam category an 80% average. The running average is `50% × 40% + 80% × 60% = 68%`.

Until a category has a recorded score, that category stays outside the running-average denominator. Once it has scores, its current average uses only its graded assignments. This is a running estimate, not a prediction that remaining work will earn the same marks. Coverage separately multiplies each positive category's weight by the fraction of its possible points with recorded scores. If only the 5/10 quiz has a score, quiz coverage is 10% and contributes 4 percentage points of overall grading coverage. Together with the graded exam, overall coverage is **64%**, while the running average is still **68%**. Recording a zero for the remaining quiz changes the category average and coverage; leaving it blank does not count it as zero.

An empty positive-weight category is permitted while faculty finish setup, but its weight contributes no coverage. Staff see an empty-category warning before saving; faculty and students see category names, weights, current averages, graded assignment counts and explicit empty/excluded labels. Thus a future final exam category cannot make a partially built gradebook look fully graded. Assignments without a category and work in 0% categories do not affect the overall result.

Category setup reuses the existing section revision, lock, audit, endpoints and calculator. Each membership is checked against that section's current bounded assignment list; duplicate membership, foreign assignments and stale edits are rejected. No student score or academic event is copied into setup. ### Drop lowest scores within a category

A recorded zero is eligible; an ungraded assignment is never dropped or treated as zero. The omitted assignment contributes neither earned nor possible points to that category's current average or coverage. The category's weight does not change, and the underlying score stays recorded. Zero-weight categories and unassigned work remain exclusions, rather than being counted as drops.

## Second-person grade correction approval

Institutions choose the rule under **Students → Grade policy**. **Direct correction** is the default and keeps the existing reviewed correction workflow. **Require a different registrar’s approval** changes a posted-grade correction into a pending request. Initial grades retain their existing teaching authority. This implements one independent approval; it does not introduce an arbitrary approval-chain designer.

1. An authorized records officer opens the existing final-grade review, selects a retained institutional grade, enters the effective date and explains the correction. **Submit request** records the proposal while the original grade, earned credits, GPA inputs and transcript facts remain in effect.
2. A different authorized registrar opens **Students → Grade corrections**, selects the pending request and reviews its before/proposed grade, effective date and protected request reason. Assignment to a case does not grant access. The worklist is paged at 50 requests and reads indexed projections without replaying every student's history; selecting a case verifies that credit's bounded protected stream.
3. The reviewer chooses **Approve correction** or **Reject request**, supplies a decision reason, reviews the decision and explicitly confirms. Approval posts the requested grade through the same academic-credit calculation used by direct corrections. Rejection preserves the posted grade. The requesting officer can instead **Cancel my request**. Even a super administrator cannot approve or reject their own request.

The authority requirements are deliberately separate:

| Task | Required permission |
| --- | --- |
| Request a correction, read a case or view the pending worklist | `sis:gradebook:amend` and `sis:student:read` |
| Approve/reject another officer's request | Both permissions above and `sis:gradebook:approve` |
| Cancel a pending request | The requesting officer, still holding amendment and student-read authority |
| Correct or approve a recorded withdrawn grade | The corresponding correction/approval duties above, plus `sis:gradebook:closed` |
| Configure the institutional rule | `sis:gradebook:policy` |

The two new permissions are protected system definitions. Migration `20261117000017` adds them without granting them to any existing role or person. Assign the independent approval duty to the appropriate registrar staff. Super administrators retain their general access, subject to the explicit self-review prohibition.

A policy change invalidates an unconfirmed correction review, preventing an older direct-correction screen from bypassing newly required approval. Turning approval off does not approve, cancel or bypass existing requests; those requests must still be approved by a different authorized registrar, rejected or cancelled. Only one request may remain pending for a credit. A changed grade, completion date or other accepted academic fact prevents approval of an outdated proposal; staff can still reject or cancel it and prepare a fresh correction.

Decision reviews expire after 20 minutes and are bound to their actor, request, outcome and retained proposal. **Change review** retains the entered decision and reason. Exact successful retries return their original receipt, including after expiry or later academic decisions, without reopening the request or overwriting a newer grade. Missing or altered request projections stop selected-record verification; they cannot silently create a new baseline. Audit failure, authority loss, policy drift or concurrent academic changes roll back the entire affected transaction, including events, projections and receipts.

`GradeChangeWasRequested` and `GradeChangeWasResolved` append to the existing academic-credit stream in the single `sis_history_events` journal. Request/cancellation/rejection events do not change the academic-credit projection version or posted grade. Approval changes that version once, retaining the previous academic facts and the original completion date. For an older untracked graded record, the actual submission observes its existing facts and establishes stream routing without rewriting the grade or its revision. Earlier decision authors and times remain unknown. Earlier events remain unchanged; there is no automatic conversion or invented request history.

## Coursework rounding and the final-grade cap

Open **Teaching → section → Gradebook setup → Final-grade comparison policy**. Institution-authorized staff can choose the rule for that section. The protected **`sis:gradebook:calculation-policy`** duty is separate from assignment/score management, grade certification and the grade-correction approval policy. It also requires access to the section as assigned faculty or gradebook management. Administrators must grant this duty explicitly; the migration grants it to no role or user. Faculty without it can read the current rules and save assignment/category weights while preserving those rules.

The built-in institutional fallback is **Exact percentage (no rounding)**, with the cap unchecked. Sections without an explicit override inherit the institution’s current reviewed policy. Existing explicitly saved section rules stay fixed until an authorized user restores inheritance. See [institutional publication and inheritance](#institutional-comparison-policy-and-section-inheritance) below. A rounding rule requires **zero to three decimal places**:

| Rule | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Exact percentage | Compare the original rational percentage; no rounding. |
| Nearest, ties up | Choose the nearest value; an exact halfway value rounds upward. At zero places, 92.5 becomes 93. |
| Nearest, ties to even | Choose the nearest value; a halfway value chooses the even final digit. At zero places, 92.5 becomes 92 and 93.5 becomes 94. |
| Round down | Discard the remaining fractional part at the selected precision. At three places, 92.9994 becomes 92.999. |
| Round up | Any nonzero remaining fractional part increases the last retained digit. At three places, 92.9994 becomes 93. |

**Cap the final-grade percentage at 100% before rounding** changes a raw bonus result such as 110% to a 100% comparison percentage. The cap is applied first, followed by the selected rounding rule. It does not change recorded points or assignment bonus limits. Without the cap, a comparison result above 100% needs manual grading. Institutions should choose these settings deliberately to match their published teaching policy; the system does not infer a rule from grade bands.

Save setup before requesting a fresh suggestion. Faculty and student coursework feedback explain the saved policy and keep the original running average, including bonus results above 100%. Coverage, missing-work/excusal/drop rules, category normalization and the requirement to complete included work stay the same. Changing setup never posts or revises an official grade automatically.
