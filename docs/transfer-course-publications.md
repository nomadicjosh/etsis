# Accepted course publications for transfer awards

New transfer awards require an explicit **Accepted institutional course publication**. This records which receiving-institution definition the evaluator accepted, rather than inferring a definition from today's catalog. Course publications belong to the institutional course selected by the reviewed equivalency.

## Staff workflow

1. Catalog staff publish the receiving course through the existing course review/publication workflow.
2. Open **Transfers → Equivalencies**, review the equivalency and choose **Award transfer credit**.
3. Select the student, local posting term, sending-course completion date and received grade rank. Deliberately select the accepted publication; none is selected automatically. **Load more course publications** retrieves older choices in bounded pages.
4. Confirm the award. Its institutional code and transcript title come from that exact publication. Awarded credits come from the reviewed equivalency, which can differ from the publication's default course credits.

An evaluator needs both `sis:transfer:manage` and `sis:student:read`. Catalog maintenance alone grants no student-award authority. The posted-award screen states whether its accepted publication was retained or is unknown. Browsing curated publication choices requires those same two permissions, without requiring catalog-edit access. Browser requests retain CSRF protection and private/no-store responses.

The receiving definition is an acceptance fact recorded now. It is not a claim about which institutional course was being taught on the sending institution's earlier completion date. External completion and sending-period dates retain their independent meanings.

## Historical behavior

The genuine opening award event retains `course_version_id` in protected event metadata. A later `transfer-course-was-accepted` decision retains its predecessor, previous/new publication references and accepted code/transcript title in the same award stream. A `transfer-course-was-replaced` decision additionally retains the earlier/new receiving-course identities and earlier accepted labels. The aggregate preserves the original opening reference separately for initial-command retries. It also retains the accepted labels, awarded units, selected grading facts and source/equivalency context. Course, grade, credit and source-period corrections remain separate; credit amendments and void/reinstatement preserve the accepted course reference. Later catalog changes cannot relabel this award.

A repeat initial submission must select its original opening publication and posting term, even after a later acceptance correction. It cannot replace the accepted publication, bind an earlier unknown award or reinstate a voided award. A [reviewed receiving-course correction](#review-an-existing-awards-accepted-course) can now select another publication of the same course or deliberately replace the institutional course on this award. The original receiving course and publication remain opening facts for retries.

Earlier awards without a recorded publication remain **unknown**. No migration, read or initial-award retry assigns today's definition to those records. An evaluator can explicitly record a new acceptance decision using the review below; the earlier unknown decision remains unknown. A known reference can participate in existing exact-publication course matching and degree-group placement. Different publications do not become equivalent merely because they share a course identity or code. Earlier equivalence review, broader grade-policy compatibility and retained final degree evaluation remain outstanding.

## Review an existing award's accepted course

Open the posted transfer award and choose **Review accepted course**. The existing receiving course opens first. To replace it, expand **Choose another course**, search by code or title, and choose the correct identity. Search returns at most 20 matches per page and loads nothing until requested; **More courses** continues that search. Current catalog labels help discovery, including retired identities needed for historical corrections. They do not establish an accepted definition. Select an actual published definition of the chosen course, enter an effective date and explain the acceptance evidence. No publication is selected automatically; older choices load in bounded pages. The effective date must be between external completion and today. It expresses when this correction applies, while the journal separately retains when staff actually recorded it.

**Review accepted definition** compares the current and proposed course code, transcript title and publication status. Awarded credits and grade are shown alongside them. **Revise accepted course** and **Refresh award** preserve the selected course/publication, reason and effective date. Choosing a different course clears the previous publication choice, requiring a deliberate new selection. If a course has no publications, the same screen explains the gap and still allows another course search. **Confirm course acceptance** applies the reviewed definition; an interrupted response can be retried with the same protected review token. The review expires after 20 minutes for a new confirmation. Completed retries still return their original receipt after expiry, later corrections or a void, without changing newer decisions.

Both `sis:transfer:manage` and `sis:student:read` are required for choices, review, confirmation and history. A voided award must be reinstated before a new acceptance review. Selecting the currently accepted publication is rejected as an unchanged request. A changed student/award, different actor, stale revision or malformed token requires a fresh authorized review. Institutions should grant this duty only to their authorized evaluators.

The comparison identifies a receiving-course replacement explicitly. Confirmation changes the receiving-course identity when requested and records the exact accepted publication, code and transcript title. Student, award/credit identity, original equivalency, sending course/institution, completion date, local posting term, source academic period, pursuit, awarded credit units, grade and financial facts remain unchanged. For example, replacing a 3-credit award with a course whose catalog default is 4 credits still leaves the award at 3 credits; any credit correction needs its existing separate review. Course matching, repeat-course comparisons and degree placement can consequently change. This decision applies to one award; it does not create a reusable equivalence rule or reinterpret other students' awards.

For an untracked earlier award, confirmation first records an attributed observation of its then-current facts at confirmation time, followed by the actual new acceptance decision with its reason/effective date. Review and reads create neither event. Earlier authors, accepted definitions and decision dates are not invented. The award continues to identify its earlier-history gap; other missing grade/source evidence may still prevent a complete degree evaluation.

**Course definition history** separately shows original awards, explicit observations, later acceptances and receiving-course replacements, with their retained labels, known/unknown references and effective/recorded times. Pages omit raw events, actors and protected reasons. Credit/grade and source-period histories retain their separate purposes. Course decisions also appear as context in **Academic history**, without pretending they changed source dates or posting term. Replacement rows retain both earlier and accepted course labels.

Previously prepared or issued transcript artifacts retain their exact bytes. Changed academic labels invalidate a stale preparation for delivery; staff must prepare a fresh revision. Course acceptance alone never issues a transcript or changes charges.

## Storage, verification and recovery

This stage adds no tables or schema migration. It uses the existing authoritative `sis_history_events`, `sis_course_versions` and `sis_credit_course_versions`. Definition, subject and classification projections are shared with section bindings; a transfer award does not require a section. The credit-version index is reconstructible and must agree with the award's latest accepted reference in protected history. Missing, injected or conflicting references reject verification instead of becoming trusted academic inputs.

Confirmation verifies the chosen publication against the deliberately selected course through bounded replay. Search and publication browsing require the same award scope and registrar/student-read permissions as confirmation, without granting catalog-edit access. Degree exceptions and transfer reviews share the catalog-owned `CourseSearchRepository` and its existing parameterized query rather than maintaining separate search implementations. A new acceptance also increments the academic credit revision and atomically changes its indexed publication reference. Initial awards retain their student/catalog locks. Acceptance corrections serialize on student and award/credit locks, with current permission and actor rechecks. Events, definition/index projections, academic rows and operational audit share one transaction. A failed acceptance or replacement rolls back its observation, decision, index and academic changes together. Authority and review expiry are checked through the required audit. Accepted earlier proof/event formats remain unchanged and return their original results after later replacements. Publication choices expose only version, course labels and catalog credit units; publication actors, reasons and protected payloads are not returned.

Projection reconstruction must use the exact retained course publication. Unknown references may remain null only when reconstructing genuinely unknown earlier records. Reconstruction creates no new award, financial event or historical decision. Complete operational rebuild/swap tooling remains a separate roadmap item.
