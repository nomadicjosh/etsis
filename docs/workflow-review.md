# Staff workflow review

The legacy system is the feature inventory, not a requirement to reproduce its screen sequence. Preserve institutional capabilities and records while redesigning each staff task around its purpose, review, confirmation and follow-up. This review draws on `legacy/docs`, the legacy routers/views and migrations, and the current coverage in [backend-migration.md](backend-migration.md).

“Working” below describes a workflow available in the current code. “Partial” means useful pieces work but the broader recommendation still needs implementation. “Proposed” is a design recommendation, not completed functionality.

## Operating principles

- Start from a person, student, section or case and keep the related context visible. A staff member should not have to memorize screen acronyms or re-enter identifiers between steps. Preserve academic codes such as `MATH-101` as institutional data.
- Distinguish the task from its underlying records. One reviewed task may coordinate several aggregates in one transaction without combining their tables or weakening permissions.
- Show proposed consequences before applying consequential changes. Use readable reasons, effective dates and explicit choices; institution-specific policy determines defaults rather than hidden assumptions.
- Keep record-level permissions independent. A registration duty does not grant finance, transfer evaluation, transcript fulfillment, employment or account administration. Sensitive staff comments remain separately protected.
- Treat uncertainty as a normal case: retain operation identifiers, support checking saved outcomes and reject stale reviews. Never silently repeat a charge or institutional transition after an interrupted response.
- Preserve history through amendments, voids and explicit corrections. A voided history record does not silently reverse academic or financial actions already applied.
- Use bounded, searched pickers and result pages. Do not fetch an entire institution into a form. Keep private drafts in memory; longer-lived drafts would need authorized, encrypted server storage rather than browser storage.
- Use accessible labels, keyboard interaction, visible focus, in-context errors/status and narrow-screen layouts. Prefer a short sequence of understandable decisions to a single oversized form.

## Institutional drop policy

The implemented rule removes the enrollment and academic-credit records when a course is dropped during the inclusive drop/add grace period. It automatically reverses the original course charge, if present, in the same transaction. Financial events are retained. An unpaid charge can reverse to zero; payments and remaining charges determine whether the account has a credit, a zero balance or money still owed. An account credit is not an automatic cash refund. Separate institutional term fees remain posted for their own policy review.

After grace, the course and original charge remain. Catalog staff configure dated grade periods on each term using existing institutional grade codes (such as D and W), inclusive cutoffs and an open final period. There is no assumed ordering of D and W. Late drops earn zero credits; the selected grade's configured GPA treatment applies. A late D retains the course because deletion depends on the grace-period deadline, independently of the grade marker. Without an explicit term policy, the late-drop marker remains W, excluded from GPA.

Catalog staff now [review and publish retained term policies](registration-history.md#review-and-publish-term-drop-policies), with reasons, dated definitions and paged history. Later live-term edits do not overwrite the published deadline; a new review is required. Completed drops retain their original policy reference and outcome.

Student self-service, staff course drops and guided effective-dated hiatus cases use this shared policy. Every browser confirmation includes the reviewed enrollment identity/version, date and policy fingerprint. Encrypted, actor-bound receipts survive academic deletion; retries recover the same outcome and cannot drop a newly added enrollment. Early re-registration creates new academic records and a new course-charge source. Retakes in another billing term incur a new term charge while preserving the original late-drop history and charge.

Posted final grades, earned credits and grade points are protected. Reviewed reasoned grade corrections now use independent registrar authority. Multi-person approval, closed-enrollment correction/replacement, refund schedules and cash-refund decisions remain separate institutional workflows.


## Report requests and specialist handoff

The approved faculty-to-registrar example now has a connected case. A requester describes the question, its purpose and an optional date needed; a report specialist takes responsibility, asks for clarification if needed, builds a report in the existing studio and reviews the selected report/requester before sharing. The requested date is a planning hint, not a delivery guarantee. There is no automatic email, attachment or scheduled execution. A shared report can use live results or a fixed capture, as described below.

Request creation and private own-case reads use the existing report execution grant (`sis:reporting:run`); report specialists use `sis:reporting:manage`. Faculty already have execution access by default. Institutions can grant that capability to other staff without making them report authors. Only authorized report specialists see the team queue; requesters cannot see one another's questions or messages.

Questions, titles, purpose, conversation and action receipts are encrypted. Assignment, clarification, requester reply, completion, cancellation/reopening and messages compare case versions and append immutable history. Taking over another specialist's work requires a reason. Sharing, delivered status, operation receipt and metadata audit commit together. A changed report definition requires another handoff review. Replaying an earlier handoff does not grant sharing again after a deliberate revocation.

Report sharing preserves existing source-data checks and never adds role/direct data permissions. A recipient may need an administrator to grant the required data access. The handoff records the shared report and reviewed definition revision; it does not prove that the requester can currently read it. Dynamic reports return current results, while static reports retain their captured results. Either can expire. Reopening/cancelling a case does not silently revoke prior shares; report owners manage those explicitly. Messages are visible to the requester and the authorized report team; separate private specialist notes and departmental queue delegation remain future work.


### Saved report results and automatic removal

Open a saved report and choose **Report settings**. The owner, or an administrator with report administration and management permissions, makes two independent choices:

| Choice | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| **Dynamic report** | The saved definition reads current authorized data each time it runs. Existing reports default to this behavior. |
| **Static one-time report** | Capture the complete matching results once. Later source changes leave those results unchanged. |
| **Temporary / one-off** | Optionally remove either kind after a chosen number of days (1–3,650). Leave this off to retain it. |

For example, a weekly advising list can stay dynamic and be retained, a census extract can stay static and be retained, and a temporary investigation can use either result type with automatic removal after seven days. Saving settings starts the chosen removal period from the accepted save time. Changing it requires a new save; retrying an already accepted operation returns its original result without resetting the period.

Static capture uses one consistent database read transaction, then stores encrypted result pages and a bound, encrypted manifest atomically with the settings revision, operation receipt and metadata audit. It captures **all results**, up to **5,000 rows and 8 MiB**, rather than silently freezing only the first result page. A larger result is rejected without a partial saved capture. A between-query work budget stops unusually long captures; individual query timeouts remain a database/deployment concern. Large background exports remain future work. The saved capture time indicates completion, not an assertion that independently maintained source projections were all refreshed simultaneously.

A static report keeps both its definition and captured results fixed. Its expiry can still be changed. **Create a new report from this definition** opens a separate editable draft when different results are needed. Shared recipients still need current report-run and source-field permissions; a fixed result never bypasses revoked sharing or removed data permissions. Static pages and CSV page exports read the encrypted capture without rerunning live student queries. Snapshot encryption uses the existing application message cipher/key management; preserve that key according to the deployment's backup policy.

Earlier simple student reports also expose **Report settings**. Explicitly saving those settings converts the existing program, institutional-entry-term and active-student filters to the current typed Students source under the same report ID and owner. It then opens in Report Studio; there is no separate snapshot implementation for simple reports. Merely opening a report does not convert it.

At expiry, the report immediately disappears from report directories and cannot be read, exported, shared or edited. The hourly cleanup removes its definition, retained result pages, encrypted change receipts and shares. A minimal parent marker remains so linked office request records and their recorded case history stay intact; those cases have their own retention needs. Cleanup does not delete student, registration, financial or authoritative event history. Existing downloads and backups require the institution's separate retention controls.

Apply migration `20261114000002_CreateSisReportLifecycles` before using these settings. The normal scheduler runs a bounded batch of up to 50 expired reports hourly. Ensure the existing scheduler is invoked every minute from the project directory, using the deployment's PHP 8.4+ interpreter:

```sh
php codex schedule:run
```

To inspect or run a bounded cleanup manually:

```sh
# Preview only; no content is removed.
php codex sis:reports:prune --limit=50
# Explicitly remove eligible content.
php codex sis:reports:prune --apply --limit=50
```

The maximum batch is 100 reports. Commands print aggregate counts only. If a larger backlog exists, successive hourly batches continue removal; access is blocked from the expiry instant regardless of cleanup timing. The migration uses portable schema definitions compiled against the framework's five supported dialects. Runtime capture and cleanup have been exercised on SQLite; the configured MariaDB connection also passed a bounded, read-only capture check. PostgreSQL, SQL Server and Oracle require deployment integration acceptance before claiming runtime certification.

### Complete report exports

Open a saved **Report Studio** report and choose **Prepare CSV** in **Complete export**. This queues a private export of all matching results, rather than downloading only the visible page. **Export page** remains available for a quick page download. The complete-export panel also works for shared recipients who retain report-run and source-data permissions; creating or changing a report still requires the separate management duty.

The panel shows **Queued**, **Preparing** or **Ready**, with an indeterminate preparation bar, **Check status**, **Cancel export** and **Download CSV**. There is no invented completion percentage. Staff may leave the screen and return to the same report to see their current export. Polling stops when the screen closes and pauses requests while the tab is hidden. Requests retain their operation reference after an uncertain response; repeated clicks reuse an existing queued/running export. Preparing a new export replaces that account's earlier ready download, keeping one downloadable result per report and requester.

Dynamic exports capture current data when the worker runs, using one consistent read transaction for the complete traversal. They preserve the saved definition revision queued by the requester; definition/lifecycle changes require a fresh request. Static exports read the existing authenticated capture, including its original columns and results, without running a live query. An export does not change a dynamic report into a static one, alter its sharing or make an academic decision.

Complete exports are limited to **25,000 rows**, **16 MiB** and a cooperative **30-second** source traversal. The source is paged in at most fifty rows; crypto work and the source callback are included in the deadline checks. Static captures retain their separate 5,000-row/8 MiB limits. An excessive, interrupted or unauthorized export publishes no partial download. Per-query timeouts remain deployment configuration; the cooperative deadline cannot interrupt a query already executing. Large/multi-engine load acceptance remains a release task.

Only the requester can inspect, cancel or download their export, even if another account owns or administers the shared report. Current account enablement, report ownership/sharing, report expiry and every referenced source/field permission are checked before execution, publication and download. Downloads authenticate the complete manifest and every encrypted CSV chunk before sending response bytes, and recheck access/status afterward. Output has formula protection for headers and values, attachment disposition, no-store, nosniff and a sandbox policy. ### Choose and preview a transcript layout

In **Transcripts**, open a request and choose **Review preparation**. **Document layout** offers **Classic · Letter**, **Classic · A4**, **Modern · Letter** and **Modern · A4**. The two styles use the same verified academic values, retained cohort labels and totals. Classic uses a centered academic heading; Modern uses the workspace's blue accents. Paper size is explicit for both the HTML print layout and PDF.

Choose **Preview layout** to inspect the current review inside a sandboxed, labelled frame. The preview is marked **not issued**, requires the same registrar and student-read permissions, and retains CSRF, private/no-store responses, size limits and debug suppression. It creates no document, fulfillment, grade, charge or historical event. It records only an authorized-access audit. Preview is on demand; opening a request does not render a PDF. Changing a layout starts a fresh actor-bound review and clears the release acknowledgement. **Refresh review** preserves the chosen layout and also requires a new acknowledgement.

**Prepare transcript** retains the exact selected layout code, label, paper size and implementation reference in the protected document snapshot. Its review binds that choice as well as the academic sources. A different selection, changed template or expired proof requires another review; staff cannot change the layout merely by editing a confirmation request. Rendering and encryption happen before the final locked source/permission/expiry checks. Document insertion, ready status and required audit still commit or roll back together.

Prepared artifacts now contain **both the actual PDF and the actual accessible HTML**. Their content hashes, document identity and preparation time are validated when read. The accessible view returns those retained bytes, rather than invoking today's renderer against an earlier snapshot. Replacing a template, changing the default or retiring a layout therefore cannot reformat an original document. It also does not, by itself, make the student's academic facts stale or prevent recording otherwise valid delivery evidence. Academic-source changes retain their existing fresh-preparation requirement. Accepted retries return their original layout and document without rendering again.

The renderer accepts only registered codes, uses escaped text and disables remote resources, PHP and JavaScript in PDFs. The HTML includes a restrictive content policy; the in-workspace frame additionally has no script, same-origin or form privileges. Document data and proofs are not stored in browser storage or placed in preview URLs. The original accessible view remains a private separate-tab link after preparation. This HTML companion provides semantic accessible reading; these checks do not certify the PDF as a tagged or digitally signed document.

Configure the issuer and default in `config/transcript.php` before issuing documents:

```php
'issuer_name' => 'Example University',
'issuer_address' => 'Institutional mailing address',
'default_layout' => 'classic-letter',
```

Deployment developers register additional supported layouts through `TranscriptLayoutCatalog` and the document renderer/provider. Browser clients cannot supply template filenames, URLs, PHP or CSS. The current registered styles share one template instead of copying the academic calculations into separate renderers. Pending reviews detect changed template/renderer resources; historical reads need no installed copy of the older renderer because the actual output is retained.

The existing **4 MiB PDF** limit remains. Accessible HTML is capped at **1 MiB** and the combined encoded artifact at **6 MiB** before encryption, bounding memory and fitting the existing protected storage. An invalid or excessive result saves no partial document. The configured development database was inspected before this alpha format change: there were no prepared documents or delivery records to convert, and the twenty recorded historical events were untouched. No schema migration, parallel journal, inferred old HTML, permission grant or new recovery workflow is introduced.

### Institutional transcript release checks

In **Transcripts → Transcript setup**, authorized administrators can define up to twelve checks. Give each a short title, optional staff instructions, and choose **Preparation**, **Delivery** or **Both steps**. For example, preparation may require reviewing the student's release authorization, while delivery may require verifying the recipient's identity. Checks are manual institutional confirmations; a registration hold or account balance does not automatically become a transcript block.

Setup requires `sis:transcript:policy` in addition to `sis:transcript:manage` and `sis:student:read`. The protected permission is available for explicit assignment and is not automatically granted to ordinary transcript operators or roles. Super administrators retain their existing system authority. Configuration is encrypted, revisioned and change-audited CRUD, rather than a new domain event stream. Removing every check disables additional institutional checks, while retaining the existing academic-record acknowledgement and verified-source requirements.

Preparation shows its applicable checks beside the academic review. Staff must confirm every one before **Prepare transcript** becomes available; layout previews require no attestations. Delivery collects its own applicable confirmations with the timestamp and delivery evidence, then displays their titles in the final review. **Change details** preserves entered date/time and evidence but asks staff to confirm the checks again. **Refresh checks** reloads current setup without discarding those entries. Stale setup, omitted checks or extra check identities are rejected by the server, including direct API calls. A failed save leaves the open draft in place.

The review binds the exact policy revision and definitions. Settings reads and document rendering acquire no policy lock. Final acceptance takes a short policy-row lock and compares a current database read with the reviewed setup; policy changes during rendering, confirmation or required audit prevent a partial acceptance. Existing student/person/request locks, authority checks, expiring proofs and atomic document/status/audit writes remain in place.

Each new prepared artifact and delivery record retains the applied policy, confirmed check identities, staff actor and server confirmation time inside its existing encrypted content. The staff document view shows separate preparation and delivery summaries. These confirmations are not printed on the transcript. Changing setup later cannot change earlier labels, original PDF/HTML bytes or an accepted retry. It also cannot make unchanged academic facts stale: an already prepared document can proceed to delivery after satisfying the institution's current delivery checks. Earlier genuine artifacts that predate check capture remain readable; missing confirmations remain unrecorded rather than invented.

`20261119000007_CreateSisTranscriptReleasePolicy` adds one bounded singleton settings table and the independent protected permission. It converts no academic or event history and grants no roles. ### Verify the academic facts behind a transcript

Transcript preparation and delivery now share the same internal academic-input verifier used by registration and degree audits. Under the existing student/person/request locks, it checks institutional credit and registration streams, transfer awards and their projections, including retained early-drop history. Transcript rows use the retained course titles, grade definitions, quantities and recorded academic dates. A grade already retained in protected academic history uses its accepted snapshot; an edit to today's grade table cannot reinterpret that decision. A missing or altered projection, damaged protected event, or exceeded verification limit prevents a new document or delivery decision; no partial transcript is saved.

The calendar query now supplies labels and ordering only. A source's retained term code takes precedence; otherwise the term label is captured from the current calendar at preparation. It does not substitute today's term dates for a missing source-period or completion date. **Dates & history** expands each work row in the workspace, and the PDF/HTML companion includes the recorded dates. A retained offering reference remains the source for its instructional period; an explicit source-period date is a different fact.

The protected snapshot retains visible credit identities, academic/registration versions, applicable course/offering references and a digest of the complete verified reference set. Removed early-drop credit identities and protected reasons are not exposed in the transcript snapshot or rendered document. The original domain events remain in the existing journal. A later grade, date or transfer correction invalidates the reviewed academic revision and requires another preparation/release review; original accepted confirmations and stored PDF bytes remain unchanged.

The same capture pass now verifies the canonical person's name/ID projection against the protected profile stream, and the exact primary pursuit against its selection and assignment-status history. A name/ID correction requires a fresh review; an unrelated phone/address change does not invalidate the prepared transcript. Only the core-identity version is retained in this evidence, with no contact details or profile-change reasons. Earlier identity history is explicitly unknown when no creation history was recorded.

Program labels come from the student's selected cohort publication when recorded, then from retained assignment/primary-selection labels, and only otherwise from an explicitly observed current program. Publishing a later academic year's requirements or editing today's program draft cannot rename the earlier cohort on a new transcript. Publication identity, revision, year and the initial retained cohort binding are checked. New [reviewed cohort changes](student-program-assignments.md#review-a-cohort-change) now append to the same pursuit stream and verify their binding projection before these sources are used. Older receipts stay preserved as earlier evidence; missing past decisions are not reconstructed.

A recorded primary pursuit remains resolvable after closure. Student records, assignment history and transcript preparation can read it without granting registration eligibility or reopening it. For an older pursuit without recorded primary-selection history, an active assignment takes precedence; otherwise exactly one closed assignment to that program can be observed. Multiple closed candidates are rejected rather than guessed from dates. The workspace identifies a closed primary pursuit and omits active-pursuit registration/program-change actions. This is a read contract, not a graduation or institutional-withdrawal workflow.

**Earlier history incomplete** means some current facts are observations with missing earlier decisions. These are labelled honestly, including older ungraded work; the existing explicit registrar release acknowledgement remains required. A recorded grade, earned award or withdrawal without a verified applicable grading definition instead needs the existing grade-history review before preparation or release. The system does not infer a grade or silently calculate a partial GPA.

**Current history unavailable** is different: the current record could not be verified. The workspace keeps the protected original document and its download/accessible view available to authorized staff, displays a warning and withholds **Record delivery**. Direct PDF and accessible-HTML downloads read the retained artifact without replaying current academic history. New preparation and delivery still verify independently. A failed nested journal read is rolled back before a separate, authorized original-document read; audit or permission failures never become a successful fallback. The document retains the verification evidence actually captured at preparation; a fresh verified revision is required when current sources cannot establish a new release. This does not alter previously recorded delivery evidence.

Each capture pass uses the existing bounded inventory (200 rows per source index) and one shared 400-page/2,000-event replay allowance across identity, primary pursuit, selected program publication and academic inputs; the outer 500-row guard rejects oversized projection lists before verification. Preparation rechecks the captured facts before and after rendering and through the final required audit, rather than reusing stale cached verification. No partial fallback or automatic background job is created when a limit is reached. Larger institutional bulk issuance, independent reconciliation tools, transcript repeat/GPA policies, custom release rules and signed electronic delivery remain separate work. The existing all-attempt totals and retained grade GPA flags are unchanged; a degree-audit pursuit's policy is not silently applied to the entire career transcript.

Academic-source capture and identity checking change no schema or live institutional records and add no event type, parallel journal, compatibility model or recovery screen. Subsequent cohort maintenance adds its business decision to the existing pursuit stream, as described in the linked cohort workflow. Fictional tests cover institutional and transfer chronology, identity/primary-pointer damage, retained cohort labels, closed and ambiguous older pursuits, contact-only changes, original-result retries, projection/journal damage, removed early drops, shared replay bounds, explicit unknowns, current grade-table edits, immutable original PDFs, concurrent changes, real provider/CSRF/private-response boundaries and browser accessibility.

## Approved co-requisite workflow

The institution will choose whether a co-requisite may be satisfied by qualifying prior completion or must be taken in the same academic term. **Prior completion is allowed by default.** Work must belong to the current program pursuit or have explicit registrar-approved reuse. A previously satisfied lab does not need another registration or lab charge merely because the student is registering for its lecture. A stricter same-term policy remains an institutional option.

The institution will also choose the response to dropping a supporting co-requisite. The approved default is to **block an unsupported drop unless the dependent courses are dropped in the same reviewed transaction**. Warnings are an institutional alternative, not a browser bypass. Registration, staff entry, selected schedules, section changes and drops must use the same retained rules and verified pursuit-scoped evidence. Later catalog edits must not silently change earlier course relationships or accepted decisions.

The [co-requisite workflow](registration-history.md#co-requisites-and-linked-drops) is implemented across the shared editor, publications, institutional registration settings, staff/student commands, atomic schedule plans and linked drops. It retains accepted relationships and pursuit-scoped support, supports same-term lab replacements, and preserves separate academic and billing outcomes. Broader prerequisite/co-requisite expression grammar remains future work.
