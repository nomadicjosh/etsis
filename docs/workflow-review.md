# Staff workflow review

The legacy system is the feature inventory, not a requirement to reproduce its screen sequence. Preserve institutional capabilities and records while redesigning each staff task around its purpose, review, confirmation and follow-up. This review draws on `legacy/docs`, the legacy routers/views and migrations, and the current coverage in [backend-migration.md](backend-migration.md).

“Working” below describes a workflow available in the current code. “Partial” means useful pieces work but the broader recommendation still needs implementation. “Proposed” is a design recommendation, not completed functionality.

## Priority: security and historical academic integrity

The [event-sourcing direction](event-sourcing.md) supersedes the earlier finance-only assumption. Apply the [architecture and retrofit gates](event-sourcing-architecture.md) and [legacy academic field review](academic-history-field-review.md) before expanding academic writes. Existing “working” workflows below describe current functionality, not completion of the new historical-integrity requirements. Secure event foundations, immutable published definitions and attributable grade/credit history take priority over additional features.

## Operating principles

- Start from a person, student, section or case and keep the related context visible. A staff member should not have to memorize screen acronyms or re-enter identifiers between steps. Preserve academic codes such as `MATH-101` as institutional data.
- Distinguish the task from its underlying records. One reviewed task may coordinate several aggregates in one transaction without combining their tables or weakening permissions.
- Show proposed consequences before applying consequential changes. Use readable reasons, effective dates and explicit choices; institution-specific policy determines defaults rather than hidden assumptions.
- Keep record-level permissions independent. A registration duty does not grant finance, transfer evaluation, transcript fulfillment, employment or account administration. Sensitive staff comments remain separately protected.
- Treat uncertainty as a normal case: retain operation identifiers, support checking saved outcomes and reject stale reviews. Never silently repeat a charge or institutional transition after an interrupted response.
- Preserve history through amendments, voids and explicit corrections. A voided history record does not silently reverse academic or financial actions already applied.
- Use bounded, searched pickers and result pages. Do not fetch an entire institution into a form. Keep private drafts in memory; longer-lived drafts would need authorized, encrypted server storage rather than browser storage.
- Use accessible labels, keyboard interaction, visible focus, in-context errors/status and narrow-screen layouts. Prefer a short sequence of understandable decisions to a single oversized form.

## Approved implementation roadmap

The user has approved implementing these recommendations. The first stages now include guided hiatus/withdrawal, return/readmission, course draft cloning, offering draft/publication, an explicit registrar-to-finance hand-off, protected transcript preparation/fulfillment, term-based course-drop policy, and report requests with reviewed specialist handoff. Their remaining boundaries are recorded above.

Next stages are:

1. Reviewed closed-enrollment corrections and transfer-award replacement workflows for course/term/completion identities. Posted-credit/grade amendments, voids and reinstatement are now available.
2. Section-to-room linking. Reusable offering templates now feed the reviewed draft/publication workflow.
3. Institution-specific transcript release policy, template selection and electronic-delivery integrations.
4. Broader relational report sources and advanced expressions using the legacy SELECT acceptance matrix, report-driven cohort selection and broader import templates. Degree-audit student evaluation and protected individual adjustments follow the implemented reusable-group and composition builders.
5. The remaining admissions, office delegation, finance policy, communications and content recommendations in the table.

Approval of the roadmap does not imply that every item is already implemented. Institutional policy choices—refund schedules, grade-change approval, standing/graduation rules, delegated office scope and external delivery—must be made visible and configurable instead of copied as hidden assumptions from legacy code.

## Institutional drop policy

The implemented rule removes the enrollment and academic-credit records when a course is dropped during the inclusive drop/add grace period. It automatically reverses the original course charge, if present, in the same transaction. Financial events are retained. An unpaid charge can reverse to zero; payments and remaining charges determine whether the account has a credit, a zero balance or money still owed. An account credit is not an automatic cash refund. Separate institutional term fees remain posted for their own policy review.

After grace, the course and original charge remain. Catalog staff configure dated grade periods on each term using existing institutional grade codes (such as D and W), inclusive cutoffs and an open final period. There is no assumed ordering of D and W. Late drops earn zero credits; the selected grade's configured GPA treatment applies. A late D retains the course because deletion depends on the grace-period deadline, independently of the grade marker. Without an explicit term policy, the late-drop marker remains W, excluded from GPA.

Student self-service, staff course drops and guided effective-dated hiatus cases use this shared policy. Every browser confirmation includes the reviewed enrollment identity/version, date and policy fingerprint. Encrypted, actor-bound receipts survive academic deletion; retries recover the same outcome and cannot drop a newly added enrollment. Early re-registration creates new academic records and a new course-charge source. Retakes in another billing term incur a new term charge while preserving the original late-drop history and charge.

Posted final grades, earned credits and grade points are protected. Reviewed reasoned grade corrections now use independent registrar authority. Multi-person approval, closed-enrollment correction/replacement, refund schedules and cash-refund decisions remain separate institutional workflows.


## Report requests and specialist handoff

The approved faculty-to-registrar example now has a connected case. A requester describes the question, its purpose and an optional date needed; a report specialist takes responsibility, asks for clarification if needed, builds a report in the existing studio and reviews the selected report/requester before sharing. The requested date is a planning hint, not a delivery guarantee. There is no automatic email, attachment, scheduled execution or frozen export.

Request creation and private own-case reads use the existing report execution grant (`sis:reporting:run`); report specialists use `sis:reporting:manage`. Faculty already have execution access by default. Institutions can grant that capability to other staff without making them report authors. Only authorized report specialists see the team queue; requesters cannot see one another's questions or messages.

Questions, titles, purpose, conversation and action receipts are encrypted. Assignment, clarification, requester reply, completion, cancellation/reopening and messages compare case versions and append immutable history. Taking over another specialist's work requires a reason. Sharing, delivered status, operation receipt and metadata audit commit together. A changed report definition requires another handoff review. Replaying an earlier handoff does not grant sharing again after a deliberate revocation.

Report sharing preserves existing source-data checks and never adds role/direct data permissions. A recipient may need an administrator to grant the required data access. The handoff records that a live report was shared; it does not prove that the requester can currently execute it, nor preserve historical results. Reopening/cancelling a case does not silently revoke prior shares; report owners manage those explicitly. Messages are visible to the requester and the authorized report team; separate private specialist notes and departmental queue delegation remain future work.


## Reusable offering templates

Catalog staff can save a planned section list, or its reviewed saved offering defaults, as a named reusable template. Codes, courses, capacity, faculty, delivery mode, section fees and intended self-registration settings are defaults. Template payloads exclude source section/term identities, rooms/bookings, enrollment, finance and catalog-year assignments. Program requirements and matriculation cohorts stay independent.

Choose an active template and a target term to load a fresh, editable plan. Each preparation generates new section ULIDs without inserting academic records. Staff review year-specific codes, current courses/faculty and prices before the ordinary offering review, draft save and separate publication. Template edits do not rewrite previously prepared plans. Retired templates cannot be selected for a new preparation; existing plans remain independent. Restoring a template is a reasoned, versioned configuration decision.

Default revisions reuse the planner without asking for a working term. That editing path reviews and saves template configuration only. Catalog changes and template version changes invalidate pending saves; unchanged retries return the original receipt instead of repeating or overwriting a later revision. Missing/inactive source defaults are visible for review, and ordinary offering validation still governs actual draft creation/publication. Names are searchable; template lists and history are bounded. Section-to-room linking remains a separate stage, including optional rooms for online and hybrid delivery.


## Academic calendar and matriculation clarification

The [separate setup guide](academic-calendar-setup.md) describes the implemented order: term types, academic years, then term maintenance. Entering start year 2026 supplies end year 2027 and generates actual terms from every active template after review. Standard terms cannot overlap; non-standard sessions can. Templates affect future generation and the separate Terms entry remains available.

Catalog years are derived cohorts, not another setup form. J.J. pursuing a B.A. from 2022-2023 retains its 2022-2023 curriculum reference through graduation; Sadie pursuing the B.A. from 2024-2025 retains its 2024-2025 reference. New years and curriculum revisions leave both existing assignments untouched. New assignments follow explicit term/year membership, never a date-range guess. Historical missing assignments remain visible for review. This underpins the separate published degree-requirements builder; student evaluation remains pending.


Term-template setup now includes explicit first/second calendar-year examples, optional J-Term and a February Spring starting point, plus single Summer or Summer 1/Summer 2 examples. All dates and codes remain editable; only active templates generate terms. A protected preview uses the same generation rules without saving, and previews clear when settings change. Year reviews list terms chronologically and label their min/max range as active-term coverage. See [Academic calendar setup](academic-calendar-setup.md).

### Separate reusable course groups and degree compositions

The clarified workflow is now implemented as two visual builders. **Academic catalog → Course group library** creates independent blocks with no program or academic-year owner. Its source courses use checkboxes and explicit published versions; requirement cards offer all/choose/credit-pool controls, drag/drop and move buttons. **Programs → Degree audit requirements** composes published group versions into an ordered tree, with degree minimums and institutional policies in a separate settings panel. General education can be reused by many programs without copying it.

Editing or publishing a group preserves every degree's earlier selected version. Adopting a new group publication is an explicit composition edit and new degree publication. Existing inline drafts can be moved into library drafts in one atomic action without manufacturing publications or changing history. The specification reference's expandable tree informs the preview; completion checkmarks await actual student evaluation. See [setup and workflow](degree-audit-builder.md).

### Retain student-specific degree requirements

**Students → Student programs → Degree requirements** now connects each pursuit to an explicitly reviewed published degree version matching its matriculation cohort. The registrar records an actual pursuit start date and a reason; the next screen shows the retained requirements and estimated completion before confirmation. New course, group and degree publications leave that selection unchanged. A later cohort correction shows a review notice instead of silently rewriting the selection. The independent `sis:degree-audit:assign` permission and student-read authority are both required. Owner reads, curated history and immutable publication resolution do not require catalog-editing access. See [setup and scope](student-degree-requirements.md). Student evaluation and individual adjustments remain pending.

### Course-based waivers and explicit minimum exceptions

A waiver applies to the course throughout **one student-program pursuit**, regardless of group membership. A dual-degree pursuit has separate approvals. **Student programs → Degree requirements → Student exceptions** now offers searchable course/threshold cards, explicit before/after values, reasoned review/confirmation, and revocation with retained history. Waivers create no earned credits or GPA points and do not lower minimums; credit/GPA reductions are separate approvals tied to the reviewed degree version. The `sis:degree-audit:adjust` office permission is independent of catalog, registration and requirement assignment. [Workflow and migration](student-degree-exceptions.md) distinguish these implemented inputs from the still-pending evaluator.

### Course quantities, grading scales and substitutions

Reusable groups now choose a required credit total or a distinct-course count, with an optional institution-selected minimum grade-point value for each applied course. Earlier average-group-GPA publications remain unchanged; the new floor is never inferred from their GPA. Global grading setup supports grade code, score range, GPA points and separate GPA/earned-credit choices. A program can explicitly override the whole scale, with reviewed return to global inheritance. The maximum is derived; only unconfigured setup displays 4.0. New degree publications retain the resolved scale. [Setup and staged academic integration](grading-scale-setup.md) explain the remaining authoritative academic event conversion.

Student exceptions now include exact-publication course substitutions, including before either course is taken. Approval is pursuit-scoped and degree-version-specific, with explicit replacement publication selection, conflict checks and retained reasoned revocations. The future evaluator must consume these approved inputs without inventing earned credit or changing other pursuits. [Exception workflow](student-degree-exceptions.md).

### Consolidated grading definitions

The scale editor now supplies the academic workflows through immutable, publication-scoped `sis_grades` definitions. Final-grade dialogs fetch the affected student's applicable scale, with a program choice for different dual-degree scales. Transfer awards and late-drop policies use the shared catalog. Already graded records and their corrections retain the original scale; reports/transcripts continue using exact grade IDs. The obsolete single-grade API is removed, and verified migration preserves old grade identities and recorded events/receipts before removing the duplicate JSON projection.
