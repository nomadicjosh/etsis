# Staff workflow review

The legacy system is the feature inventory, not a requirement to reproduce its screen sequence. Preserve institutional capabilities and records while redesigning each staff task around its purpose, review, confirmation and follow-up. This review draws on `legacy/docs`, the legacy routers/views and migrations, and the current coverage in [backend-migration.md](backend-migration.md).

“Working” below describes a workflow available in the current code. “Partial” means useful pieces work but the broader recommendation still needs implementation. “Proposed” is a design recommendation, not completed functionality.

## Priority: security and historical academic integrity

The [event-sourcing direction](event-sourcing.md) supersedes the earlier finance-only assumption. Apply the [architecture and retrofit gates](event-sourcing-architecture.md) and [legacy academic field review](academic-history-field-review.md) before expanding academic writes. Existing “working” workflows below describe current functionality, not completion of the new historical-integrity requirements. Secure event foundations, immutable published definitions, attributable grade/credit history and protected person profile history take priority over additional features. Profiles across student, parent, staff and faculty roles now belong inside the canonical person event-sourcing boundary; routine reference setup, facilities, drafts and report management remain CRUD where appropriate.

## Operating principles

- Start from a person, student, section or case and keep the related context visible. A staff member should not have to memorize screen acronyms or re-enter identifiers between steps. Preserve academic codes such as `MATH-101` as institutional data.
- Distinguish the task from its underlying records. One reviewed task may coordinate several aggregates in one transaction without combining their tables or weakening permissions.
- Show proposed consequences before applying consequential changes. Use readable reasons, effective dates and explicit choices; institution-specific policy determines defaults rather than hidden assumptions.
- Keep record-level permissions independent. A registration duty does not grant finance, transfer evaluation, transcript fulfillment, employment or account administration. Sensitive staff comments remain separately protected.
- Treat uncertainty as a normal case: retain operation identifiers, support checking saved outcomes and reject stale reviews. Never silently repeat a charge or institutional transition after an interrupted response.
- Preserve history through amendments, voids and explicit corrections. A voided history record does not silently reverse academic or financial actions already applied.
- Use bounded, searched pickers and result pages. Do not fetch an entire institution into a form. Keep private drafts in memory; longer-lived drafts would need authorized, encrypted server storage rather than browser storage.
- Use accessible labels, keyboard interaction, visible focus, in-context errors/status and narrow-screen layouts. Prefer a short sequence of understandable decisions to a single oversized form.

## Institutional drop policy

The implemented rule removes the enrollment and academic-credit records when a course is dropped during the inclusive drop/add grace period. It automatically reverses the original course charge, if present, in the same transaction. Financial events are retained. An unpaid charge can reverse to zero; payments and remaining charges determine whether the account has a credit, a zero balance or money still owed. An account credit is not an automatic cash refund. Separate institutional term fees remain posted for their own policy review.

After grace, the course and original charge remain. Catalog staff configure dated grade periods on each term using existing institutional grade codes (such as D and W), inclusive cutoffs and an open final period. There is no assumed ordering of D and W. Late drops earn zero credits; the selected grade's configured GPA treatment applies. A late D retains the course because deletion depends on the grace-period deadline, independently of the grade marker. Without an explicit term policy, the late-drop marker remains W, excluded from GPA.

Student self-service, staff course drops and guided effective-dated hiatus cases use this shared policy. Every browser confirmation includes the reviewed enrollment identity/version, date and policy fingerprint. Encrypted, actor-bound receipts survive academic deletion; retries recover the same outcome and cannot drop a newly added enrollment. Early re-registration creates new academic records and a new course-charge source. Retakes in another billing term incur a new term charge while preserving the original late-drop history and charge.

Posted final grades, earned credits and grade points are protected. Reviewed reasoned grade corrections now use independent registrar authority. Multi-person approval, closed-enrollment correction/replacement, refund schedules and cash-refund decisions remain separate institutional workflows.


## Report requests and specialist handoff

The approved faculty-to-registrar example now has a connected case. A requester describes the question, its purpose and an optional date needed; a report specialist takes responsibility, asks for clarification if needed, builds a report in the existing studio and reviews the selected report/requester before sharing. The requested date is a planning hint, not a delivery guarantee. There is no automatic email, attachment or scheduled execution. A shared report can use live results or a fixed capture, as described below.

Request creation and private own-case reads use the existing report execution grant (`sis:reporting:run`); report specialists use `sis:reporting:manage`. Faculty already have execution access by default. Institutions can grant that capability to other staff without making them report authors. Only authorized report specialists see the team queue; requesters cannot see one another's questions or messages.

Questions, titles, purpose, conversation and action receipts are encrypted. Assignment, clarification, requester reply, completion, cancellation/reopening and messages compare case versions and append immutable history. Taking over another specialist's work requires a reason. Sharing, delivered status, operation receipt and metadata audit commit together. A changed report definition requires another handoff review. Replaying an earlier handoff does not grant sharing again after a deliberate revocation.

Report sharing preserves existing source-data checks and never adds role/direct data permissions. A recipient may need an administrator to grant the required data access. The handoff records the shared report and reviewed definition revision; it does not prove that the requester can currently read it. Dynamic reports return current results, while static reports retain their captured results. Either can expire. Reopening/cancelling a case does not silently revoke prior shares; report owners manage those explicitly. Messages are visible to the requester and the authorized report team; separate private specialist notes and departmental queue delegation remain future work.


### Saved report results and automatic removal

Open a saved report and choose **Report settings**. The owner, or an administrator with report administration and management permissions, makes two independent choices:

| Choice | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| **Dynamic report** | The saved definition reads current authorized data each time it runs. Existing reports default to this behavior. |
| **Static one-time report** | Capture the complete matching results once. Later source changes leave those results unchanged. |
| **Temporary / one-off** | Optionally remove either kind after a chosen number of days (1–3,650). Leave this off to retain it. |

For example, a weekly advising list can stay dynamic and be retained, a census extract can stay static and be retained, and a temporary investigation can use either result type with automatic removal after seven days. Saving settings starts the chosen removal period from the accepted save time. Changing it requires a new save; retrying an already accepted operation returns its original result without resetting the period.

Static capture uses one consistent database read transaction, then stores encrypted result pages and a bound, encrypted manifest atomically with the settings revision, operation receipt and metadata audit. It captures **all results**, up to **5,000 rows and 8 MiB**, rather than silently freezing only the first result page. A larger result is rejected without a partial saved capture. A between-query work budget stops unusually long captures; individual query timeouts remain a database/deployment concern. Large background exports remain future work. The saved capture time indicates completion, not an assertion that independently maintained source projections were all refreshed simultaneously.

A static report keeps both its definition and captured results fixed. Its expiry can still be changed. **Create a new report from this definition** opens a separate editable draft when different results are needed. Shared recipients still need current report-run and source-field permissions; a fixed result never bypasses revoked sharing or removed data permissions. Static pages and CSV page exports read the encrypted capture without rerunning live student queries. Snapshot encryption uses the existing application message cipher/key management; preserve that key according to the deployment's backup policy.

Earlier simple student reports also expose **Report settings**. Explicitly saving those settings converts the existing program, institutional-entry-term and active-student filters to the current typed Students source under the same report ID and owner. It then opens in Report Studio; there is no separate snapshot implementation for simple reports. Merely opening a report does not convert it.

At expiry, the report immediately disappears from report directories and cannot be read, exported, shared or edited. The hourly cleanup removes its definition, retained result pages, encrypted change receipts and shares. A minimal parent marker remains so linked office request records and their recorded case history stay intact; those cases have their own retention needs. Cleanup does not delete student, registration, financial or authoritative event history. Existing downloads and backups require the institution's separate retention controls.

Apply migration `20261114000002_CreateSisReportLifecycles` before using these settings. The normal scheduler runs a bounded batch of up to 50 expired reports hourly. Ensure the existing scheduler is invoked every minute from the project directory, using the deployment's PHP 8.4+ interpreter:

```sh
php codex schedule:run
```

To inspect or run a bounded cleanup manually:

```sh
# Preview only; no content is removed.
php codex sis:reports:prune --limit=50
# Explicitly remove eligible content.
php codex sis:reports:prune --apply --limit=50
```

The maximum batch is 100 reports. Commands print aggregate counts only. If a larger backlog exists, successive hourly batches continue removal; access is blocked from the expiry instant regardless of cleanup timing. The migration uses portable schema definitions compiled against the framework's five supported dialects. Runtime capture and cleanup have been exercised on SQLite; the configured MariaDB connection also passed a bounded, read-only capture check. PostgreSQL, SQL Server and Oracle require deployment integration acceptance before claiming runtime certification.

### Register students selected from a saved report

Open a saved **dynamic Report Studio** report based on **Students & current primary program**, **Student programs & catalog cohorts** or **Academic credits & final grades**, then choose **Choose students for registration**. The action is available for detail reports with no summarized columns or post-summary conditions. Section reports and summaries remain report results; etSIS does not guess which individual students a headcount represents. Static captures and unsaved drafts do not provide this live handoff. Earlier simple reports can join this workflow by explicitly saving their report settings first.

1. Review the distinct matching students, with institutional IDs, names and activity. Pages contain at most 50 students; repeated program or credit rows identify a student only once. This is the report's live population, rather than only its current visible result page.
2. Select up to 25 students across pages. The visible shortlist supports individual removal. Refreshing or paging preserves it while the saved definition is unchanged; a changed definition clears the old shortlist with an explanation.
3. Choose **Review selected students for registration**. The server checks the saved definition revision, live membership and current report/source/registration authority again. A missing member or changed definition requires a new review. No seat, credit, charge or academic decision is created by selecting students.
4. The existing **Register a group** screen opens with those students selected. Choose the course section and review the current students, prerequisites, seats and fees before explicitly confirming. **Back to report selection** returns to the preserved shortlist. The group remains editable, so adding another student uses ordinary manual registration authority. Registration uses each student's current primary pursuit; a report over an earlier pursuit does not change it.

Population derivation reuses the typed query compiler, preserving original filters, relationship choices and related-row conditions. A separately bound student restriction preserves the report's full 40-condition/four-level allowance. The saved definition, original result columns and export are unchanged. No raw SQL, journal payloads or automatic full-report enrollment is exposed.

Every population page and selection requires `sis:reporting:run`, `sis:student:read`, `sis:registration:manage`, access to the saved report and all original/derived source-field permissions. Sharing a report grants none of those additional duties. Current actor/authority are rechecked after the metadata-only access audit; failure rolls back that audit and returns no population. The existing registration command independently enforces eligibility, locks, history, atomic charges and original retries. The new POST endpoints retain CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression. Choices remain only in workspace memory; denied access clears displayed/cached population choices. Population selection adds no stored population snapshot, event format or parallel registration implementation. The separate report-lifecycle migration supports optional fixed results and expiry. Billing and messaging actions remain separate future work.

### General report design and named joins

The legacy SELECTs are examples and acceptance checks. The reporting target is one general visual engine covering authorized institutional data—from admissions and registrations through recorded academic work and, once those facts exist, graduation—not a finite list of canned reports. Starting points are editable definitions in that engine. They do not lock the author into their columns, statuses, thresholds or grouping.

Choose a starting source, join related records, select and order fields, set ordinary or related-record conditions, and choose calculations, summaries and grouped-result conditions. **Section headcounts, including empty sections** can become “less than 12” by counting distinct enrollment references, limiting matched enrollment statuses to `A`/`N`, keeping the left relationship and setting the summary condition to less than 12. Change the threshold or term when needed. Putting enrollment-status conditions in the ordinary WHERE-style row conditions would instead remove empty sections; matching conditions and related-row conditions serve distinct purposes.

To use the same related records independently, choose **Add another [record]** in the building-block library. Name the new card—for example, **Fall registrations** or **Spring registrations**—then select its fields or use it in a matching card. Its child records, field choices, join mode and conditions are independent of the original branch. A layout-only copy adds no rows until referenced or explicitly joined. Every drag operation retains its ordinary click/checkbox alternative.

For example, add a registration copy, require an original matching registration in `26/FA`, and require a matching copy in `27/SP`. Those two cards can find different registrations for one student while preserving a student-level result. For side-by-side detail, select output fields from both branches. Two Fall rows and three Spring rows can produce six combinations: this is the chosen join grain. Use distinct counts for student/enrollment headcounts, or matching cards when the purpose is only to require/exclude related records. Unqualified sums across multiple fact branches are not automatically safe; pre-aggregated financial sources remain necessary.

Removing a named copy removes its fields, dependent output joins, sort/summary references and matching conditions that reference that branch. Removing an ordinary output record keeps independently defined matching cards. Names are readable labels; physical identifiers and relationship paths come from the server registry. Copying does not grant access to a source, field or ancestor relationship. Authors may add up to three copies, within a 64-relationship graph ceiling and the existing output/filter/join limits. Copies of copies and arbitrary physical table/SQL input are not accepted.

The **Applications & admissions** starting source has one row per application, with current applicant names/ID, status, program and intended entry term. It can connect to the person's current student record, registrations and recorded academic credits. The shared student-work relationships are also available from the student, program, section and academic-credit roots. Registration and credit branches are labelled **all pursuits**; they do not imply that the work belongs to the selected application or program, approve reuse of earlier work, or change prerequisite/degree-audit boundaries. Recorded course labels and credit values come from academic-credit snapshots; current application program and term labels remain current reference data.

Admissions reports require report author/run duties plus the existing `sis:admissions:manage` data authority. Student-linked fields and conditions additionally require `sis:student:read`. Sharing grants none of these duties. Answers, private decision reasons, actor details, credentials and raw journal payloads are not fields. [Recorded submission dates](application-submission-history.md#submission-date-reporting) now come from a small indexed projection of authenticated submission events. Earlier unknown dates remain blank; dates are not inferred from ULIDs, today's rows or intended entry dates. A linked current student record also does not prove that a particular application matriculated; application status remains the appropriate current outcome field.

Saved definitions, encrypted static captures, expiry, private CSV pages and authorized student-population handoffs share the current query model and compiler. The metadata endpoint `POST /sis/report-studio/graph/` resolves permitted named branches with CSRF, private/no-store responses and debug suppression. It reads no student result rows. Metadata is permission-filtered, and execution checks every referenced/copied source again. Named joins add no schema migration or event format; the submission-date read model has the additive migration documented above.

Source coverage and expression power are still being extended. Authoritative graduation/award facts, derived financial aggregates, richer expression trees and configurable joins beyond the approved relationship registry remain roadmap work. A graduation decision is not guessed from closed program status, and ordinary reports do not replace official academic eligibility or financial commands.

System-provided field, card and condition-group labels now use short names without parenthetical codes or scope notes. Registration, enrollment, application outcome and submission-channel comparisons offer readable choices, while compiled predicates retain their exact stored values. Multiple-value conditions keep separate selected codes; custom/retained values and text comparisons survive editing and saved-report reopening. Choices are permission-filtered metadata and copied branches inherit them. Authored report/column names and captured report headers are retained.

Registration and academic-credit links still include all pursuits; shortening labels does not change that scope. **Submitted on** uses the original submission timezone; **Submission recorded on** uses UTC. Ordinary report setup, saved definitions, shares and settings remain audited CRUD. Previewing or editing a report appends no domain events.

### Related-record conditions

In Report Studio, open **Conditions → Filter by related records**, choose a **Related record source**, and select **Add related-record condition**. Choose **Has matching records** or **Has no matching records**. Leave the conditions empty to check record presence, or use the familiar condition builder to define a match. Remove the card to stop applying it.
